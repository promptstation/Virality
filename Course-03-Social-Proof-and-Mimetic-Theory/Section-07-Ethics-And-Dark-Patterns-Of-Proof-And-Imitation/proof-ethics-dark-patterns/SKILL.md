# Ethics and Dark Patterns of Proof and Imitation: Professional Module Builder

## Mission

Produce professional-level material on where the line is between evidence and manipulation in proof and imitation design — the manipulation modes, the trust economics of discovery, and the honest redesign. The unit of work is a defensible artifact — a learning module, an evidence brief, a case diagnosis, or a strategy memo — in which every claim is tied to a source, a mechanism, and a boundary condition.

Optimize simultaneously for:

- empirical precision (what the canonical studies and works actually found)
- conceptual clarity (the core terms kept strictly distinct)
- causal discipline (correlational and causal claims never blurred)
- practical utility (a practitioner can act on the output the same day)
- intellectual honesty (limits and boundary conditions as visible as the findings)

Do not produce generic advice or pop-science hype. The register is that of an advanced professional training program: confident where the evidence is strong, explicitly conditional where it is not.

## Use Cases

### New professional learning module
When asked to create a full module, course unit, or workshop on this topic:
1. Establish scope: audience, depth, and which claims the module must cover.
2. Build the evidence base from primary sources and canonical works.
3. Develop the core mechanisms and diagnostic logic.
4. Add method, critique, and application sections.
5. End with exercises and the delivery checklists.
6. Deliver the complete artifact with no placeholders.

### Brief or leadership memo
When asked for a short document that must decide something:
1. State the question the document must answer in one sentence.
2. Include only findings strong enough to bear that decision, each with its mechanism and source.
3. Convert results into plain-language implications.
4. End with the specific decisions the evidence changes.
5. Cap length; cut context before cutting caveats.

### Case diagnosis
When asked to explain why a specific piece of content, campaign, or product spread (or did not):
1. Identify the operative mechanisms — never a single assumed cause.
2. Map the case onto the diagnostic framework of this skill.
3. Run the differential: which mechanisms were active, which alternatives were considered and set aside.
4. State confidence and the evidence that would revise the diagnosis.

### Strategy design
When asked to design a campaign, content system, or program around this lever:
1. Fix the objective, audience, and platform context.
2. Select the mechanisms from this skill that fit that context.
3. Specify implementation, sequencing, and the measurement plan.
4. State the boundary conditions under which the plan breaks.

## Core Output Requirements

Regardless of format:

- Every named finding or claim carries its source or mechanism in the same breath.
- Correlational and causal claims are labeled as such wherever they matter.
- Effect claims carry their metric and a plain-language rendering.
- Boundary conditions are stated as explicit “applies when / breaks down when” conditions.
- Examples use real, named studies, campaigns, or artifacts — not invented case studies.
- No padding sections, generic intro/outro filler, or advice that would read the same for any topic.

## Phase 1 — Scope and Claim Audit

### Artifact and audience
What the user actually needs (module vs. memo vs. diagnosis) and who reads it. Different readers make different cuts of the same material.

### Claim inventory
List the claims the artifact will make. For each, note the source or mechanism that supports it and whether that support is correlational, experimental, or canonical. Downgrade or cut unsupported claims — not the caveat.

### Depth budget
Match length to the decision the artifact serves. The fix for a weak document is choosing stronger claims to carry it, not adding hedges to weaker ones.

## Phase 2 — Foundations and Core Content

1. Inventory the manipulation modes: manufactured crowds (bots, purchased engagement), staged urgency (fake scarcity, planted FOMO), astroturfing (paid voices as organic), laggard shaming, and herding engineering that manufactures the bandwagon.
2. Teach the discovery-cost economics: the manipulation works until it is discovered, and the discovery cost — the trust collapse, the regulatory action, the platform sanction — is deferred, not avoided; the honest tactic front-loads its cost, the dark one defers it.
3. Show the honest equivalents: the real crowd (the genuine adoption, visible), the real urgency (the real deadline, the real quantity), the disclosed partnership (the model who is paid and says so), the inclusive prompt (the nudge that does not name the deviant).
4. Cover the policy landscape: the platform rules on inauthentic behavior, the consumer-protection rules on fake reviews and dark patterns, and the emerging regulatory standards — the compliance floor under the ethics ceiling.
5. Build the ethics review process: the manipulation-mode scan, the authenticity verification, the disclosure plan, the vulnerable-audience check, and the sign-off — run before launch, not after discovery.

## Phase 3 — Method and Technique

- The manipulation-mode scan: each tactic classified (evidence / disclosure-needed / manipulation)
- The authenticity verification: the source of each proof element, the contract, the consent
- The disclosure design: what is disclosed, where, in what format (the native disclosure slots)
- The vulnerable-audience check: the age, the dependency, the pressure capacity
- The honest-redesign workshop: the dark pattern in, the honest equivalent out, the effect preserved

## Phase 4 — Analysis and Diagnosis

- Classify the tactic: evidence, disclosure-needed, or manipulation — with the structural reason (what makes it manipulation is the falsity of the signal, not the goal).
- Compute the deferred cost: the discovery probability, the trust-collapse magnitude, the regulatory exposure — the case for front-loading the cost.
- Run the vulnerable-audience check: the pressure capacity of the target, the exit ease, the reversal cost.
- Verify the honest equivalent's effect: the redesign must be tested, not assumed to work.

## Phase 5 — Application and Design

- Run the ethics review before launch: the scan, the verification, the disclosure, the check, the sign-off.
- Choose the honest equivalent by default: if the effect exists in the honest form, the manipulation form is unnecessary and banned.
- Instrument the trust metric: the trust-collapse indicator (complaints, corrections, sanctions) as the KPI of the ethics program.
- Publish the never-fake list per brand: the standing document that defines the line for the team.

## Phase 6 — Ethics and Boundary Conditions

- The line is the signal's truth: a proof element that is false, or presented as organic when it is manufactured, is manipulation regardless of the outcome.
- The vulnerable audience raises the standard: the same tactic on a child, a dependent, or a crisis audience is a different category.
- The platform's own rules are the floor: compliance is necessary, not sufficient — the professional standard is above the policy minimum.

## Phase 7 — Exercises and Assessment

For learning artifacts, include exercises that force the diagnosis skill, not recall. Use the standard set:

1. Run the manipulation-mode scan on five real tactics (two honest, two gray, one dark): the classification, the structural reason, and the recommendation.
2. Compute the deferred cost of a manufactured-crowd tactic: the discovery probability, the trust-collapse estimate, the regulatory exposure — versus the front-loaded cost of the honest crowd.
3. Redesign a FOMO campaign into its honest equivalent: the real urgency, the disclosure, and the A/B test of the effect.
4. Build the brand's never-fake list: the standing document, the verification process, and the sign-off chain.
5. Design the disclosure plan for a partnership campaign: what, where, in what format — using the platform's native slots.
6. Run the full ethics review on a live campaign before launch: the scan, the verification, the check, the sign-off — with the findings and the conditions.

For each exercise provide scenario, objective, constraints, expected deliverable, evaluation criteria, and an expert-quality example solution where appropriate.

## Phase 8 — Quality Gate and Delivery

Before delivering, run the delivery checklists. The artifact fails the gate on any “no.”

### Mode scan
- Every tactic is classified
- The structural reason is stated
- The manipulation forms are banned by default

### Authenticity
- The source of each proof element is verified
- The contracts and consents are on file
- The never-fake list is checked

### Disclosure
- The disclosure is planned (what, where, format)
- The native slots are used
- The vulnerable-audience check is run

### Delivery gate
- The sign-off is recorded
- The trust metric is instrumented
- The post-launch discovery watch is scheduled

## Anti-Patterns

the manipulation form kept 'because it works'; the deferred cost ignored; the vulnerable audience unchecked; the compliance floor treated as the ceiling; the disclosure after discovery; the never-fake list never enforced.

## Decision Heuristic

Before any claim or recommendation, ask:

1. Is the signal true — is what the proof element says about the world actually the case?
2. If discovered, what is the cost, and is it deferred?
3. Does the honest equivalent exist, and was it tested?
4. Who is the most vulnerable person this tactic touches, and is the pressure capacity checked?
5. Is the sign-off recorded, and is the trust metric live?

If a claim cannot survive all of these questions, it is not in the artifact.

## Final Principle

The goal is not to avoid the effect. It is to have the effect on a signal that is true — so that the win does not carry the collapse inside it, waiting for discovery.
