# Step 2 — Generated Prompt (Result of the Reusable Prompt)

```
Develop a comprehensive, professional-level learning module on **“Ethics and Dark Patterns of Proof and Imitation”** as part of an advanced course on how others' behavior — observed, measured, and imitated — drives adoption, sharing, and trend formation. The module should where the line is between evidence and manipulation in proof and imitation design — the manipulation modes, the trust economics of discovery, and the honest redesign.

The material should teach the ethics architecture: the manipulation modes, the discovery-cost economics, and the redesign of each dark pattern into its honest equivalent. Go beyond the assumption that 'it's just marketing' — the line is observable in the tactic's structure and examine the manipulation modes one by one, with the honest equivalent that keeps the effect.

Inventory the manipulation modes: manufactured crowds (bots, purchased engagement), staged urgency (fake scarcity, planted FOMO), astroturfing (paid voices as organic), laggard shaming, and herding engineering that manufactures the bandwagon.

Teach the discovery-cost economics: the manipulation works until it is discovered, and the discovery cost — the trust collapse, the regulatory action, the platform sanction — is deferred, not avoided; the honest tactic front-loads its cost, the dark one defers it.

Show the honest equivalents: the real crowd (the genuine adoption, visible), the real urgency (the real deadline, the real quantity), the disclosed partnership (the model who is paid and says so), the inclusive prompt (the nudge that does not name the deviant).

Cover the policy landscape: the platform rules on inauthentic behavior, the consumer-protection rules on fake reviews and dark patterns, and the emerging regulatory standards — the compliance floor under the ethics ceiling.

Build the ethics review process: the manipulation-mode scan, the authenticity verification, the disclosure plan, the vulnerable-audience check, and the sign-off — run before launch, not after discovery.

Include practical methods for working at a professional level:
* The manipulation-mode scan: each tactic classified (evidence / disclosure-needed / manipulation)
* The authenticity verification: the source of each proof element, the contract, the consent
* The disclosure design: what is disclosed, where, in what format (the native disclosure slots)
* The vulnerable-audience check: the age, the dependency, the pressure capacity
* The honest-redesign workshop: the dark pattern in, the honest equivalent out, the effect preserved

Include practical exercises that require the learner to:
1. Run the manipulation-mode scan on five real tactics (two honest, two gray, one dark): the classification, the structural reason, and the recommendation.
2. Compute the deferred cost of a manufactured-crowd tactic: the discovery probability, the trust-collapse estimate, the regulatory exposure — versus the front-loaded cost of the honest crowd.
3. Redesign a FOMO campaign into its honest equivalent: the real urgency, the disclosure, and the A/B test of the effect.
4. Build the brand's never-fake list: the standing document, the verification process, and the sign-off chain.
5. Design the disclosure plan for a partnership campaign: what, where, in what format — using the platform's native slots.
6. Run the full ethics review on a live campaign before launch: the scan, the verification, the check, the sign-off — with the findings and the conditions.

For each exercise, provide the scenario, objective, constraints, expected deliverable, evaluation criteria, and an expert-quality example solution where appropriate.

Structure the material progressively, beginning with the manipulation modes and the structural line, moving through the discovery economics and the honest equivalents, then to the standing ethics program: the scan, the list, the sign-off. Establish clear conceptual distinctions between **evidence, disclosure, manipulation, deferred cost, and the vulnerable audience** before showing how they interact.

Use professional terminology from social psychology, behavioral economics, and cultural theory where relevant, but explain specialized terminology in clear language. Do not make the material sound like generic AI-generated advice; the register should be that of an advanced professional training module used by behavioral researchers, brand strategists, and social scientists.

Research the subject using high-quality professional and academic sources where external research materially strengthens the material. Prioritize authoritative sources: the conformity literature (Asch, Sherif), Cialdini's social-proof research, the norm-influence literature, and Girard's mimetic theory with its anthropological and economic applications. Avoid relying primarily on low-quality SEO articles, generic social proof mimetic theory blogs, or unsupported “best practice” claims. Where useful, distinguish established research findings from professional conventions and informed recommendations.

Pay particular attention to the relationship between this module and principles such as **signal truth, front-loaded versus deferred cost, vulnerable-audience standard, and compliance as floor**. Connect these concepts to concrete analytical and design decisions rather than discussing them only theoretically.

Create a professional framework that behavioral researchers, brand strategists, and social scientists could actually use in a real project. The final material should therefore function simultaneously as a learning resource, a practical reference, and a working methodology for behavioral strategy, brand communication, and trend analysis.

Throughout the module, prioritize language that is:

* Precise without being jargon-heavy
* Empirical without being statistical theater
* Confident about what the evidence establishes
* Explicit about what the evidence does not establish
* Conditional where the findings are conditional
* Practical where the decisions are practical

Where a claim depends on context (platform, audience, topic, measurement), explicitly explain the trade-off and the boundary condition rather than presenting it as an absolute rule.

End the module with **Mode scan checklist**, **Authenticity checklist**, **Disclosure checklist**, and a **Delivery gate checklist** that a professional team can use before acting on behavioral strategy, brand communication, and trend analysis.

The final result should be comprehensive enough to serve as an advanced professional training module, but organized clearly enough that a learner can study it progressively and apply each concept in behavioral strategy, brand communication, and trend analysis.
```
