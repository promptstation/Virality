# Step 2 — Generated Prompt (Result of the Reusable Prompt)

```
Develop a comprehensive, professional-level learning module on **“Classic Conformity Research: Asch, Sherif, and the Conditions of Following”** as part of an advanced course on how others' behavior — observed, measured, and imitated — drives adoption, sharing, and trend formation. The module should what the classic conformity experiments establish about the conditions of social influence — and how to read them as design parameters.

The material should teach the experimental record: what makes people conform (ambiguity, unanimity, group size, commitment) and what breaks it (a single ally, a clear standard). Go beyond the folk belief that 'people always follow the crowd' and examine the paradigms' exact conditions and the replications that bound them.

Reconstruct Sherif's autokinetic study: under true ambiguity, groups form shared norms, and the norm persists after individuals leave — the origin of group standards.

Reconstruct Asch's line studies: with a visible majority giving a wrong answer, a substantial minority conforms publicly, and private judgment tracks the public response less than intuition assumes.

Extract the conditions: ambiguity is the engine (conformity collapses when the standard is objective and clear), unanimity is the switch (a single dissenter breaks the majority's power), and group size saturates (three to four conformers carry most of the effect).

Teach the commitment and identity amplifiers: public commitment and in-group identity raise conformity, and the private-public gap is the compliance-versus-acceptance distinction.

Introduce minority influence: a consistent, confident minority can shift the group's norm over time — the experimental root of trend formation and opinion leadership.

Include practical methods for working at a professional level:
* Scoring a decision context for ambiguity (is there an objective standard?)
* Auditing the visible majority: size, unanimity, and the presence of a dissenter
* Designing a conformity experiment: majority condition, dissenter condition, public/private measures
* Measuring the private-public gap (compliance versus acceptance)
* Estimating the group-size saturation point for a specific population

Include practical exercises that require the learner to:
1. Score a target decision for ambiguity, majority size, unanimity, and ally presence; predict the conformity level with the paradigm's numbers.
2. Design the two-condition experiment (unanimous majority vs. majority + one dissenter) with the public and private measures.
3. Take a real adoption case and diagnose it: which conditions were present, which were missing, and what the data should show.
4. Estimate the saturation point: from a dataset of adoption counts, show where adding more visible adopters stops adding effect.
5. Design a minority-influence seeding plan: the consistent message, the credible seed, and the time horizon the paradigm implies.
6. Write the ethics review for a visible-majority campaign: authenticity, coercion check, and the vulnerable-minority check.

For each exercise, provide the scenario, objective, constraints, expected deliverable, evaluation criteria, and an expert-quality example solution where appropriate.

Structure the material progressively, beginning with the two paradigms and their exact conditions, moving through the design parameters (ambiguity, unanimity, size, ally), then to conformity design with the breakers (ally, standard) built in. Establish clear conceptual distinctions between **ambiguity, unanimity, compliance, acceptance, and minority influence** before showing how they interact.

Use professional terminology from social psychology, behavioral economics, and cultural theory where relevant, but explain specialized terminology in clear language. Do not make the material sound like generic AI-generated advice; the register should be that of an advanced professional training module used by behavioral researchers, brand strategists, and social scientists.

Research the subject using high-quality professional and academic sources where external research materially strengthens the material. Prioritize authoritative sources: the conformity literature (Asch, Sherif), Cialdini's social-proof research, the norm-influence literature, and Girard's mimetic theory with its anthropological and economic applications. Avoid relying primarily on low-quality SEO articles, generic social proof mimetic theory blogs, or unsupported “best practice” claims. Where useful, distinguish established research findings from professional conventions and informed recommendations.

Pay particular attention to the relationship between this module and principles such as **ambiguity as engine, unanimity as switch, size saturation, and the private-public gap**. Connect these concepts to concrete analytical and design decisions rather than discussing them only theoretically.

Create a professional framework that behavioral researchers, brand strategists, and social scientists could actually use in a real project. The final material should therefore function simultaneously as a learning resource, a practical reference, and a working methodology for behavioral strategy, brand communication, and trend analysis.

Throughout the module, prioritize language that is:

* Precise without being jargon-heavy
* Empirical without being statistical theater
* Confident about what the evidence establishes
* Explicit about what the evidence does not establish
* Conditional where the findings are conditional
* Practical where the decisions are practical

Where a claim depends on context (platform, audience, topic, measurement), explicitly explain the trade-off and the boundary condition rather than presenting it as an absolute rule.

End the module with **Context scoring checklist**, **Experiment design checklist**, **Diagnosis checklist**, and a **Delivery gate checklist** that a professional team can use before acting on behavioral strategy, brand communication, and trend analysis.

The final result should be comprehensive enough to serve as an advanced professional training module, but organized clearly enough that a learner can study it progressively and apply each concept in behavioral strategy, brand communication, and trend analysis.
```
