# Step 2 — Generated Prompt (Result of the Reusable Prompt)

```
Develop a comprehensive, professional-level learning module on **“Synthesis: Proof, Imitation, and the Diffusion System”** as part of an advanced course on how others' behavior — observed, measured, and imitated — drives adoption, sharing, and trend formation. The module should how proof and mimesis operate as a system — conditions, structure, measurement, and ethics — and how to integrate them with diffusion, loops, and network topology into one adoption program.

The material should teach the system layer: the proof-imitation program running as a standing system, and its integration with the other virality mechanics. Go beyond the tactic habit (one proof element here, one imitation play there) and examine how the pieces interact — the conformity conditions set where, the model chain sets what, the measurement proves it, the ethics holds the line.

Teach the operating model: the conformity conditions select the context (ambiguity, salience), the mimetic structure selects the object and the model, the platform instruments carry the proof, the experiment proves the effect, and the ethics review holds the line — the five as one system.

Build the proof-imitation program: the baseline measurement, the model-chain mapping, the instrument placement, the pre-registered experiment, and the standing ethics review — the program's standing document and its cadence.

Integrate with diffusion: the proof and imitation drive the S-curve's slope — the adoption rate is the proof effect plus the product effect, and the curve's shape is the system's state.

Integrate with K-factor and networks: the proof converts the invited (the loop), the model chain is the network topology (who is imitated is the graph's structure), and the system's ceiling is the network's saturation.

Run the synthesis case: a full adoption campaign — the baseline, the context scoring, the model-chain design, the instrument plan, the experiment, the ethics sign-off — from brief to post-mortem, with the post-mortem feeding the next baseline.

Include practical methods for working at a professional level:
* The program's standing document: the baseline, the chain, the instruments, the experiment, the ethics record
* The integration analysis: proof effect on the S-curve slope, on the K-factor, on the network saturation
* The synthesis case run: brief → design → measurement → sign-off → post-mortem
* The post-mortem feedback: the results updating the baseline and the chain map
* The cadence: the quarterly program review, the standing ethics re-scan

Include practical exercises that require the learner to:
1. Build the program's standing document for a real adoption case: the baseline, the chain, the instruments, the experiment, the ethics record.
2. Run the integration analysis: the proof effect on the S-curve slope, the loop conversion, and the network spread — with the three reads compared.
3. Execute the synthesis case: the full campaign run, brief to post-mortem, with the sign-off chain documented.
4. Diagnose a stalled adoption by component: the test per component, the finding, and the fix.
5. Design the quarterly cadence: the review agenda, the standing updates, the ethics re-scope.
6. Write the system's integrity review: the ethics record, the aggregate checks, and the stale-map risk — with the conditions for the next run.

For each exercise, provide the scenario, objective, constraints, expected deliverable, evaluation criteria, and an expert-quality example solution where appropriate.

Structure the material progressively, beginning with the five-component operating model, moving through the program, the integrations, and the synthesis case, then to the standing system: cadence, dashboard, and integrity review. Establish clear conceptual distinctions between **context condition, model chain, proof instrument, experiment, and ethics line** before showing how they interact.

Use professional terminology from social psychology, behavioral economics, and cultural theory where relevant, but explain specialized terminology in clear language. Do not make the material sound like generic AI-generated advice; the register should be that of an advanced professional training module used by behavioral researchers, brand strategists, and social scientists.

Research the subject using high-quality professional and academic sources where external research materially strengthens the material. Prioritize authoritative sources: the conformity literature (Asch, Sherif), Cialdini's social-proof research, the norm-influence literature, and Girard's mimetic theory with its anthropological and economic applications. Avoid relying primarily on low-quality SEO articles, generic social proof mimetic theory blogs, or unsupported “best practice” claims. Where useful, distinguish established research findings from professional conventions and informed recommendations.

Pay particular attention to the relationship between this module and principles such as **component integrity, the three reads, cadence discipline, and post-mortem feedback**. Connect these concepts to concrete analytical and design decisions rather than discussing them only theoretically.

Create a professional framework that behavioral researchers, brand strategists, and social scientists could actually use in a real project. The final material should therefore function simultaneously as a learning resource, a practical reference, and a working methodology for behavioral strategy, brand communication, and trend analysis.

* Empirical without being statistical theater
* Confident about what the evidence establishes

Where a claim depends on context (platform, audience, topic, measurement), explicitly explain the trade-off and the boundary condition rather than presenting it as an absolute rule.
```
