# Synthesis: Proof, Imitation, and the Diffusion System: Professional Module Builder

## Mission

Produce professional-level material on how proof and mimesis operate as a system — conditions, structure, measurement, and ethics — and how to integrate them with diffusion, loops, and network topology into one adoption program. The unit of work is a defensible artifact — a learning module, an evidence brief, a case diagnosis, or a strategy memo — in which every claim is tied to a source, a mechanism, and a boundary condition.

Optimize simultaneously for:

- empirical precision (what the canonical studies and works actually found)
- conceptual clarity (the core terms kept strictly distinct)
- causal discipline (correlational and causal claims never blurred)
- practical utility (a practitioner can act on the output the same day)
- intellectual honesty (limits and boundary conditions as visible as the findings)

Do not produce generic advice or pop-science hype. The register is that of an advanced professional training program: confident where the evidence is strong, explicitly conditional where it is not.

## Use Cases

### New professional learning module
When asked to create a full module, course unit, or workshop on this topic:
1. Establish scope: audience, depth, and which claims the module must cover.
2. Build the evidence base from primary sources and canonical works.
3. Develop the core mechanisms and diagnostic logic.
4. Add method, critique, and application sections.
5. End with exercises and the delivery checklists.
6. Deliver the complete artifact with no placeholders.

### Brief or leadership memo
When asked for a short document that must decide something:
1. State the question the document must answer in one sentence.
2. Include only findings strong enough to bear that decision, each with its mechanism and source.
3. Convert results into plain-language implications.
4. End with the specific decisions the evidence changes.
5. Cap length; cut context before cutting caveats.

### Case diagnosis
When asked to explain why a specific piece of content, campaign, or product spread (or did not):
1. Identify the operative mechanisms — never a single assumed cause.
2. Map the case onto the diagnostic framework of this skill.
3. Run the differential: which mechanisms were active, which alternatives were considered and set aside.
4. State confidence and the evidence that would revise the diagnosis.

### Strategy design
When asked to design a campaign, content system, or program around this lever:
1. Fix the objective, audience, and platform context.
2. Select the mechanisms from this skill that fit that context.
3. Specify implementation, sequencing, and the measurement plan.
4. State the boundary conditions under which the plan breaks.

## Core Output Requirements

Regardless of format:

- Every named finding or claim carries its source or mechanism in the same breath.
- Correlational and causal claims are labeled as such wherever they matter.
- Effect claims carry their metric and a plain-language rendering.
- Boundary conditions are stated as explicit “applies when / breaks down when” conditions.
- Examples use real, named studies, campaigns, or artifacts — not invented case studies.
- No padding sections, generic intro/outro filler, or advice that would read the same for any topic.

## Phase 1 — Scope and Claim Audit

### Artifact and audience
What the user actually needs (module vs. memo vs. diagnosis) and who reads it. Different readers make different cuts of the same material.

### Claim inventory
List the claims the artifact will make. For each, note the source or mechanism that supports it and whether that support is correlational, experimental, or canonical. Downgrade or cut unsupported claims — not the caveat.

### Depth budget
Match length to the decision the artifact serves. The fix for a weak document is choosing stronger claims to carry it, not adding hedges to weaker ones.

## Phase 2 — Foundations and Core Content

1. Teach the operating model: the conformity conditions select the context (ambiguity, salience), the mimetic structure selects the object and the model, the platform instruments carry the proof, the experiment proves the effect, and the ethics review holds the line — the five as one system.
2. Build the proof-imitation program: the baseline measurement, the model-chain mapping, the instrument placement, the pre-registered experiment, and the standing ethics review — the program's standing document and its cadence.
3. Integrate with diffusion: the proof and imitation drive the S-curve's slope — the adoption rate is the proof effect plus the product effect, and the curve's shape is the system's state.
4. Integrate with K-factor and networks: the proof converts the invited (the loop), the model chain is the network topology (who is imitated is the graph's structure), and the system's ceiling is the network's saturation.
5. Run the synthesis case: a full adoption campaign — the baseline, the context scoring, the model-chain design, the instrument plan, the experiment, the ethics sign-off — from brief to post-mortem, with the post-mortem feeding the next baseline.

## Phase 3 — Method and Technique

- The program's standing document: the baseline, the chain, the instruments, the experiment, the ethics record
- The integration analysis: proof effect on the S-curve slope, on the K-factor, on the network saturation
- The synthesis case run: brief → design → measurement → sign-off → post-mortem
- The post-mortem feedback: the results updating the baseline and the chain map
- The cadence: the quarterly program review, the standing ethics re-scan

## Phase 4 — Analysis and Diagnosis

- Diagnose a stalled adoption by system component: the context (conditions), the structure (chain), the signal (instruments), or the line (ethics) — each with its test.
- Verify the integration: the proof effect must show in the curve's slope, the loop's conversion, and the network's spread — the three read the same.
- Detect the component failure the aggregate hides: a good curve with a manipulated proof is a system failure.
- Read the post-mortem as the system's learning: the next baseline is the previous result, and the chain map is updated from the observed imitation.

## Phase 5 — Application and Design

- Run the program on a cadence: the quarterly review, the standing document, the ethics re-scan.
- Design the synthesis case as the practice: one full run per quarter, with the post-mortem feeding the next.
- Instrument the integration: the three reads (curve, loop, network) on one dashboard.
- Scale the program: the team roles (the measurer, the model-chain analyst, the ethics reviewer) and the sign-off chain.

## Phase 6 — Ethics and Boundary Conditions

- The system's integrity is the ethics component's integrity: a program that bypasses the review is a program that manufactures the collapse.
- The aggregate hides the component: the KPI must include the ethics record, not only the curve.
- The fickle model (the chain's mobility) means the program must be re-mapped regularly; the standing map is a standing liability if stale.

## Phase 7 — Exercises and Assessment

For learning artifacts, include exercises that force the diagnosis skill, not recall. Use the standard set:

1. Build the program's standing document for a real adoption case: the baseline, the chain, the instruments, the experiment, the ethics record.
2. Run the integration analysis: the proof effect on the S-curve slope, the loop conversion, and the network spread — with the three reads compared.
3. Execute the synthesis case: the full campaign run, brief to post-mortem, with the sign-off chain documented.
4. Diagnose a stalled adoption by component: the test per component, the finding, and the fix.
5. Design the quarterly cadence: the review agenda, the standing updates, the ethics re-scope.
6. Write the system's integrity review: the ethics record, the aggregate checks, and the stale-map risk — with the conditions for the next run.

For each exercise provide scenario, objective, constraints, expected deliverable, evaluation criteria, and an expert-quality example solution where appropriate.

## Phase 8 — Quality Gate and Delivery

Before delivering, run the delivery checklists. The artifact fails the gate on any “no.”

### Program document
- The baseline is current
- The chain is mapped
- The instruments are placed
- The experiment is pre-registered
- The ethics record is signed

### Integration reads
- The curve slope shows the proof effect
- The loop conversion is measured
- The network spread is tracked

### Synthesis run
- The full case is executed
- The sign-off chain is documented
- The post-mortem is scheduled

### Delivery gate
- The integrity review is run
- The stale-map risk is checked
- The next cadence is set

## Anti-Patterns

the tactic without the system; the aggregate without the component; the program without the cadence; the chain map that goes stale; the ethics review as a formality; the KPI that excludes the record.

## Decision Heuristic

Before any claim or recommendation, ask:

1. Which component is the bottleneck — context, structure, signal, or line?
2. Do the three reads (curve, loop, network) agree?
3. Is the baseline current, and is the chain map current?
4. Was the ethics review run, and is the sign-off on the document?
5. What does the post-mortem change in the next baseline?

If a claim cannot survive all of these questions, it is not in the artifact.

## Final Principle

The goal is not one clever proof. It is a system that knows its context, its chain, its signal, and its line — and that learns, on a cadence, from every run.
