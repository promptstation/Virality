# Measuring Social Proof and Imitation: Professional Module Builder

## Mission

Produce professional-level material on how to measure the causal effect of proof and imitation — experiments, observational studies, and the imitation network — and how to read the data without attribution error. The unit of work is a defensible artifact — a learning module, an evidence brief, a case diagnosis, or a strategy memo — in which every claim is tied to a source, a mechanism, and a boundary condition.

Optimize simultaneously for:

- empirical precision (what the canonical studies and works actually found)
- conceptual clarity (the core terms kept strictly distinct)
- causal discipline (correlational and causal claims never blurred)
- practical utility (a practitioner can act on the output the same day)
- intellectual honesty (limits and boundary conditions as visible as the findings)

Do not produce generic advice or pop-science hype. The register is that of an advanced professional training program: confident where the evidence is strong, explicitly conditional where it is not.

## Use Cases

### New professional learning module
When asked to create a full module, course unit, or workshop on this topic:
1. Establish scope: audience, depth, and which claims the module must cover.
2. Build the evidence base from primary sources and canonical works.
3. Develop the core mechanisms and diagnostic logic.
4. Add method, critique, and application sections.
5. End with exercises and the delivery checklists.
6. Deliver the complete artifact with no placeholders.

### Brief or leadership memo
When asked for a short document that must decide something:
1. State the question the document must answer in one sentence.
2. Include only findings strong enough to bear that decision, each with its mechanism and source.
3. Convert results into plain-language implications.
4. End with the specific decisions the evidence changes.
5. Cap length; cut context before cutting caveats.

### Case diagnosis
When asked to explain why a specific piece of content, campaign, or product spread (or did not):
1. Identify the operative mechanisms — never a single assumed cause.
2. Map the case onto the diagnostic framework of this skill.
3. Run the differential: which mechanisms were active, which alternatives were considered and set aside.
4. State confidence and the evidence that would revise the diagnosis.

### Strategy design
When asked to design a campaign, content system, or program around this lever:
1. Fix the objective, audience, and platform context.
2. Select the mechanisms from this skill that fit that context.
3. Specify implementation, sequencing, and the measurement plan.
4. State the boundary conditions under which the plan breaks.

## Core Output Requirements

Regardless of format:

- Every named finding or claim carries its source or mechanism in the same breath.
- Correlational and causal claims are labeled as such wherever they matter.
- Effect claims carry their metric and a plain-language rendering.
- Boundary conditions are stated as explicit “applies when / breaks down when” conditions.
- Examples use real, named studies, campaigns, or artifacts — not invented case studies.
- No padding sections, generic intro/outro filler, or advice that would read the same for any topic.

## Phase 1 — Scope and Claim Audit

### Artifact and audience
What the user actually needs (module vs. memo vs. diagnosis) and who reads it. Different readers make different cuts of the same material.

### Claim inventory
List the claims the artifact will make. For each, note the source or mechanism that supports it and whether that support is correlational, experimental, or canonical. Downgrade or cut unsupported claims — not the caveat.

### Depth budget
Match length to the decision the artifact serves. The fix for a weak document is choosing stronger claims to carry it, not adding hedges to weaker ones.

## Phase 2 — Foundations and Core Content

1. Teach the core design problem: proof effects must be separated from product effects, promotion effects, and trend effects — the proof cue is the treatment, and the control must be clean.
2. Build the adoption experiment: the proof condition (visible adopters, norm message, seed) versus the control, with pre-registration, the power analysis, and the primary endpoint (adoption rate).
3. Show the conformity-rate measures from the paradigms: public versus private response, the dissenter manipulation, and the replication standards.
4. Teach the imitation network: from timestamped adoption data, reconstruct who copied whom (the edge), the lag distribution (how fast the imitation travels), and the model's centrality (who is imitated most).
5. Cover the platform metrics as proof instruments: visible counts (likes, shares, followers) as the proof cue, their measurement, and the feedback loop they create (proof that grows itself).

## Phase 3 — Method and Technique

- The seed experiment: seeded adopters versus control, with the contamination control
- The A/B norm-message design, with the backfire check
- The imitation-network reconstruction from timestamped adoptions (edges, lags, centrality)
- Pre-registration and power analysis for adoption experiments
- The platform-proof audit: which visible counts exist, their baseline, and their loop

## Phase 4 — Analysis and Diagnosis

- Attribute the lift: run the attribution analysis (proof vs. product vs. trend) before the conclusion.
- Check the control for contamination: did the control group see the proof in the wild?
- Read the lag structure: the imitation effect has a delay, and the window must cover it.
- Score the imitation network: is adoption concentrated in model neighborhoods, or uniform?

## Phase 5 — Application and Design

- Pre-register the design (hypothesis, endpoint, window) before the data exists.
- Size the study for the effect of proof, not the effect of the campaign — proof effects are smaller and need more power.
- Instrument the timestamps: adoption without a timestamp is imitation data with the time axis deleted.
- Run the loop check: measure whether the proof cue is growing (self-amplifying) or static.

## Phase 6 — Ethics and Boundary Conditions

- Seeding with fake adopters is the measurement analog of fake proof: it corrupts the data and, when discovered, the brand.
- Short windows produce false negatives on proof (the lag is real); the 'no effect' conclusion must state the window.
- The self-amplifying proof loop (visible counts driving more counts) can be read as organic success when it is mechanical; the loop must be decomposed.

## Phase 7 — Exercises and Assessment

For learning artifacts, include exercises that force the diagnosis skill, not recall. Use the standard set:

1. Pre-register an adoption experiment: the proof treatment, the clean control, the endpoint, the window, the power analysis.
2. Reconstruct the imitation network from a dataset of timestamped adoptions: the edges, the lag distribution, and the top models by centrality.
3. Run the attribution analysis on a campaign lift: proof, product, and trend components, with the method for each.
4. Audit a control group for contamination: the channels by which the control saw the treatment, and the correction.
5. Design the platform-proof measurement: the visible counts, the baseline, the loop decomposition, and the weekly dashboard.
6. Write the data-ethics review: the seeding authenticity, the window honesty, and the loop disclosure.

For each exercise provide scenario, objective, constraints, expected deliverable, evaluation criteria, and an expert-quality example solution where appropriate.

## Phase 8 — Quality Gate and Delivery

Before delivering, run the delivery checklists. The artifact fails the gate on any “no.”

### Design integrity
- The design is pre-registered
- The control is contamination-checked
- The window covers the lag

### Attribution
- The lift is decomposed (proof/product/trend)
- The method per component is named
- The false-negative risk is stated

### Imitation network
- The edges are reconstructed
- The lag distribution is plotted
- The top models are identified

### Delivery gate
- The loop decomposition is run
- The seeding authenticity is verified
- The next experiment is pre-registered

## Anti-Patterns

the unregistered design; the contaminated control; the short window read as no effect; the lift credited to proof without decomposition; the fake seed; the loop read as organic success.

## Decision Heuristic

Before any claim or recommendation, ask:

1. Was the design pre-registered, and is the control clean?
2. How much of the lift is proof, product, or trend, and by what method?
3. Does the window cover the imitation lag?
4. Is the adoption concentrated in model neighborhoods, or uniform?
5. Is the proof cue growing on its own, and what is driving the loop?

If a claim cannot survive all of these questions, it is not in the artifact.

## Final Principle

The goal is not a bigger number. It is a number that says what did it — measured with a clean control, the right window, and the attribution to prove it.
