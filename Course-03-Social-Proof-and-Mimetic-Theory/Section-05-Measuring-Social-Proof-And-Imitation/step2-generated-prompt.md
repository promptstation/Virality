# Step 2 — Generated Prompt (Result of the Reusable Prompt)

```
Develop a comprehensive, professional-level learning module on **“Measuring Social Proof and Imitation”** as part of an advanced course on how others' behavior — observed, measured, and imitated — drives adoption, sharing, and trend formation. The module should how to measure the causal effect of proof and imitation — experiments, observational studies, and the imitation network — and how to read the data without attribution error.

The material should teach the evidence craft: designs that separate proof from product, the imitation network with lags, and the field-experiment standards. Go beyond the dashboard view (more followers means more proof, so we won) and examine the designs that establish causality — seed experiments, A/B norm messages, and the contamination controls.

Teach the core design problem: proof effects must be separated from product effects, promotion effects, and trend effects — the proof cue is the treatment, and the control must be clean.

Build the adoption experiment: the proof condition (visible adopters, norm message, seed) versus the control, with pre-registration, the power analysis, and the primary endpoint (adoption rate).

Show the conformity-rate measures from the paradigms: public versus private response, the dissenter manipulation, and the replication standards.

Teach the imitation network: from timestamped adoption data, reconstruct who copied whom (the edge), the lag distribution (how fast the imitation travels), and the model's centrality (who is imitated most).

Cover the platform metrics as proof instruments: visible counts (likes, shares, followers) as the proof cue, their measurement, and the feedback loop they create (proof that grows itself).

Include practical methods for working at a professional level:
* The seed experiment: seeded adopters versus control, with the contamination control
* The A/B norm-message design, with the backfire check
* The imitation-network reconstruction from timestamped adoptions (edges, lags, centrality)
* Pre-registration and power analysis for adoption experiments
* The platform-proof audit: which visible counts exist, their baseline, and their loop

Include practical exercises that require the learner to:
1. Pre-register an adoption experiment: the proof treatment, the clean control, the endpoint, the window, the power analysis.
2. Reconstruct the imitation network from a dataset of timestamped adoptions: the edges, the lag distribution, and the top models by centrality.
3. Run the attribution analysis on a campaign lift: proof, product, and trend components, with the method for each.
4. Audit a control group for contamination: the channels by which the control saw the treatment, and the correction.
5. Design the platform-proof measurement: the visible counts, the baseline, the loop decomposition, and the weekly dashboard.
6. Write the data-ethics review: the seeding authenticity, the window honesty, and the loop disclosure.

For each exercise, provide the scenario, objective, constraints, expected deliverable, evaluation criteria, and an expert-quality example solution where appropriate.

Structure the material progressively, beginning with the design problem (proof vs. product vs. trend), moving through the experiment, the imitation network, and the platform loop, then to a pre-registered measurement program with attribution built in. Establish clear conceptual distinctions between **proof effect, product effect, attribution, lag, and the self-amplifying loop** before showing how they interact.

Use professional terminology from social psychology, behavioral economics, and cultural theory where relevant, but explain specialized terminology in clear language. Do not make the material sound like generic AI-generated advice; the register should be that of an advanced professional training module used by behavioral researchers, brand strategists, and social scientists.

Research the subject using high-quality professional and academic sources where external research materially strengthens the material. Prioritize authoritative sources: the conformity literature (Asch, Sherif), Cialdini's social-proof research, the norm-influence literature, and Girard's mimetic theory with its anthropological and economic applications. Avoid relying primarily on low-quality SEO articles, generic social proof mimetic theory blogs, or unsupported “best practice” claims. Where useful, distinguish established research findings from professional conventions and informed recommendations.

Pay particular attention to the relationship between this module and principles such as **clean controls, pre-registration, lag structure, and loop decomposition**. Connect these concepts to concrete analytical and design decisions rather than discussing them only theoretically.

Create a professional framework that behavioral researchers, brand strategists, and social scientists could actually use in a real project. The final material should therefore function simultaneously as a learning resource, a practical reference, and a working methodology for behavioral strategy, brand communication, and trend analysis.

Throughout the module, prioritize language that is:

* Precise without being jargon-heavy
* Empirical without being statistical theater
* Confident about what the evidence establishes
* Explicit about what the evidence does not establish
* Conditional where the findings are conditional
* Practical where the decisions are practical

Where a claim depends on context (platform, audience, topic, measurement), explicitly explain the trade-off and the boundary condition rather than presenting it as an absolute rule.

End the module with **Design integrity checklist**, **Attribution checklist**, **Imitation network checklist**, and a **Delivery gate checklist** that a professional team can use before acting on behavioral strategy, brand communication, and trend analysis.

The final result should be comprehensive enough to serve as an advanced professional training module, but organized clearly enough that a learner can study it progressively and apply each concept in behavioral strategy, brand communication, and trend analysis.
```
