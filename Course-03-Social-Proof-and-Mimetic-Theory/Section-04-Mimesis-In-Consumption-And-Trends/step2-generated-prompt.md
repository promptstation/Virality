# Step 2 — Generated Prompt (Result of the Reusable Prompt)

```
Develop a comprehensive, professional-level learning module on **“Mimesis in Consumption and Trends”** as part of an advanced course on how others' behavior — observed, measured, and imitated — drives adoption, sharing, and trend formation. The module should how mimetic dynamics operate at market scale — trend cycles, status goods, aspirational modeling — and how to read a trend's phase and model chain.

The material should teach the market mechanics of imitation: trend life cycles, the model chain as transmission route, and the bandwagon–snob interaction. Go beyond the trend-as-hype view (trends are irrational and simply fade) and examine the cycle evidence and the model-chain data showing trends are structured, not random.

Explain the trend cycle: innovation (a model makes the object desirable), diffusion (mimetic convergence), saturation (the object becomes common), and exhaustion (the model moves; the object's desire value collapses) — with the cycle's length set by the model's mobility.

Teach status goods and conspicuous consumption: the desire value of a status good is the distance it encodes, and the Veblen logic — price and inaccessibility as the signal — is mimetic mechanics in product form.

Show aspirational modeling in advertising: the cast is the model, and the ad works when the audience imitates the cast's desire, not merely the cast's purchase — the gap between casting and identification.

Teach the bandwagon and snob effects: the bandwagon accelerates diffusion (the crowd itself becomes a model), the snob effect decays it (commonness destroys value) — the two forces set the cycle's shape.

Read the modern visible model chain: on social platforms the model chain is public and real time — the influencer, the early adopter, the copycat — and the phase can be read from the chain's structure, not just the sales curve.

Include practical methods for working at a professional level:
* Mapping a trend's model chain (who is imitating whom, with timestamps)
* Scoring a trend's phase: innovation, diffusion, saturation, exhaustion
* Tracking the bandwagon signal (adoption velocity vs. commonness)
* Auditing an ad's modeling structure: cast as model, identification gap
* Measuring the snob decay: the price and prestige response to rising commonness

Include practical exercises that require the learner to:
1. Map the model chain of a live trend with timestamps; score its phase and predict the next phase with the model-mobility argument.
2. Decompose a status good's price into the autonomous (cost, function) and the mimetic (distance, signal) components.
3. Audit a brand's advertising for modeling structure: the cast, the model function, the identification gap — and the fix.
4. Plot a trend's adoption curve with the bandwagon and snob components separated; identify the phase turning points.
5. Design the trend-entry plan for a product: the phase to enter, the chain position, the distance management, and the exit triggers.
6. Write the ethics review for a trend campaign: the authenticity of the model, the FOMO check, and the exclusion check on the signal.

For each exercise, provide the scenario, objective, constraints, expected deliverable, evaluation criteria, and an expert-quality example solution where appropriate.

Structure the material progressively, beginning with the cycle and the model chain, moving through status goods, modeling, and the bandwagon–snob interaction, then to a phase-timed trend strategy with an exit plan. Establish clear conceptual distinctions between **trend phase, model chain, bandwagon effect, and snob effect** before showing how they interact.

Use professional terminology from social psychology, behavioral economics, and cultural theory where relevant, but explain specialized terminology in clear language. Do not make the material sound like generic AI-generated advice; the register should be that of an advanced professional training module used by behavioral researchers, brand strategists, and social scientists.

Research the subject using high-quality professional and academic sources where external research materially strengthens the material. Prioritize authoritative sources: the conformity literature (Asch, Sherif), Cialdini's social-proof research, the norm-influence literature, and Girard's mimetic theory with its anthropological and economic applications. Avoid relying primarily on low-quality SEO articles, generic social proof mimetic theory blogs, or unsupported “best practice” claims. Where useful, distinguish established research findings from professional conventions and informed recommendations.

Pay particular attention to the relationship between this module and principles such as **cycle structure, model mobility, distance encoding, and the bandwagon–snob balance**. Connect these concepts to concrete analytical and design decisions rather than discussing them only theoretically.

Create a professional framework that behavioral researchers, brand strategists, and social scientists could actually use in a real project. The final material should therefore function simultaneously as a learning resource, a practical reference, and a working methodology for behavioral strategy, brand communication, and trend analysis.

Throughout the module, prioritize language that is:

* Precise without being jargon-heavy
* Empirical without being statistical theater
* Confident about what the evidence establishes
* Explicit about what the evidence does not establish
* Conditional where the findings are conditional
* Practical where the decisions are practical

Where a claim depends on context (platform, audience, topic, measurement), explicitly explain the trade-off and the boundary condition rather than presenting it as an absolute rule.

End the module with **Phase scoring checklist**, **Modeling audit checklist**, **Strategy design checklist**, and a **Delivery gate checklist** that a professional team can use before acting on behavioral strategy, brand communication, and trend analysis.

The final result should be comprehensive enough to serve as an advanced professional training module, but organized clearly enough that a learner can study it progressively and apply each concept in behavioral strategy, brand communication, and trend analysis.
```
