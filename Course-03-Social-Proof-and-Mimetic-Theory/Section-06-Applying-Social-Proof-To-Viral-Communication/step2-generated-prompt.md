# Step 2 — Generated Prompt (Result of the Reusable Prompt)

```
Develop a comprehensive, professional-level learning module on **“Applying Social Proof to Viral Communication”** as part of an advanced course on how others' behavior — observed, measured, and imitated — drives adoption, sharing, and trend formation. The module should how to design social proof into viral communication — the platform instruments, the proof architecture, and the integration with referral and algorithmic systems.

The material should teach the proof design: which visible evidence to place, when, and in what format — and how the proof rides with the content and converts the invited. Go beyond the assumption that proof is a marketing claim ('thousands agree!') and examine the platform instruments as measured proof cues and the integration with the systems that read them.

Inventory the platform-native proof instruments: visible counts (likes, shares, followers), social actions (comments, tags, reactions), live signals (live viewers, trending), and reference framing ('people like you', 'your network') — each with its measured effect on uptake.

Teach the proof placement: the proof must be visible at the decision point (before the share, before the click, before the follow) — the placement rule and the platform's native slots for it.

Design the proof architecture of a viral asset: the evidence that rides with the content (the adoption curve visible in the asset, the named users, the running count) and the follow-up proof (the post-launch visible momentum).

Integrate with the referral loop (K-factor): the invited sees the proof of adoption at the landing — the proof that converts the cold visitor — and with the algorithm: the engagement counts are the proof the ranking system reads, and the proof and the algorithm are the same signal.

Build the proof calendar: the proof to build (generated, honest), the proof to borrow (partnership, press, community), and the proof never to fake (the non-negotiable line).

Include practical methods for working at a professional level:
* The platform-proof audit per asset: which instruments are present, their baseline, their placement
* Proof placement design: the decision point, the native slot, the format
* The referral-landing proof: the adoption evidence at the invited's landing, measured
* The algorithm-proof integration: the engagement signal the ranking reads, and the test of its effect
* The proof calendar: build / borrow / never-fake classification per campaign

Include practical exercises that require the learner to:
1. Audit five assets for proof architecture: instruments present, placement at the decision point, and the missing element — with the fix per asset.
2. Design the proof placement for a new asset: the decision point, the native slot, the format, and the baseline to beat.
3. Build the referral-landing proof: the adoption evidence, the A/B design, and the conversion endpoint.
4. Decompose an algorithm loop: the engagement-proof signal, the distribution effect, and the growth of the proof — with the numbers.
5. Draft the proof calendar for a quarter: the build / borrow / never-fake classification per item, with the source and the authenticity check.
6. Write the proof-ethics review: the never-fake list, the shaming check, and the engagement-bait line.

For each exercise, provide the scenario, objective, constraints, expected deliverable, evaluation criteria, and an expert-quality example solution where appropriate.

Structure the material progressively, beginning with the platform instruments and the placement rule, moving through the architecture, the referral integration, and the algorithm loop, then to a proof calendar running on the build/borrow/never-fake discipline. Establish clear conceptual distinctions between **proof instrument, decision point, proof architecture, and the self-amplifying loop** before showing how they interact.

Use professional terminology from social psychology, behavioral economics, and cultural theory where relevant, but explain specialized terminology in clear language. Do not make the material sound like generic AI-generated advice; the register should be that of an advanced professional training module used by behavioral researchers, brand strategists, and social scientists.

Research the subject using high-quality professional and academic sources where external research materially strengthens the material. Prioritize authoritative sources: the conformity literature (Asch, Sherif), Cialdini's social-proof research, the norm-influence literature, and Girard's mimetic theory with its anthropological and economic applications. Avoid relying primarily on low-quality SEO articles, generic social proof mimetic theory blogs, or unsupported “best practice” claims. Where useful, distinguish established research findings from professional conventions and informed recommendations.

Pay particular attention to the relationship between this module and principles such as **placement at the decision point, evidence that rides with the asset, loop decomposition, and the never-fake line**. Connect these concepts to concrete analytical and design decisions rather than discussing them only theoretically.

Create a professional framework that behavioral researchers, brand strategists, and social scientists could actually use in a real project. The final material should therefore function simultaneously as a learning resource, a practical reference, and a working methodology for behavioral strategy, brand communication, and trend analysis.

Throughout the module, prioritize language that is:

* Precise without being jargon-heavy
* Empirical without being statistical theater
* Confident about what the evidence establishes
* Explicit about what the evidence does not establish
* Conditional where the findings are conditional
* Practical where the decisions are practical

Where a claim depends on context (platform, audience, topic, measurement), explicitly explain the trade-off and the boundary condition rather than presenting it as an absolute rule.

End the module with **Placement audit checklist**, **Architecture checklist**, **Integration checklist**, and a **Delivery gate checklist** that a professional team can use before acting on behavioral strategy, brand communication, and trend analysis.

The final result should be comprehensive enough to serve as an advanced professional training module, but organized clearly enough that a learner can study it progressively and apply each concept in behavioral strategy, brand communication, and trend analysis.
```
