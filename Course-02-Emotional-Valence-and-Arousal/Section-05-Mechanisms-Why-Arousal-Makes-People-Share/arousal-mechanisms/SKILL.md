# Mechanisms: Why Arousal Makes People Share: Professional Module Builder

## Mission

Produce professional-level material on the mechanisms under the effect — action readiness, self-presentation, bonding, urgency — and the mediation chain that links them to the transmission. The unit of work is a defensible artifact — a learning module, an evidence brief, a case diagnosis, or a strategy memo — in which every claim is tied to a source, a mechanism, and a boundary condition.

Optimize simultaneously for:

- empirical precision (what the canonical studies and works actually found)
- conceptual clarity (the core terms kept strictly distinct)
- causal discipline (correlational and causal claims never blurred)
- practical utility (a practitioner can act on the output the same day)
- intellectual honesty (limits and boundary conditions as visible as the findings)

Do not produce generic advice or pop-science hype. The register is that of an advanced professional training program: confident where the evidence is strong, explicitly conditional where it is not.

## Use Cases

### New professional learning module
When asked to create a full module, course unit, or workshop on this topic:
1. Establish scope: audience, depth, and which claims the module must cover.
2. Build the evidence base from primary sources and canonical works.
3. Develop the core mechanisms and diagnostic logic.
4. Add method, critique, and application sections.
5. End with exercises and the delivery checklists.
6. Deliver the complete artifact with no placeholders.

### Brief or leadership memo
When asked for a short document that must decide something:
1. State the question the document must answer in one sentence.
2. Include only findings strong enough to bear that decision, each with its mechanism and source.
3. Convert results into plain-language implications.
4. End with the specific decisions the evidence changes.
5. Cap length; cut context before cutting caveats.

### Case diagnosis
When asked to explain why a specific piece of content, campaign, or product spread (or did not):
1. Identify the operative mechanisms — never a single assumed cause.
2. Map the case onto the diagnostic framework of this skill.
3. Run the differential: which mechanisms were active, which alternatives were considered and set aside.
4. State confidence and the evidence that would revise the diagnosis.

### Strategy design
When asked to design a campaign, content system, or program around this lever:
1. Fix the objective, audience, and platform context.
2. Select the mechanisms from this skill that fit that context.
3. Specify implementation, sequencing, and the measurement plan.
4. State the boundary conditions under which the plan breaks.

## Core Output Requirements

Regardless of format:

- Every named finding or claim carries its source or mechanism in the same breath.
- Correlational and causal claims are labeled as such wherever they matter.
- Effect claims carry their metric and a plain-language rendering.
- Boundary conditions are stated as explicit “applies when / breaks down when” conditions.
- Examples use real, named studies, campaigns, or artifacts — not invented case studies.
- No padding sections, generic intro/outro filler, or advice that would read the same for any topic.

## Phase 1 — Scope and Claim Audit

### Artifact and audience
What the user actually needs (module vs. memo vs. diagnosis) and who reads it. Different readers make different cuts of the same material.

### Claim inventory
List the claims the artifact will make. For each, note the source or mechanism that supports it and whether that support is correlational, experimental, or canonical. Downgrade or cut unsupported claims — not the caveat.

### Depth budget
Match length to the decision the artifact serves. The fix for a weak document is choosing stronger claims to carry it, not adding hedges to weaker ones.

## Phase 2 — Foundations and Core Content

1. Teach action readiness: the activation that needs discharge — sharing as one discharge channel, the energy per the arousal, the channel per the context.
2. Teach self-presentation: the sharer's image per the share — the social currency, the positive and the impressive, the image per the audience.
3. Teach social bonding: the self's diminishment per the high-arousal positive (the awe's shrink), the connection's desire, the inclusion per the state.
4. Teach information urgency: the flag per the negative high-arousal — the threat, the injustice, the warning per the sharer's compulsion.
5. Teach the mediation chain and the substitution: the emotion → arousal → transmission chain per the mediation analyses, and the cases where the practical value or the curiosity carries the transmission without the emotion — the chain per the evidence, the substitution per the case.

## Phase 3 — Method and Technique

- The mechanism inventory: the account per the element (readiness, presentation, bonding, urgency)
- The emotion-mechanism map: the account per the emotion (the awe per the bonding, the anger per the urgency)
- The mediation read: the chain per the evidence, the link per the stage
- The substitution check: the value per the case, the curiosity per the case
- The design note: the design per the mechanism, per the content

## Phase 4 — Analysis and Diagnosis

- Diagnose the mechanism per the case: the account that carries the transmission (the readiness, the presentation, the bonding, the urgency) — the account per the emotion, and the gap per the design.
- Check the chain: the emotion present without the arousal (the misread), the arousal present without the discharge channel (the blocked transmission) — the element per the chain, and the fix.
- Read the substitution: the value or the curiosity carrying the transmission without the emotion — the element per the case, and the read per the substitution.

## Phase 5 — Application and Design

- Map the mechanism per the content: the account per the emotion, the chain per the stage — with the read per the case.
- Design per the mechanism: the discharge channel per the readiness, the image per the presentation, the inclusion per the bonding, the flag per the urgency — with the note per the element.
- Check the substitution per the case: the value, the curiosity — with the read per the element.

## Phase 6 — Ethics and Boundary Conditions

- The mechanism (the account per the emotion) is the analyst's model per the evidence; its use (the design per the account, the prediction per the chain) is per the honesty of the model.
- The urgency mechanism (the flag per the anger, the warning per the anxiety) is the sharer's compulsion per the state; its use (the flag per the injustice, the warning per the threat) is per the dignity of the flag.
- The self-presentation mechanism (the image per the share) is the sharer's state per the audience; its use (the image per the content, the image per the audience) is per the honesty of the image.

## Phase 7 — Exercises and Assessment

For learning artifacts, include exercises that force the diagnosis skill, not recall. Use the standard set:

1. Inventory the four mechanisms: the account per the element — with the chain per the stage.
2. Map the mechanisms on six emotions: the account per the emotion (the awe, the anger, the amusement, the anxiety, the contentment, the sadness) — with the read per the emotion.
3. Read the mediation chain on a case: the emotion, the arousal, the transmission — with the link per the stage.
4. Check the substitution on three cases: the value, the curiosity — with the read per the case.
5. Design per the mechanism for a content type: the channel, the image, the inclusion, the flag — with the note per the element.
6. Build the mechanism reference: the inventory, the map, the chain, the substitution, the design note — as the standing instrument, with the owner.

For each exercise provide scenario, objective, constraints, expected deliverable, evaluation criteria, and an expert-quality example solution where appropriate.

## Phase 8 — Quality Gate and Delivery

### Chain

## Anti-Patterns

the arousal as the black box; the mechanism as the footnote; the account without the emotion; the chain without the mediation; the substitution unexamined; the design without the mechanism.

## Decision Heuristic

Before any claim or recommendation, ask:

1. Which mechanism carries this transmission — the readiness, the presentation, the bonding, the urgency?
2. Is the chain complete — the emotion, the arousal, the channel?
3. Is this the emotion's work, or the value's or the curiosity's?
4. Does the design support the mechanism, per the element?

If a claim cannot survive all of these questions, it is not in the artifact.

## Final Principle

The goal is not the arousal claim. It is the mechanism read per the element — the account per the emotion, the chain per the evidence, the substitution per the case — so the transmission is explained on its machinery, and the machinery says where the design belongs.
