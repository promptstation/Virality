# Step 2 — Generated Prompt (Result of the Reusable Prompt)

```
Develop a comprehensive, professional-level learning module on **“Mechanisms: Why Arousal Makes People Share”** as part of an advanced course on the science of how emotion — its valence and its arousal — drives whether content is shared. The module should the mechanisms under the effect — action readiness, self-presentation, bonding, urgency — and the mediation chain that links them to the transmission.

The material should teach the machinery: the four mechanism accounts, the mediation chain, and the substitution cases. Go beyond the arousal as the black box, and the mechanism as the optional footnote and examine the mechanism per the emotion — which account carries the awe, which carries the anger — and the substitution where the value or the curiosity does the work.

Teach action readiness: the activation that needs discharge — sharing as one discharge channel, the energy per the arousal, the channel per the context.

Teach self-presentation: the sharer's image per the share — the social currency, the positive and the impressive, the image per the audience.

Teach social bonding: the self's diminishment per the high-arousal positive (the awe's shrink), the connection's desire, the inclusion per the state.

Teach information urgency: the flag per the negative high-arousal — the threat, the injustice, the warning per the sharer's compulsion.

Teach the mediation chain and the substitution: the emotion → arousal → transmission chain per the mediation analyses, and the cases where the practical value or the curiosity carries the transmission without the emotion — the chain per the evidence, the substitution per the case.

Include practical methods for working at a professional level:
* The mechanism inventory: the account per the element (readiness, presentation, bonding, urgency)
* The emotion-mechanism map: the account per the emotion (the awe per the bonding, the anger per the urgency)
* The mediation read: the chain per the evidence, the link per the stage
* The substitution check: the value per the case, the curiosity per the case
* The design note: the design per the mechanism, per the content

Include practical exercises that require the learner to:
1. Inventory the four mechanisms: the account per the element — with the chain per the stage.
2. Map the mechanisms on six emotions: the account per the emotion (the awe, the anger, the amusement, the anxiety, the contentment, the sadness) — with the read per the emotion.
3. Read the mediation chain on a case: the emotion, the arousal, the transmission — with the link per the stage.
4. Check the substitution on three cases: the value, the curiosity — with the read per the case.
5. Design per the mechanism for a content type: the channel, the image, the inclusion, the flag — with the note per the element.
6. Build the mechanism reference: the inventory, the map, the chain, the substitution, the design note — as the standing instrument, with the owner.

For each exercise, provide the scenario, objective, constraints, expected deliverable, evaluation criteria, and an expert-quality example solution where appropriate.

Structure the material progressively, beginning with the four mechanism accounts, moving through the mediation chain and the substitution, then to the design per the mechanism, running per the case. Establish clear conceptual distinctions between **readiness, presentation, bonding, urgency, and substitution** before showing how they interact.

Use professional terminology from emotion science, social psychology, and communication research where relevant, but explain specialized terminology in clear language. Do not make the material sound like generic AI-generated advice; the register should be that of an advanced professional training module used by content strategists, brand communicators, and researchers of emotional communication.

Research the subject using high-quality professional and academic sources where external research materially strengthens the material. Prioritize authoritative sources: the emotion-science literature (Ekman's basic-emotion work, Russell's circumplex, the Mehrabian–Russell VAD framework), the viral-content literature (Berger & Milkman 2012, Berger 2011, the Twitter replications), and the affect-measurement literature (LIWC, the NRC Emotion Lexicon, FACS, PANAS). Avoid relying primarily on low-quality SEO articles, generic emotional valence arousal blogs, or unsupported “best practice” claims. Where useful, distinguish established research findings from professional conventions and informed recommendations.

Pay particular attention to the relationship between this module and principles such as **the account per the emotion, the chain per the evidence, and the substitution per the case**. Connect these concepts to concrete analytical and design decisions rather than discussing them only theoretically.

Create a professional framework that content strategists, brand communicators, and researchers of emotional communication could actually use in a real project. The final material should therefore function simultaneously as a learning resource, a practical reference, and a working methodology for designing and analyzing content for emotional transmission.

Throughout the module, prioritize language that is:

* Precise without being jargon-heavy
* Empirical without being statistical theater
* Confident about what the evidence establishes
* Explicit about what the evidence does not establish
* Conditional where the findings are conditional
* Practical where the decisions are practical

Where a claim depends on context (platform, audience, topic, measurement), explicitly explain the trade-off and the boundary condition rather than presenting it as an absolute rule.

End the module with **Mechanisms checklist**, **Urgency checklist**, **Chain checklist**, and a **Delivery gate checklist** that a professional team can use before acting on designing and analyzing content for emotional transmission.

The final result should be comprehensive enough to serve as an advanced professional training module, but organized clearly enough that a learner can study it progressively and apply each concept in designing and analyzing content for emotional transmission.
```
