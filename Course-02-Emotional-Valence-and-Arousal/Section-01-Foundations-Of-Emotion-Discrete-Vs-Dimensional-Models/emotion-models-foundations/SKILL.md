# Foundations of Emotion: Discrete vs. Dimensional Models: Professional Module Builder

## Mission

Produce professional-level material on the two ways emotion is modeled — discrete categories and dimensional values — and why valence and arousal are the working dimensions for transmission. The unit of work is a defensible artifact — a learning module, an evidence brief, a case diagnosis, or a strategy memo — in which every claim is tied to a source, a mechanism, and a boundary condition.

Optimize simultaneously for:

- empirical precision (what the canonical studies and works actually found)
- conceptual clarity (the core terms kept strictly distinct)
- causal discipline (correlational and causal claims never blurred)
- practical utility (a practitioner can act on the output the same day)
- intellectual honesty (limits and boundary conditions as visible as the findings)

Do not produce generic advice or pop-science hype. The register is that of an advanced professional training program: confident where the evidence is strong, explicitly conditional where it is not.

## Use Cases

### New professional learning module
When asked to create a full module, course unit, or workshop on this topic:
1. Establish scope: audience, depth, and which claims the module must cover.
2. Build the evidence base from primary sources and canonical works.
3. Develop the core mechanisms and diagnostic logic.
4. Add method, critique, and application sections.
5. End with exercises and the delivery checklists.
6. Deliver the complete artifact with no placeholders.

### Brief or leadership memo
When asked for a short document that must decide something:
1. State the question the document must answer in one sentence.
2. Include only findings strong enough to bear that decision, each with its mechanism and source.
3. Convert results into plain-language implications.
4. End with the specific decisions the evidence changes.
5. Cap length; cut context before cutting caveats.

### Case diagnosis
When asked to explain why a specific piece of content, campaign, or product spread (or did not):
1. Identify the operative mechanisms — never a single assumed cause.
2. Map the case onto the diagnostic framework of this skill.
3. Run the differential: which mechanisms were active, which alternatives were considered and set aside.
4. State confidence and the evidence that would revise the diagnosis.

### Strategy design
When asked to design a campaign, content system, or program around this lever:
1. Fix the objective, audience, and platform context.
2. Select the mechanisms from this skill that fit that context.
3. Specify implementation, sequencing, and the measurement plan.
4. State the boundary conditions under which the plan breaks.

## Core Output Requirements

Regardless of format:

- Every named finding or claim carries its source or mechanism in the same breath.
- Correlational and causal claims are labeled as such wherever they matter.
- Effect claims carry their metric and a plain-language rendering.
- Boundary conditions are stated as explicit “applies when / breaks down when” conditions.
- Examples use real, named studies, campaigns, or artifacts — not invented case studies.
- No padding sections, generic intro/outro filler, or advice that would read the same for any topic.

## Phase 1 — Scope and Claim Audit

### Artifact and audience
What the user actually needs (module vs. memo vs. diagnosis) and who reads it. Different readers make different cuts of the same material.

### Claim inventory
List the claims the artifact will make. For each, note the source or mechanism that supports it and whether that support is correlational, experimental, or canonical. Downgrade or cut unsupported claims — not the caveat.

### Depth budget
Match length to the decision the artifact serves. The fix for a weak document is choosing stronger claims to carry it, not adding hedges to weaker ones.

## Phase 2 — Foundations and Core Content

1. Define the discrete model: the basic-emotion categories (happiness, sadness, anger, fear, disgust, surprise — Ekman's set), each with its own cause and expression, and its unit (the category label).
2. Define the dimensional model: the continuous values along valence (pleasant–unpleasant), arousal (calm–agitated), and dominance (submissive–dominant) — the VAD/PAD framework of Mehrabian and Russell — and its unit (the coordinate).
3. Compare the models: the discrete model's strength (the interpretability, the cultural label) and its failure (the blended state — excited but anxious — that the label cannot hold); the dimensional model's strength (the continuity, the adjacency) and its cost (the abstraction, the loss of the label's story).
4. Establish the working choice: valence and arousal as the operative dimensions for transmission — valence for the direction of the reaction, arousal for its energy — and dominance as the context variable, not the transmission driver.
5. State the boundary: where the discrete label still carries the meaning (the cultural reference, the brand's voice) and where the coordinate carries it (the comparison, the prediction, the design) — the hybrid practice per the task.

## Phase 3 — Method and Technique

- The model comparison: the discrete versus the dimensional, per the criterion (interpretability, continuity, prediction)
- The dimension definition: valence, arousal, dominance — per the scale, the anchor, the measurement
- The blended-state read: the coordinate versus the label, per the case
- The working-choice statement: valence and arousal per the transmission question
- The boundary statement: the label per the culture, the coordinate per the design

## Phase 4 — Analysis and Diagnosis

- Diagnose the modeling failure per the case: the label forced on the blended state (the misclassification), the coordinate without the label (the meaning lost) — the element per the failure, and the correction.
- Check the dimension's use: valence as the direction, arousal as the energy, dominance as the context — per the element, with the role per the dimension.
- Read the hybrid state: the label plus the coordinate, per the case — the practice per the task.

## Phase 5 — Application and Design

- Model the emotion per the task: the discrete label where the culture carries it, the dimensional coordinate where the comparison carries it — with the statement per the case.
- Fix the working dimensions: valence and arousal per the transmission question, dominance per the context — with the justification per the element.
- Hold the boundary per the practice: the label per the brand's voice, the coordinate per the design — with the record per the case.

## Phase 6 — Ethics and Boundary Conditions

- The emotion label (the cultural reference) is the person's meaning per the element; its use (the brand's voice per the label) is per the dignity of the meaning.
- The dimensional coordinate (the valence, the arousal) is the analyst's construction per the data; its use (the design per the coordinate, the prediction per the model) is per the honesty of the construction.
- The hybrid practice (the label plus the coordinate) is the discipline per the task; its honesty (the label per the culture, the coordinate per the data) is the standing per the record.

## Phase 7 — Exercises and Assessment

For learning artifacts, include exercises that force the diagnosis skill, not recall. Use the standard set:

1. Compare the models on five content cases: the discrete label, the dimensional coordinate, the fit per the case — with the choice per the task.
2. Define the dimensions for a measurement plan: valence, arousal, dominance — per the scale, the anchor, the method.
3. Read three blended states: the coordinate versus the label, per the case — with the hybrid read.
4. Write the working-choice statement: why valence and arousal carry the transmission question — with the justification per the element.
5. Write the boundary statement: where the label carries, where the coordinate carries — per the task, with the record.
6. Build the emotion-modeling primer: the comparison, the dimensions, the working choice, the boundary — as the standing reference, with the owner.

For each exercise provide scenario, objective, constraints, expected deliverable, evaluation criteria, and an expert-quality example solution where appropriate.

## Anti-Patterns

the label forced on the blended state; the coordinate without the meaning; dominance used as the transmission driver; the model chosen for the habit, not the task.

## Decision Heuristic

Before any claim or recommendation, ask:

1. Is this a label or a coordinate, and does the task need one or the other?
2. What is the valence, and what is the arousal, per this case?
3. Where does dominance sit — as the context, not the driver?
4. Does the blended state survive the label, or does it need the coordinate?
5. Is the working choice stated, with the justification per the element?

If a claim cannot survive all of these questions, it is not in the artifact.

## Final Principle

The goal is not the emotion label. It is the emotion modeled per the task — the label where the culture carries it, the coordinate where the comparison carries it, valence and arousal as the working dimensions — so the feeling is described on its terms, and the terms say where they are sure.
