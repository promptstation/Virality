# Step 2 — Generated Prompt (Result of the Reusable Prompt)

```
Develop a comprehensive, professional-level learning module on **“Foundations of Emotion: Discrete vs. Dimensional Models”** as part of an advanced course on the science of how emotion — its valence and its arousal — drives whether content is shared. The module should the two ways emotion is modeled — discrete categories and dimensional values — and why valence and arousal are the working dimensions for transmission.

The material should teach the models: the discrete categories, the VAD dimensions, and the trade-off between them. Go beyond the emotion as a single label, and the dimensional view as a luxury rather than a tool and examine how blended and ambiguous feelings defeat the discrete model, and what the dimensional view gains in exchange.

Define the discrete model: the basic-emotion categories (happiness, sadness, anger, fear, disgust, surprise — Ekman's set), each with its own cause and expression, and its unit (the category label).

Define the dimensional model: the continuous values along valence (pleasant–unpleasant), arousal (calm–agitated), and dominance (submissive–dominant) — the VAD/PAD framework of Mehrabian and Russell — and its unit (the coordinate).

Compare the models: the discrete model's strength (the interpretability, the cultural label) and its failure (the blended state — excited but anxious — that the label cannot hold); the dimensional model's strength (the continuity, the adjacency) and its cost (the abstraction, the loss of the label's story).

Establish the working choice: valence and arousal as the operative dimensions for transmission — valence for the direction of the reaction, arousal for its energy — and dominance as the context variable, not the transmission driver.

State the boundary: where the discrete label still carries the meaning (the cultural reference, the brand's voice) and where the coordinate carries it (the comparison, the prediction, the design) — the hybrid practice per the task.

Include practical methods for working at a professional level:
* The model comparison: the discrete versus the dimensional, per the criterion (interpretability, continuity, prediction)
* The dimension definition: valence, arousal, dominance — per the scale, the anchor, the measurement
* The blended-state read: the coordinate versus the label, per the case
* The working-choice statement: valence and arousal per the transmission question
* The boundary statement: the label per the culture, the coordinate per the design

Include practical exercises that require the learner to:
1. Compare the models on five content cases: the discrete label, the dimensional coordinate, the fit per the case — with the choice per the task.
2. Define the dimensions for a measurement plan: valence, arousal, dominance — per the scale, the anchor, the method.
3. Read three blended states: the coordinate versus the label, per the case — with the hybrid read.
4. Write the working-choice statement: why valence and arousal carry the transmission question — with the justification per the element.
5. Write the boundary statement: where the label carries, where the coordinate carries — per the task, with the record.
6. Build the emotion-modeling primer: the comparison, the dimensions, the working choice, the boundary — as the standing reference, with the owner.

For each exercise, provide the scenario, objective, constraints, expected deliverable, evaluation criteria, and an expert-quality example solution where appropriate.

Structure the material progressively, beginning with the two models and their units, moving through the dimensions and the trade-off, then to the working choice, stated per the case. Establish clear conceptual distinctions between **discrete label, dimensional coordinate, valence, and arousal** before showing how they interact.

Use professional terminology from emotion science, social psychology, and communication research where relevant, but explain specialized terminology in clear language. Do not make the material sound like generic AI-generated advice; the register should be that of an advanced professional training module used by content strategists, brand communicators, and researchers of emotional communication.

Research the subject using high-quality professional and academic sources where external research materially strengthens the material. Prioritize authoritative sources: the emotion-science literature (Ekman's basic-emotion work, Russell's circumplex, the Mehrabian–Russell VAD framework), the viral-content literature (Berger & Milkman 2012, Berger 2011, the Twitter replications), and the affect-measurement literature (LIWC, the NRC Emotion Lexicon, FACS, PANAS). Avoid relying primarily on low-quality SEO articles, generic emotional valence arousal blogs, or unsupported “best practice” claims. Where useful, distinguish established research findings from professional conventions and informed recommendations.

Pay particular attention to the relationship between this module and principles such as **the model per the task, the dimension per the role, and the hybrid per the practice**. Connect these concepts to concrete analytical and design decisions rather than discussing them only theoretically.

Create a professional framework that content strategists, brand communicators, and researchers of emotional communication could actually use in a real project. The final material should therefore function simultaneously as a learning resource, a practical reference, and a working methodology for designing and analyzing content for emotional transmission.

Throughout the module, prioritize language that is:

* Precise without being jargon-heavy
* Empirical without being statistical theater
* Confident about what the evidence establishes
* Explicit about what the evidence does not establish
* Conditional where the findings are conditional
* Practical where the decisions are practical

Where a claim depends on context (platform, audience, topic, measurement), explicitly explain the trade-off and the boundary condition rather than presenting it as an absolute rule.

End the module with **Models checklist**, **Dimensions checklist**, **Working choice checklist**, and a **Delivery gate checklist** that a professional team can use before acting on designing and analyzing content for emotional transmission.

The final result should be comprehensive enough to serve as an advanced professional training module, but organized clearly enough that a learner can study it progressively and apply each concept in designing and analyzing content for emotional transmission.
```
