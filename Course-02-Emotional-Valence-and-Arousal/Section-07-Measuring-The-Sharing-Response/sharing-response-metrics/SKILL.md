# Measuring the Sharing Response: Professional Module Builder

## Mission

Produce professional-level material on the outcome measurement — the metrics, the denominators, the confounders, the design — and the pipeline to the defensible estimate. The unit of work is a defensible artifact — a learning module, an evidence brief, a case diagnosis, or a strategy memo — in which every claim is tied to a source, a mechanism, and a boundary condition.

Optimize simultaneously for:

- empirical precision (what the canonical studies and works actually found)
- conceptual clarity (the core terms kept strictly distinct)
- causal discipline (correlational and causal claims never blurred)
- practical utility (a practitioner can act on the output the same day)
- intellectual honesty (limits and boundary conditions as visible as the findings)

Do not produce generic advice or pop-science hype. The register is that of an advanced professional training program: confident where the evidence is strong, explicitly conditional where it is not.

## Use Cases

### New professional learning module
When asked to create a full module, course unit, or workshop on this topic:
1. Establish scope: audience, depth, and which claims the module must cover.
2. Build the evidence base from primary sources and canonical works.
3. Develop the core mechanisms and diagnostic logic.
4. Add method, critique, and application sections.
5. End with exercises and the delivery checklists.
6. Deliver the complete artifact with no placeholders.

### Brief or leadership memo
When asked for a short document that must decide something:
1. State the question the document must answer in one sentence.
2. Include only findings strong enough to bear that decision, each with its mechanism and source.
3. Convert results into plain-language implications.
4. End with the specific decisions the evidence changes.
5. Cap length; cut context before cutting caveats.

### Case diagnosis
When asked to explain why a specific piece of content, campaign, or product spread (or did not):
1. Identify the operative mechanisms — never a single assumed cause.
2. Map the case onto the diagnostic framework of this skill.
3. Run the differential: which mechanisms were active, which alternatives were considered and set aside.
4. State confidence and the evidence that would revise the diagnosis.

### Strategy design
When asked to design a campaign, content system, or program around this lever:
1. Fix the objective, audience, and platform context.
2. Select the mechanisms from this skill that fit that context.
3. Specify implementation, sequencing, and the measurement plan.
4. State the boundary conditions under which the plan breaks.

## Core Output Requirements

Regardless of format:

- Every named finding or claim carries its source or mechanism in the same breath.
- Correlational and causal claims are labeled as such wherever they matter.
- Effect claims carry their metric and a plain-language rendering.
- Boundary conditions are stated as explicit “applies when / breaks down when” conditions.
- Examples use real, named studies, campaigns, or artifacts — not invented case studies.
- No padding sections, generic intro/outro filler, or advice that would read the same for any topic.

## Phase 1 — Scope and Claim Audit

### Artifact and audience
What the user actually needs (module vs. memo vs. diagnosis) and who reads it. Different readers make different cuts of the same material.

### Claim inventory
List the claims the artifact will make. For each, note the source or mechanism that supports it and whether that support is correlational, experimental, or canonical. Downgrade or cut unsupported claims — not the caveat.

### Depth budget
Match length to the decision the artifact serves. The fix for a weak document is choosing stronger claims to carry it, not adding hedges to weaker ones.

## Phase 2 — Foundations and Core Content

1. Teach the metrics: the raw share, the share per impression, the retweet probability, the email-to-read ratio, the sharing velocity — the metric per the form, with the meaning per the metric.
2. Teach the denominators and the selection: the raw count's failure (the placement, the article age, the audience size, the platform algorithm) and the correction (the rate per the denominator, the comparison per the control) — the denominator per the correction, with the element per the failure.
3. Teach the statistics: the rate ratio, the effect size per the literature, the confounder control (the surprise, the interestingness, the practical usefulness, the prominence) — the statistic per the claim, with the control per the confounder.
4. Teach the designs: the correlational per the observation, the causal per the manipulation, the before–after per the time, the split per the audience — the design per the claim, with the sufficiency per the design.
5. Build the pipeline: the content dataset per the input, the metric per the form, the denominator per the correction, the confounder per the control, the estimate per the design — the pipeline per the library, with the defensible estimate per the case.

## Phase 4 — Analysis and Diagnosis

- Diagnose the metric's failure: the raw count read as the success (the denominator missing), the rate without the comparison (the control missing) — the element per the failure, and the correction.
- Check the confounder: the surprise, the interestingness, the usefulness, the prominence — per the confounder, with the control per the element.
- Read the design per the claim: the correlational read as the causal (the claim past the design), the before–after without the time control (the drift unexamined) — the element per the design, and the boundary.

## Phase 6 — Ethics and Boundary Conditions

- The sharing metric (the count, the rate) is the audience's behavior per the data; its use (the claim per the metric, the strategy per the rate) is per the honesty of the denominator.
- The design (the correlational, the causal) is the analyst's construction per the claim; its use (the claim per the design, the decision per the estimate) is per the boundary of the design.
- The estimate (the defensible number) is the organization's record per the case; its use (the claim per the estimate, the report per the number) is per the honesty of the estimate.

## Phase 7 — Exercises and Assessment

For learning artifacts, include exercises that force the diagnosis skill, not recall. Use the standard set:

1. Compute the five metrics on a content dataset: the raw share, the rate, the probability, the ratio, the velocity — with the denominator per the correction.
2. Diagnose three raw-count cases: the placement, the age, the audience, the algorithm — with the correction per the element.
3. Control the confounders on a dataset: the surprise, the interestingness, the usefulness, the prominence — with the control per the confounder.
4. Choose the design for three claims: the correlational, the causal, the before–after, the split — with the sufficiency per the claim.
5. Build the minimal analytics pipeline: the input, the metric, the denominator, the confounder, the estimate — with the defensible estimate per the case.
6. Write the measurement report: the metric, the denominator, the confounder, the design, the estimate — as the standing document, with the owner.

For each exercise provide scenario, objective, constraints, expected deliverable, evaluation criteria, and an expert-quality example solution where appropriate.

## Phase 8 — Quality Gate and Delivery

Before delivering, run the delivery checklists. The artifact fails the gate on any “no.”

### Metrics
- The raw is per the form
- The rate is per the denominator
- The velocity is per the time

### Correction
- The placement is named
- The age is named
- The audience is named

### Control
- The surprise is controlled
- The interestingness is controlled
- The usefulness is controlled

### Delivery gate
- The design is per the claim
- The pipeline is per the library
- The estimate is defensible

## Anti-Patterns

the raw count as the success; the rate without the comparison; the confounder uncontrolled; the correlational read as the causal; the pipeline without the denominator; the estimate without the design.

## Decision Heuristic

Before any claim or recommendation, ask:

1. What is the denominator, and does the rate correct the count?
2. What is the confounder, and is it controlled per the claim?
3. What is the design, and does it support the claim?
4. Does the pipeline produce the defensible estimate, per the case?

If a claim cannot survive all of these questions, it is not in the artifact.

## Final Principle

The goal is not the share count. It is the response measured per the element — the rate per the denominator, the control per the confounder, the design per the claim — so the sharing is read on its metrics, and the metrics say where they are sure.
