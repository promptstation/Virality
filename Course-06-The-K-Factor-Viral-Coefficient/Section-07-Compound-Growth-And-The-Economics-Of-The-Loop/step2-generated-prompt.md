Develop a comprehensive, professional-level learning module on **“Compound Growth and the Economics of the Loop”** as part of an advanced course on the mathematics of product-led viral growth — the referral loop, its coefficient, and the economics of compounding. The module should how the loop's growth compounds in the full business recursion, and how its economics — the cost per referred, the viral CAC, the payback — decide the investment.

The material should teach the economics layer: the compound recursion, the saturation, the unit economics, and the invest/stop decision from the numbers. Go beyond the K-factor as a marketing metric, disconnected from the cost and the ceiling and examine the loop's contribution as a multiple on the paid input, its cost structure, and the saturation that the epidemic mathematics describes.

Build the full recursion: the user base at the next period from the current base (minus the churn), plus the paid input, plus the loop's addition (the k on the active referrers) — the growth equation with all three terms, and the decomposition that attributes the growth to its sources.

Teach the time-to-scale in the realistic world: the k<1 case with the paid multiplier (the loop as the multiplier on the paid, the multiple = 1/(1−k)), the time to the target size from the parameters, and the sensitivity to the k's drift (the decay of the share habit).

Cover the saturation: the market's ceiling (the addressable pool), the loop's exhaustion (the susceptible pool depletes as the network fills — the epidemic connection, the slowing of the k as the reachable-invitee pool shrinks), and the read: the loop's contribution has a horizon, and the strategy's question is what carries the growth after it.

Compute the unit economics: the cost per referred (the incentive cost, the landing and the onboarding cost, the loop's infrastructure amortized), the referred's LTV (the retention-qualified, the cohort's curve), the viral CAC (the paid CAC divided by the loop's multiplier, the formula, and the honesty rule — the viral CAC is a planning number, not an accounting one, and its use as a real cost is the category error), and the payback (the referred's contribution against the cost per referred, per cohort).

Teach the invest/stop decision: the invest case (the k headroom to the target, the quality held, the market's size unexhausted, the cost per referred below the LTV) and the stop case (the knee of the marginal decay reached, the quality gap opening, the saturation near, the incentive cost exceeding the contribution) — the decision from the standing series, with the re-entry condition stated.

Produce the growth story's decomposition: the new users by source (the paid, the loop, the organic), the loop's multiple on the paid, the loop's cost, the loop's contribution to the net growth — the one page the board can audit, and the quarterly cadence that keeps it true.

* The recursion model: the three terms, the parameters, the scenario run (the base, the k-drift, the saturation)

* The decomposition page: the sources, the multiple, the cost, the contribution — the quarterly deliverable

6. Design the succession plan: the saturation horizon, the question of what carries the growth after, and the investment case for the answer — as the loop's endgame document.

For each exercise, provide the scenario, objective, constraints, expected deliverable, evaluation criteria, and an expert-quality example solution where appropriate.

Structure the material progressively, beginning with the recursion and the time-to-scale, moving through the unit economics and the viral CAC's honesty, then to the invest/stop decision and the succession plan. Establish clear conceptual distinctions between **paid input, loop addition, churn, viral CAC, and the contribution multiple** before showing how they interact.

Use professional terminology from product growth, referral marketing, and unit economics where relevant, but explain specialized terminology in clear language.
