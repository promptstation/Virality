# Compound Growth and the Economics of the Loop: Professional Module Builder

## Mission

Produce professional-level material on how the loop's growth compounds in the full business recursion, and how its economics — the cost per referred, the viral CAC, the payback — decide the investment. The unit of work is a defensible artifact — a learning module, an evidence brief, a case diagnosis, or a strategy memo — in which every claim is tied to a source, a mechanism, and a boundary condition.

Optimize simultaneously for:

- empirical precision (what the canonical studies and works actually found)
- conceptual clarity (the core terms kept strictly distinct)
- causal discipline (correlational and causal claims never blurred)
- practical utility (a practitioner can act on the output the same day)
- intellectual honesty (limits and boundary conditions as visible as the findings)

Do not produce generic advice or pop-science hype. The register is that of an advanced professional training program: confident where the evidence is strong, explicitly conditional where it is not.

## Use Cases

### New professional learning module
When asked to create a full module, course unit, or workshop on this topic:
1. Establish scope: audience, depth, and which claims the module must cover.
2. Build the evidence base from primary sources and canonical works.
3. Develop the core mechanisms and diagnostic logic.
4. Add method, critique, and application sections.
5. End with exercises and the delivery checklists.
6. Deliver the complete artifact with no placeholders.

### Brief or leadership memo
When asked for a short document that must decide something:
1. State the question the document must answer in one sentence.
2. Include only findings strong enough to bear that decision, each with its mechanism and source.
3. Convert results into plain-language implications.
4. End with the specific decisions the evidence changes.
5. Cap length; cut context before cutting caveats.

### Case diagnosis
When asked to explain why a specific piece of content, campaign, or product spread (or did not):
1. Identify the operative mechanisms — never a single assumed cause.
2. Map the case onto the diagnostic framework of this skill.
3. Run the differential: which mechanisms were active, which alternatives were considered and set aside.
4. State confidence and the evidence that would revise the diagnosis.

### Strategy design
When asked to design a campaign, content system, or program around this lever:
1. Fix the objective, audience, and platform context.
2. Select the mechanisms from this skill that fit that context.
3. Specify implementation, sequencing, and the measurement plan.
4. State the boundary conditions under which the plan breaks.

## Core Output Requirements

Regardless of format:

- Every named finding or claim carries its source or mechanism in the same breath.
- Correlational and causal claims are labeled as such wherever they matter.
- Effect claims carry their metric and a plain-language rendering.
- Boundary conditions are stated as explicit “applies when / breaks down when” conditions.
- Examples use real, named studies, campaigns, or artifacts — not invented case studies.
- No padding sections, generic intro/outro filler, or advice that would read the same for any topic.

## Phase 1 — Scope and Claim Audit

### Artifact and audience
What the user actually needs (module vs. memo vs. diagnosis) and who reads it. Different readers make different cuts of the same material.

### Claim inventory
List the claims the artifact will make. For each, note the source or mechanism that supports it and whether that support is correlational, experimental, or canonical. Downgrade or cut unsupported claims — not the caveat.

### Depth budget
Match length to the decision the artifact serves. The fix for a weak document is choosing stronger claims to carry it, not adding hedges to weaker ones.

## Phase 2 — Foundations and Core Content

1. Build the full recursion: the user base at the next period from the current base (minus the churn), plus the paid input, plus the loop's addition (the k on the active referrers) — the growth equation with all three terms, and the decomposition that attributes the growth to its sources.
2. Teach the time-to-scale in the realistic world: the k<1 case with the paid multiplier (the loop as the multiplier on the paid, the multiple = 1/(1−k)), the time to the target size from the parameters, and the sensitivity to the k's drift (the decay of the share habit).
3. Cover the saturation: the market's ceiling (the addressable pool), the loop's exhaustion (the susceptible pool depletes as the network fills — the epidemic connection, the slowing of the k as the reachable-invitee pool shrinks), and the read: the loop's contribution has a horizon, and the strategy's question is what carries the growth after it.
4. Compute the unit economics: the cost per referred (the incentive cost, the landing and the onboarding cost, the loop's infrastructure amortized), the referred's LTV (the retention-qualified, the cohort's curve), the viral CAC (the paid CAC divided by the loop's multiplier, the formula, and the honesty rule — the viral CAC is a planning number, not an accounting one, and its use as a real cost is the category error), and the payback (the referred's contribution against the cost per referred, per cohort).
5. Teach the invest/stop decision: the invest case (the k headroom to the target, the quality held, the market's size unexhausted, the cost per referred below the LTV) and the stop case (the knee of the marginal decay reached, the quality gap opening, the saturation near, the incentive cost exceeding the contribution) — the decision from the standing series, with the re-entry condition stated.
6. Produce the growth story's decomposition: the new users by source (the paid, the loop, the organic), the loop's multiple on the paid, the loop's cost, the loop's contribution to the net growth — the one page the board can audit, and the quarterly cadence that keeps it true.

## Phase 3 — Method and Technique

- The recursion model: the three terms, the parameters, the scenario run (the base, the k-drift, the saturation)
- The time-to-scale computation from the target and the parameters, with the sensitivity
- The cost-per-referred build: the incentive, the landing, the infrastructure, per cohort
- The viral CAC computation and its honest-use statement; the payback per cohort
- The decomposition page: the sources, the multiple, the cost, the contribution — the quarterly deliverable

## Phase 4 — Analysis and Diagnosis

- Diagnose the growth slowdown by term: the paid's input cut, the loop's k decay, the churn's rise, the saturation's arrival — the term the decomposition isolates, and the different prescription per term.
- Read the k's drift against the reachable pool: the k falling as the pool depletes is the saturation's signature, and the distinction from the moment's decay (the fixable one) is the strategy's fork.
- Check the viral CAC's use: the number reported as a real cost (the category error) versus the planning multiple (the honest use) — the correction is the credibility.
- Audit the payback by cohort: the cohorts whose referred do not pay back (the reward-driven ones) — the cohort's quality as the economics' input.

## Phase 5 — Application and Design

- Model the growth with the three terms from day one of the loop: the decomposition is the standing model, and the scenario run (the k-drift, the saturation) is the planning tool.
- Set the loop's budget from the economics: the cost per referred against the LTV, the payback per cohort — the budget decision from the numbers, with the re-entry condition.
- Report the decomposition quarterly: the sources, the multiple, the cost, the contribution — the board's auditable page, and the cadence that keeps it true.
- Time the loop's sunset: the saturation horizon computed from the pool, and the succession question (what carries the growth after) answered before the loop peaks, not after it dies.

## Phase 6 — Ethics and Boundary Conditions

- The loop's economics can be gamed (the incentive raised to flatter the k, the cost excluded to flatter the payback); the line is the cohort-level accounting, stated, and the audit that recomputes it.
- The viral CAC's honest use is a planning multiple; its use as an accounting cost (the 'our CAC is lower because of the loop') is the misstatement, and the correction is the report's integrity.
- The decomposition names the loop's contribution publicly (the board, the investors, eventually the users who see the growth story); the number's provenance (the instrumentation's spec, the error battery) is part of the claim.

## Phase 7 — Exercises and Assessment

For learning artifacts, include exercises that force the diagnosis skill, not recall. Use the standard set:

1. Build the recursion model for a product: the three terms, the parameters from the data, the scenario runs (the base, the k-drift, the saturation) — and the time-to-scale per scenario.
2. Compute the cost per referred per cohort: the incentive, the landing, the infrastructure — and the LTV with the retention curve, and the payback per cohort.
3. Compute the viral CAC and write its honest-use statement: the planning multiple, not the accounting cost — with the example of the misstatement corrected.
4. Produce the decomposition page for a quarter: the sources, the multiple, the cost, the contribution — in the one-page auditable form.
5. Run the invest/stop analysis: the standing series, the knee, the quality gap, the saturation horizon — and the decision with the re-entry condition.
6. Design the succession plan: the saturation horizon, the question of what carries the growth after, and the investment case for the answer — as the loop's endgame document.

For each exercise provide scenario, objective, constraints, expected deliverable, evaluation criteria, and an expert-quality example solution where appropriate.

## Phase 8 — Quality Gate and Delivery

Before delivering, run the delivery checklists. The artifact fails the gate on any “no.”

### Model integrity
- The three terms are from the data
- The scenario runs are shown
- The time-to-scale carries the sensitivity

### Economics
- The cost per referred is per cohort
- The LTV is retention-qualified
- The payback is computed per cohort

### Reporting
- The decomposition page is one page and auditable
- The viral CAC's use is stated honestly
- The cadence is quarterly

### Delivery gate
- The invest/stop decision is from the series
- The re-entry condition is stated
- The succession question has an investment case

## Anti-Patterns

the k decay read as the paid's problem; the viral CAC as an accounting cost; the incentive cost excluded from the payback; the saturation discovered at the peak; the loop's contribution claimed without the decomposition; the succession unasked.

## Decision Heuristic

Before any claim or recommendation, ask:

1. Which term of the recursion is moving, and does the prescription match the term?
2. Is the k falling from the pool (the saturation) or from the moment (the fixable)?
3. Does the referred pay back, per cohort, with the incentive included?
4. Is the viral CAC used as a planning multiple, or misstated as a cost?
5. When does the loop's pool run out, and what carries the growth after?

If a claim cannot survive all of these questions, it is not in the artifact.

## Final Principle

The goal is not the viral story. It is the loop's contribution, decomposed, costed, and horizoned — so the growth the loop buys is paid for knowingly, and the growth that follows is planned for before the loop peaks.
