# Step 2 — Generated Prompt (Result of the Reusable Prompt)

```
Develop a comprehensive, professional-level learning module on **“Designing the Viral Loop: The Moment, the Invitation, the Landing”** as part of an advanced course on the mathematics of product-led viral growth — the referral loop, its coefficient, and the economics of compounding. The module should how the loop is designed — the moment, the invitation, the landing — with the friction audit that prices every step and the quality gates that keep it honest.

The material should teach the design stack: the moment where the loop fires, the invitation that carries the reason, the landing that delivers the value, and the friction that prices it all. Go beyond the share button as the loop — the moment, the payload, and the landing as the actual design and examine the product's natural moments as the raw material, and the invitee's experience as the product of the design.

Teach the moment design: the value moment (the user just gained — the file synced, the document created), the winning moment (the user just achieved — the goal hit, the insight found), and the handoff moment (the product's use naturally addresses another person — the shared document, the team invite) — the three moment types, and the evidence for which one converts in the category.

Teach the invitation design: the payload (the reason to open — the value preview, the personalization, the relationship line), the channel (the in-app, the email, the share sheet, the direct link), and the tone (the user's voice, not the brand's) — the invitation as the referrer's message, with the referrer's standing on the line.

Teach the landing design: the invitee's first screen (the value before the signup — the preview, the partial access, the social proof of who sent it), the path to the first value (the minimum steps, the moment the invitee gets what the invitation promised), and the exit (the graceful non-signup, the saved state, the re-invite path) — the landing as the promise kept.

Cover the loop architecture: the single loop (one moment, one path — the clarity case) and the multi-loop product (the loops per feature, the shared landing, the combined k as the sum of the loop contributions) — and the funnel placement (the loop at onboarding, at activation, at expansion).

Run the friction audit: every step from the value moment to the new user's first value, with its conversion rate and its cost — the funnel of the loop itself, and the step with the largest drop as the design target.

Include practical methods for working at a professional level:
* The moment inventory: the product's moments scored by value, win, and handoff type, with the conversion data where present
* The invitation spec: the payload template, the personalization fields, the channel matrix
* The landing spec: the first screen, the value-before-signup design, the path to the first value, the exit
* The friction audit: the loop's own funnel, the step conversions, the cost per step
* The architecture decision: the single vs. the multi-loop, the funnel placement, the combined k model

Include practical exercises that require the learner to:
1. Inventory a product's moments: the value, win, and handoff types, with the conversion data and the recommendation for the loop's moment.
2. Write the invitation spec for the chosen moment: the payload template, the personalization, the channel matrix, and the open-rate test per referrer tier.
3. Design the landing: the first screen, the value-before-signup, the path to the first value under the step budget, and the exit.
4. Run the friction audit on a live loop: the loop's funnel, the step conversions, the largest drop, and the design target it implies.
5. Decide the architecture for a multi-feature product: the loops per feature, the shared landing, the combined k model, and the frequency cap.
6. Write the consent and fidelity spec: the send consent, the revoke, the display consent, and the free-tier boundary — as the loop's standing policy.

For each exercise, provide the scenario, objective, constraints, expected deliverable, evaluation criteria, and an expert-quality example solution where appropriate.

Structure the material progressively, beginning with the moments and the element stack, moving through the invitation, the landing, and the friction, then to the architecture decision and the standing audit. Establish clear conceptual distinctions between **moment type, invitation payload, landing promise, and friction cost** before showing how they interact.

Use professional terminology from product growth, referral marketing, and unit economics where relevant, but explain specialized terminology in clear language. Do not make the material sound like generic AI-generated advice; the register should be that of an advanced professional training module used by product growth managers, referral-program designers, and startup founders.

Research the subject using high-quality professional and academic sources where external research materially strengthens the material. Prioritize authoritative sources: the product-led-growth literature and case studies (Dropbox, Hotmail, PayPal, Airbnb, TikTok-style loops), the viral-coefficient and referral-program practice literature, and the standard growth-analytics methods. Avoid relying primarily on low-quality SEO articles, generic k factor viral coefficient blogs, or unsupported “best practice” claims. Where useful, distinguish established research findings from professional conventions and informed recommendations.

Pay particular attention to the relationship between this module and principles such as **the moment as foundation, the promise kept, the priced friction, and the consent as design**. Connect these concepts to concrete analytical and design decisions rather than discussing them only theoretically.

Create a professional framework that product growth managers, referral-program designers, and startup founders could actually use in a real project. The final material should therefore function simultaneously as a learning resource, a practical reference, and a working methodology for designing and operating referral loops and product-led growth.

Throughout the module, prioritize language that is:

* Precise without being jargon-heavy
* Empirical without being statistical theater
* Confident about what the evidence establishes
* Explicit about what the evidence does not establish
* Conditional where the findings are conditional
* Practical where the decisions are practical

Where a claim depends on context (platform, audience, topic, measurement), explicitly explain the trade-off and the boundary condition rather than presenting it as an absolute rule.

End the module with **Moment choice checklist**, **Invitation and landing checklist**, **Friction checklist**, and a **Delivery gate checklist** that a professional team can use before acting on designing and operating referral loops and product-led growth.

The final result should be comprehensive enough to serve as an advanced professional training module, but organized clearly enough that a learner can study it progressively and apply each concept in designing and operating referral loops and product-led growth.
```
