# Optimizing i: Invite Volume, Shareable Surfaces, and the Spam Line: Professional Module Builder

## Mission

Produce professional-level material on how i is raised — the surfaces, the features, the frequency architecture — and the spam line that bounds the volume. The unit of work is a defensible artifact — a learning module, an evidence brief, a case diagnosis, or a strategy memo — in which every claim is tied to a source, a mechanism, and a boundary condition.

Optimize simultaneously for:

- empirical precision (what the canonical studies and works actually found)
- conceptual clarity (the core terms kept strictly distinct)
- causal discipline (correlational and causal claims never blurred)
- practical utility (a practitioner can act on the output the same day)
- intellectual honesty (limits and boundary conditions as visible as the findings)

Do not produce generic advice or pop-science hype. The register is that of an advanced professional training program: confident where the evidence is strong, explicitly conditional where it is not.

## Use Cases

### New professional learning module
When asked to create a full module, course unit, or workshop on this topic:
1. Establish scope: audience, depth, and which claims the module must cover.
2. Build the evidence base from primary sources and canonical works.
3. Develop the core mechanisms and diagnostic logic.
4. Add method, critique, and application sections.
5. End with exercises and the delivery checklists.
6. Deliver the complete artifact with no placeholders.

### Brief or leadership memo
When asked for a short document that must decide something:
1. State the question the document must answer in one sentence.
2. Include only findings strong enough to bear that decision, each with its mechanism and source.
3. Convert results into plain-language implications.
4. End with the specific decisions the evidence changes.
5. Cap length; cut context before cutting caveats.

### Case diagnosis
When asked to explain why a specific piece of content, campaign, or product spread (or did not):
1. Identify the operative mechanisms — never a single assumed cause.
2. Map the case onto the diagnostic framework of this skill.
3. Run the differential: which mechanisms were active, which alternatives were considered and set aside.
4. State confidence and the evidence that would revise the diagnosis.

### Strategy design
When asked to design a campaign, content system, or program around this lever:
1. Fix the objective, audience, and platform context.
2. Select the mechanisms from this skill that fit that context.
3. Specify implementation, sequencing, and the measurement plan.
4. State the boundary conditions under which the plan breaks.

## Core Output Requirements

Regardless of format:

- Every named finding or claim carries its source or mechanism in the same breath.
- Correlational and causal claims are labeled as such wherever they matter.
- Effect claims carry their metric and a plain-language rendering.
- Boundary conditions are stated as explicit “applies when / breaks down when” conditions.
- Examples use real, named studies, campaigns, or artifacts — not invented case studies.
- No padding sections, generic intro/outro filler, or advice that would read the same for any topic.

## Phase 1 — Scope and Claim Audit

### Artifact and audience
What the user actually needs (module vs. memo vs. diagnosis) and who reads it. Different readers make different cuts of the same material.

### Claim inventory
List the claims the artifact will make. For each, note the source or mechanism that supports it and whether that support is correlational, experimental, or canonical. Downgrade or cut unsupported claims — not the caveat.

### Depth budget
Match length to the decision the artifact serves. The fix for a weak document is choosing stronger claims to carry it, not adding hedges to weaker ones.

## Phase 2 — Foundations and Core Content

1. Teach the shareable surfaces: the product's outputs (the created artifact shared as the proof), the achievements (the result shared as the win), the collaborations (the shared workspace that requires the other) — the surfaces where the send is the product's behavior, not a campaign's ask, and the design of each (the default payload, the friction of the send, the channel).
2. Cover the structural features: the multi-user features (the product that needs others to be fully useful — the document, the team, the game), the gifting (the value that travels as the invitation), the challenges (the competitive loop that the scoreboard completes) — the features that raise i by changing the product's use, not by asking for the send.
3. Teach the frequency architecture: the send budget per user (the cap, the cooldown), the per-surface frequency (the collaboration invite is a different animal from the achievement share), and the state logic (the send offered when the value is fresh, not on a schedule) — the architecture that keeps the volume inside the goodwill.
4. Cover the volume-quality trade-off with the evidence: the marginal invite's conversion decays with the sender's cumulative sends (the first invite converts, the fifth is noise), the sender tail (the small fraction of high-volume senders who drive most of i and a disproportionate share of the complaints), and the read: the i optimization has a diminishing-return curve, and the knee is where the strategy stops pushing volume.
5. Teach the spam line's monitors: the opt-out rate (the invitee's rejection of the sender's world), the unfriend/unfollow rate (the relationship damage), the report rate, and the platform's rate limits (the external bound) — the four signals that define the line, and the action rule when one crosses.
6. Build the i-health measurement: the distribution of sends per user (the median, the tail), the send retention (the senders who keep sending, and the cohort that churns from sending), and the per-surface i decomposition — the standing series that prices the volume.

## Phase 3 — Method and Technique

- The surface audit: the product's surfaces, the send friction per surface, the default payload per surface
- The structural-feature inventory: the multi-user, the gifting, the challenge — the features that raise i by design
- The frequency spec: the caps, the cooldowns, the state logic, per surface
- The marginal-decay curve: the conversion per send-number, per referrer cohort
- The i-health dashboard: the distribution, the retention, the per-surface series, the four spam-line monitors

## Phase 4 — Analysis and Diagnosis

- Diagnose the i plateau by surface: which surface's send friction rose, which surface's value freshness decayed — the surface-level read before the global volume push.
- Read the marginal decay: the conversion by send-number — the knee of the curve is the volume's limit, and the push past the knee buys i at the cost of c and the goodwill.
- Audit the sender tail: the top senders' share of i, their invitees' conversion, their opt-out exposure — the tail is the loop's risk concentration.
- Check the spam-line monitors jointly: one signal crossed is a warning, two is the action (the cap tightened, the surface paused) — the rule is set in advance, not in the moment.

## Phase 5 — Application and Design

- Raise i from the surfaces and the features, in that order: the surface friction is the cheap lever, the structural feature is the durable one, and the global volume push is the last resort with the caps.
- Design the state logic: the send offered at the value moment, the payload defaulted to the value, the channel matched to the relationship — the send as the product's behavior.
- Set the frequency architecture before the launch: the caps per surface, the cooldown, the state trigger — the architecture is the spam line's first defense.
- Instrument the i-health dashboard at launch: the distribution, the decay curve, the monitors — the standing series that tells when to stop.

## Phase 6 — Ethics and Boundary Conditions

- The send under the user's name at volume is the relationship at risk; the line is the opt-out's ease (the one-tap no, the standing preference) and the user's control over the standing offer.
- The gifting and the challenge loops borrow the social graph for the product's growth; the line is the recipient's experience (the gift that is a burden, the challenge that is a pressure), measured by the rejection and the exit rates.
- The sender-tail data identifies the users whose relationships the loop is drawing down; the intervention (the soft cap, the message change) is the loop's debt management, and its absence is the loop's long-run cost.

## Phase 7 — Exercises and Assessment

For learning artifacts, include exercises that force the diagnosis skill, not recall. Use the standard set:

1. Audit a product's shareable surfaces: the friction, the payload, the channel per surface — with the redesign of the worst surface.
2. Inventory the structural-feature options (the multi-user, the gifting, the challenge) for a product: the feature spec, the i mechanism, and the build cost.
3. Design the frequency architecture: the caps, the cooldowns, the state logic, per surface — with the opt-out and the standing preference.
4. Compute the marginal-decay curve from the send data: the conversion by send-number, the knee, and the volume strategy it implies.
5. Audit the sender tail: the top senders' share, their invitees' outcomes, the opt-out exposure — and the intervention spec.
6. Write the spam-line policy: the four monitors, the thresholds, the action rules, and the user-facing controls — as the standing document.

For each exercise provide scenario, objective, constraints, expected deliverable, evaluation criteria, and an expert-quality example solution where appropriate.

## Phase 8 — Quality Gate and Delivery

Before delivering, run the delivery checklists. The artifact fails the gate on any “no.”

### Surface design
- The friction per surface is audited
- The payload defaults to the value
- The channel matches the relationship

### Frequency
- The caps and the cooldowns are set
- The state logic is the trigger
- The opt-out is one-tap

### Volume strategy
- The decay curve is computed
- The knee is named
- The tail's intervention is specified

### Delivery gate
- The spam-line policy is live
- The monitors are instrumented
- The i-health dashboard is standing

## Anti-Patterns

the global volume push before the surface fix; the send on a schedule, not a state; the marginal decay ignored (the volume past the knee); the sender tail unmanaged; the opt-out as a buried setting; the challenge loop that pressures the recipient.

## Decision Heuristic

Before any claim or recommendation, ask:

1. Is the send the product's behavior at the value moment, or a campaign's ask?
2. Where is the knee of the marginal decay, and is the volume below it?
3. What does the sender tail expose, and is the intervention set?
4. Which spam-line monitor is closest to its threshold?
5. Can the user stop the standing offer in one tap?

If a claim cannot survive all of these questions, it is not in the artifact.

## Final Principle

The goal is not more invites. It is the invites the product's value generates, at the volume the goodwill absorbs, with the decay measured and the tail managed — so the i is an asset the loop compounds, not a liability it pays off.
