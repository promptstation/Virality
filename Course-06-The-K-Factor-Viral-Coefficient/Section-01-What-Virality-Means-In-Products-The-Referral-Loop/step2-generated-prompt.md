# Step 2 — Generated Prompt (Result of the Reusable Prompt)

```
Develop a comprehensive, professional-level learning module on **“What Virality Means in Products: The Referral Loop”** as part of an advanced course on the mathematics of product-led viral growth — the referral loop, its coefficient, and the economics of compounding. The module should what product virality is — the referral loop inside the product's use — and how the classic cases designed it.

The material should teach the loop concept: the mechanism by which an existing user brings a new one, and the cases that made it work. Go beyond the word 'viral' as a description of a growth mood rather than a measurable loop and examine the classic cases as loop designs — the incentive, the moment, the landing — not as origin stories.

Define the referral loop: an existing user (the referrer) triggers an invitation to a non-user (the invitee), who converts to a user at some probability; the loop closes when the new user can themselves refer.

Distinguish product virality from content virality: the product loop's share is an invitation with a landing (the invitee arrives at a reason to join), while content's share is a transmission with a consumption; the two have different metrics and different failure modes.

Situate the loop in product-led growth: the product is the sales channel, the loop is the product's built-in acquisition, and the paid and the viral are two inputs to the same growth equation.

Read the classic cases as designs: Hotmail (the signature line — the invitation rides the product's own output), Dropbox (the storage incentive — the reward for both sides, the utility as the hook), PayPal (the two-sided payment incentive — the economic reason to refer), Airbnb (the listing-credit split — the two-sided market's natural loop).

State the honest base rate: most loops run at k < 1, and the strategy of the course is the strategy of the sub-1 world — where the loop is a multiplier on paid growth, not a substitute for it.

Include practical methods for working at a professional level:
* The loop inventory: the product's natural share moments, the existing referral paths, the incentive structure
* The case-decomposition template: the incentive, the moment, the landing, the metric each case optimized
* The loop-versus-paid attribution baseline: the share of new users by source, before any loop claim
* The product-led read: the funnel where the product carries the sales job
* The base-rate memo: the expected k for the category, and where the product stands

Include practical exercises that require the learner to:
1. Inventory a product's loop candidates: the natural moments, the existing paths, the incentive options — with the recommendation and its base-rate argument.
2. Decompose two classic cases (e.g., Hotmail and Dropbox) with the template: the incentive, the moment, the landing, the metric optimized — and the transferable element.
3. Attribute a product's new-user growth by source: the viral cohort, the paid cohort, the organic — and the loop's honest contribution.
4. Diagnose a loop-in-name: the share button without the invitation logic — the moment, the landing, and the fix that makes it a loop.
5. Design the two-sided incentive for a new product: the economic reason per side, the cost structure, and the unit-economics check.
6. Write the base-rate memo for a category: the expected k, the evidence, and the decision rule for the investment.

For each exercise, provide the scenario, objective, constraints, expected deliverable, evaluation criteria, and an expert-quality example solution where appropriate.

Structure the material progressively, beginning with the loop definition and the product/content distinction, moving through the cases as designs and the base rates, then to a loop design grounded in the product's moments and economics. Establish clear conceptual distinctions between **product virality, content virality, referral loop, and the referred cohort** before showing how they interact.

Use professional terminology from product growth, referral marketing, and unit economics where relevant, but explain specialized terminology in clear language. Do not make the material sound like generic AI-generated advice; the register should be that of an advanced professional training module used by product growth managers, referral-program designers, and startup founders.

Research the subject using high-quality professional and academic sources where external research materially strengthens the material. Prioritize authoritative sources: the product-led-growth literature and case studies (Dropbox, Hotmail, PayPal, Airbnb, TikTok-style loops), the viral-coefficient and referral-program practice literature, and the standard growth-analytics methods. Avoid relying primarily on low-quality SEO articles, generic k factor viral coefficient blogs, or unsupported “best practice” claims. Where useful, distinguish established research findings from professional conventions and informed recommendations.

Pay particular attention to the relationship between this module and principles such as **the moment inside the use, the incentive as transferred value, the two-sidedness, and the sub-1 reality**. Connect these concepts to concrete analytical and design decisions rather than discussing them only theoretically.

Create a professional framework that product growth managers, referral-program designers, and startup founders could actually use in a real project. The final material should therefore function simultaneously as a learning resource, a practical reference, and a working methodology for designing and operating referral loops and product-led growth.

Throughout the module, prioritize language that is:

* Precise without being jargon-heavy
* Empirical without being statistical theater
* Confident about what the evidence establishes
* Explicit about what the evidence does not establish
* Conditional where the findings are conditional
* Practical where the decisions are practical

Where a claim depends on context (platform, audience, topic, measurement), explicitly explain the trade-off and the boundary condition rather than presenting it as an absolute rule.

End the module with **Loop definition checklist**, **Attribution baseline checklist**, **Design integrity checklist**, and a **Delivery gate checklist** that a professional team can use before acting on designing and operating referral loops and product-led growth.

The final result should be comprehensive enough to serve as an advanced professional training module, but organized clearly enough that a learner can study it progressively and apply each concept in designing and operating referral loops and product-led growth.
```
