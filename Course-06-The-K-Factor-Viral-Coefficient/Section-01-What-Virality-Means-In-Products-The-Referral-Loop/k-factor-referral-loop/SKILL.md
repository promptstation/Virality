# What Virality Means in Products: The Referral Loop: Professional Module Builder

## Mission

Produce professional-level material on what product virality is — the referral loop inside the product's use — and how the classic cases designed it. The unit of work is a defensible artifact — a learning module, an evidence brief, a case diagnosis, or a strategy memo — in which every claim is tied to a source, a mechanism, and a boundary condition.

Optimize simultaneously for:

- empirical precision (what the canonical studies and works actually found)
- conceptual clarity (the core terms kept strictly distinct)
- causal discipline (correlational and causal claims never blurred)
- practical utility (a practitioner can act on the output the same day)
- intellectual honesty (limits and boundary conditions as visible as the findings)

Do not produce generic advice or pop-science hype. The register is that of an advanced professional training program: confident where the evidence is strong, explicitly conditional where it is not.

## Use Cases

### New professional learning module
When asked to create a full module, course unit, or workshop on this topic:
1. Establish scope: audience, depth, and which claims the module must cover.
2. Build the evidence base from primary sources and canonical works.
3. Develop the core mechanisms and diagnostic logic.
4. Add method, critique, and application sections.
5. End with exercises and the delivery checklists.
6. Deliver the complete artifact with no placeholders.

### Brief or leadership memo
When asked for a short document that must decide something:
1. State the question the document must answer in one sentence.
2. Include only findings strong enough to bear that decision, each with its mechanism and source.
3. Convert results into plain-language implications.
4. End with the specific decisions the evidence changes.
5. Cap length; cut context before cutting caveats.

### Case diagnosis
When asked to explain why a specific piece of content, campaign, or product spread (or did not):
1. Identify the operative mechanisms — never a single assumed cause.
2. Map the case onto the diagnostic framework of this skill.
3. Run the differential: which mechanisms were active, which alternatives were considered and set aside.
4. State confidence and the evidence that would revise the diagnosis.

### Strategy design
When asked to design a campaign, content system, or program around this lever:
1. Fix the objective, audience, and platform context.
2. Select the mechanisms from this skill that fit that context.
3. Specify implementation, sequencing, and the measurement plan.
4. State the boundary conditions under which the plan breaks.

## Core Output Requirements

Regardless of format:

- Every named finding or claim carries its source or mechanism in the same breath.
- Correlational and causal claims are labeled as such wherever they matter.
- Effect claims carry their metric and a plain-language rendering.
- Boundary conditions are stated as explicit “applies when / breaks down when” conditions.
- Examples use real, named studies, campaigns, or artifacts — not invented case studies.
- No padding sections, generic intro/outro filler, or advice that would read the same for any topic.

## Phase 1 — Scope and Claim Audit

### Artifact and audience
What the user actually needs (module vs. memo vs. diagnosis) and who reads it. Different readers make different cuts of the same material.

### Claim inventory
List the claims the artifact will make. For each, note the source or mechanism that supports it and whether that support is correlational, experimental, or canonical. Downgrade or cut unsupported claims — not the caveat.

### Depth budget
Match length to the decision the artifact serves. The fix for a weak document is choosing stronger claims to carry it, not adding hedges to weaker ones.

## Phase 2 — Foundations and Core Content

1. Define the referral loop: an existing user (the referrer) triggers an invitation to a non-user (the invitee), who converts to a user at some probability; the loop closes when the new user can themselves refer.
2. Distinguish product virality from content virality: the product loop's share is an invitation with a landing (the invitee arrives at a reason to join), while content's share is a transmission with a consumption; the two have different metrics and different failure modes.
3. Situate the loop in product-led growth: the product is the sales channel, the loop is the product's built-in acquisition, and the paid and the viral are two inputs to the same growth equation.
4. Read the classic cases as designs: Hotmail (the signature line — the invitation rides the product's own output), Dropbox (the storage incentive — the reward for both sides, the utility as the hook), PayPal (the two-sided payment incentive — the economic reason to refer), Airbnb (the listing-credit split — the two-sided market's natural loop).
5. State the honest base rate: most loops run at k < 1, and the strategy of the course is the strategy of the sub-1 world — where the loop is a multiplier on paid growth, not a substitute for it.

## Phase 3 — Method and Technique

- The loop inventory: the product's natural share moments, the existing referral paths, the incentive structure
- The case-decomposition template: the incentive, the moment, the landing, the metric each case optimized
- The loop-versus-paid attribution baseline: the share of new users by source, before any loop claim
- The product-led read: the funnel where the product carries the sales job
- The base-rate memo: the expected k for the category, and where the product stands

## Phase 4 — Analysis and Diagnosis

- Diagnose the growth source honestly: the viral-attributed cohort versus the paid cohort — the loop's real contribution, not the loop's assumed one.
- Detect the loop-in-name: a share button with no invitation logic (the share goes to a wall, the invitee has no reason to join) is content sharing, not a loop.
- Read the moment: where in the product's use the invitation happens (the value moment, the winning moment, the friction moment) — the moment choice is the design, and the data show which moments convert.
- Check the two-sidedness: a loop that pays only the referrer (or only the invitee) has a structural weakness the two-sided cases fixed with an economic reason.

## Phase 5 — Application and Design

- Map the product's natural moments before designing any incentive: the loop rides the moments the product already has, not a bolted-on campaign.
- Design the incentive from the category's economics (the storage, the payment, the listing credit) — the incentive is the product's value, transferred, not a coupon from the marketing budget.
- Choose the loop's primary metric from the design: the K-factor is the summary, but the moment-level conversion and the landing behavior are the working metrics.
- Write the base-rate memo before the loop launch: the expected k, the category evidence, and the decision rule for what k justifies the investment.

## Phase 6 — Ethics and Boundary Conditions

- The incentive can become the product: users who join only for the reward (and never for the product) are a cohort the loop manufactures and the product must absorb — the retention of the referred is the honesty check.
- Two-sided incentives have a cost structure; the line is the unit economics (the cost per referred below the referred's value), stated, not assumed.
- The loop's data (who referred whom) is relationship data; its use is bound by the consent and the privacy rules of the platform and the jurisdiction.

## Phase 7 — Exercises and Assessment

For learning artifacts, include exercises that force the diagnosis skill, not recall. Use the standard set:

1. Inventory a product's loop candidates: the natural moments, the existing paths, the incentive options — with the recommendation and its base-rate argument.
2. Decompose two classic cases (e.g., Hotmail and Dropbox) with the template: the incentive, the moment, the landing, the metric optimized — and the transferable element.
3. Attribute a product's new-user growth by source: the viral cohort, the paid cohort, the organic — and the loop's honest contribution.
4. Diagnose a loop-in-name: the share button without the invitation logic — the moment, the landing, and the fix that makes it a loop.
5. Design the two-sided incentive for a new product: the economic reason per side, the cost structure, and the unit-economics check.
6. Write the base-rate memo for a category: the expected k, the evidence, and the decision rule for the investment.

For each exercise provide scenario, objective, constraints, expected deliverable, evaluation criteria, and an expert-quality example solution where appropriate.

## Phase 8 — Quality Gate and Delivery

Before delivering, run the delivery checklists. The artifact fails the gate on any “no.”

### Loop definition
- The referrer and the invitee are defined
- The moment is inside the product's use
- The landing gives the invitee a reason

### Attribution baseline
- The new-user sources are separated
- The viral cohort is measured, not assumed
- The base rate is stated

### Design integrity
- The incentive is the product's value, transferred
- The two-sidedness is checked
- The unit economics are stated

### Delivery gate
- The working metrics (moment conversion, landing behavior) are set
- The referred retention is the honesty check
- The consent handling is specified

## Anti-Patterns

the word 'viral' without a measurable loop; the share button without the invitation; the incentive as a coupon divorced from the product's value; the one-sided loop presented as two-sided; the viral cohort counted without the retention check; the base rate ignored.

## Decision Heuristic

Before any claim or recommendation, ask:

1. Is there an actual loop — a moment, an invitation, a landing — or only the word?
2. What is the honest share of growth from the loop, from the attribution?
3. Does the incentive transfer the product's value, or spend the marketing budget?
4. Do the referred stay, or do they join for the reward and leave?
5. What is the category's base rate, and what does it say this design must beat?

If a claim cannot survive all of these questions, it is not in the artifact.

## Final Principle

The goal is not the viral myth. It is the loop defined, the moment found, the incentive that transfers value, and the base rate that keeps the ambition honest — so the product grows through its own use, measurably.
