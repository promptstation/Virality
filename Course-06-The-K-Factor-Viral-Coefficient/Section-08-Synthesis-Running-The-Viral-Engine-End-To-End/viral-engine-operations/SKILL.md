# Synthesis: Running the Viral Engine End to End: Professional Module Builder

## Mission

Produce professional-level material on how the loop is operated as a standing system — the dashboard, the experiments, the economics, the product sync — and how its life is told in the postmortem. The unit of work is a defensible artifact — a learning module, an evidence brief, a case diagnosis, or a strategy memo — in which every claim is tied to a source, a mechanism, and a boundary condition.

Optimize simultaneously for:

- empirical precision (what the canonical studies and works actually found)
- conceptual clarity (the core terms kept strictly distinct)
- causal discipline (correlational and causal claims never blurred)
- practical utility (a practitioner can act on the output the same day)
- intellectual honesty (limits and boundary conditions as visible as the findings)

Do not produce generic advice or pop-science hype. The register is that of an advanced professional training program: confident where the evidence is strong, explicitly conditional where it is not.

## Use Cases

### New professional learning module
When asked to create a full module, course unit, or workshop on this topic:
1. Establish scope: audience, depth, and which claims the module must cover.
2. Build the evidence base from primary sources and canonical works.
3. Develop the core mechanisms and diagnostic logic.
4. Add method, critique, and application sections.
5. End with exercises and the delivery checklists.
6. Deliver the complete artifact with no placeholders.

### Brief or leadership memo
When asked for a short document that must decide something:
1. State the question the document must answer in one sentence.
2. Include only findings strong enough to bear that decision, each with its mechanism and source.
3. Convert results into plain-language implications.
4. End with the specific decisions the evidence changes.
5. Cap length; cut context before cutting caveats.

### Case diagnosis
When asked to explain why a specific piece of content, campaign, or product spread (or did not):
1. Identify the operative mechanisms — never a single assumed cause.
2. Map the case onto the diagnostic framework of this skill.
3. Run the differential: which mechanisms were active, which alternatives were considered and set aside.
4. State confidence and the evidence that would revise the diagnosis.

### Strategy design
When asked to design a campaign, content system, or program around this lever:
1. Fix the objective, audience, and platform context.
2. Select the mechanisms from this skill that fit that context.
3. Specify implementation, sequencing, and the measurement plan.
4. State the boundary conditions under which the plan breaks.

## Core Output Requirements

Regardless of format:

- Every named finding or claim carries its source or mechanism in the same breath.
- Correlational and causal claims are labeled as such wherever they matter.
- Effect claims carry their metric and a plain-language rendering.
- Boundary conditions are stated as explicit “applies when / breaks down when” conditions.
- Examples use real, named studies, campaigns, or artifacts — not invented case studies.
- No padding sections, generic intro/outro filler, or advice that would read the same for any topic.

## Phase 1 — Scope and Claim Audit

### Artifact and audience
What the user actually needs (module vs. memo vs. diagnosis) and who reads it. Different readers make different cuts of the same material.

### Claim inventory
List the claims the artifact will make. For each, note the source or mechanism that supports it and whether that support is correlational, experimental, or canonical. Downgrade or cut unsupported claims — not the caveat.

### Depth budget
Match length to the decision the artifact serves. The fix for a weak document is choosing stronger claims to carry it, not adding hedges to weaker ones.

## Phase 2 — Foundations and Core Content

1. Build the K-factor dashboard as the standing instrument: the k series (per cohort, per cycle, per surface, per channel), the i and the c decomposed, the quality series (the referred retention, the participation), the spam-line monitors, and the economics line (the cost per referred, the contribution) — the one screen that prices the loop's state.
2. Run the experiment pipeline in rotation: the i experiments (the surfaces, the frequency, the features) and the c experiments (the payload, the landing, the onboarding) in the queue, with the guardrails live and the learnings folded into the specs — the pipeline as the loop's standing work, with the allocation by the expected value of the change.
3. Institute the economics review on the quarterly cadence: the decomposition page, the invest/stop decision, the re-entry condition, the succession question — the review that keeps the loop's ambition inside the numbers.
4. Sync the loop with the product roadmap: the moments the product's evolution creates (the new feature's value moment, the new collaboration's handoff), the loop's design reviewed in the roadmap's cadence, and the referral program's team placed with the product, not with the marketing — the organizational design that keeps the loop inside the product.
5. Run the postmortem at the loop's transitions: the launch postmortem (the design's assumptions versus the measured k, the errors found, the spec updates), the scaling postmortem (the levers that held, the knee that arrived, the economics at scale), the plateau postmortem (the saturation's read, the quality's state, the decision), and the handoff postmortem (the engine's total contribution, the cost, the lesson to the next engine) — the four postmortems, each with the numbers and the decision they fed.
6. Set the operating cadence: the daily monitors (the spam-line, the open and the conversion, the anomaly alert), the weekly experiment read (the results, the allocation update, the queue), the monthly cohort review (the quality, the retention, the participation, the cost), and the quarterly economics (the decomposition, the decision) — the rhythm, and the owner per beat.

## Phase 3 — Method and Technique

- The dashboard spec: the series, the resolution, the monitors, the economics line, the alert rules
- The experiment pipeline: the queue, the allocation, the guardrails, the fold-back of the learnings
- The economics review agenda: the decomposition, the decision, the re-entry, the succession
- The product sync: the roadmap review of the loop, the moment pipeline, the team placement
- The postmortem templates: the four transitions, the numbers, the decision, the update

## Phase 4 — Analysis and Diagnosis

- Diagnose the engine's state from the dashboard's state: the k's level, the k's drift, the quality's gap, the economics' payback — the four reads, and the decision each one forces.
- Read the experiment pipeline's output honestly: the experiments that found nothing (the negative result, recorded, the queue adjusted) versus the ones that moved the k (the change shipped, the spec updated).
- Check the product sync's state: the moments the roadmap is creating, unclaimed by the loop — the missed surface is the engine's idle capacity.
- Audit the cadence's discipline: the beat that was skipped (the monthly review over the quarter's crunch) and the decision that went unreviewed — the cadence's debt is the engine's drift.

## Phase 5 — Application and Design

- Stand up the dashboard before the loop scales: the instrument is the system's nervous system, and the scaling without it is the growth without the read.
- Run the pipeline in the expected-value order, with the guardrails the veto — the rotation is the engine's standing work, and the queue is reviewed at the weekly read.
- Hold the economics review even in the scaling quarter: the decision's cadence is the discipline, and the re-entry condition is written in the stop, not remembered at the restart.
- Place the loop's ownership with the product: the team's seat in the roadmap, the moment pipeline as the roadmap's input, and the handoff postmortem as the engine's designed end.

## Phase 6 — Ethics and Boundary Conditions

- The dashboard's data is the user base's behavior and relationships; the access control and the retention follow the sensitivity, and the public version of any of it is the aggregated form.
- The postmortem's numbers name the loop's contribution and its cost publicly (the team, the board); the provenance (the instrumentation's spec, the error battery) is part of the record, and the correction of an earlier number is recorded, not silent.
- The handoff decision (the engine's sunset, the next engine's investment) is a resource decision with a human side (the team, the community the loop built); the line is the decision's honesty (the numbers) and its care (the people), both stated.

## Phase 7 — Exercises and Assessment

For learning artifacts, include exercises that force the diagnosis skill, not recall. Use the standard set:

1. Spec the K-factor dashboard for a live loop: the series, the resolution, the monitors, the economics line, the alerts — with the one-screen layout.
2. Design the experiment pipeline for one quarter: the queue (the i and the c candidates), the allocation, the guardrails, and the weekly read's agenda.
3. Run the quarterly economics review on real data: the decomposition page, the invest/stop decision, the re-entry condition, the succession note.
4. Build the product-sync mechanism: the roadmap review of the loop, the moment pipeline, the team placement — with the first quarter's agenda.
5. Write the four postmortem templates (the launch, the scaling, the plateau, the handoff): the numbers, the decision, the update — and fill one from a real case.
6. Set the operating cadence: the daily, the weekly, the monthly, the quarterly — with the owner per beat and the skip rule (what happens when a beat is missed).

For each exercise provide scenario, objective, constraints, expected deliverable, evaluation criteria, and an expert-quality example solution where appropriate.

## Phase 8 — Quality Gate and Delivery

Before delivering, run the delivery checklists. The artifact fails the gate on any “no.”

### Instrumentation
- The dashboard's series and monitors are live
- The alerts have owners
- The access control is set

### Pipeline
- The queue is allocated by expected value
- The guardrails are the veto
- The negative results are recorded

### Economics
- The review ran on the cadence
- The decision and the re-entry are written
- The succession note is present

### Delivery gate
- The product sync has its seat in the roadmap
- The postmortem for the transition is written
- The cadence's skip rule was honored

## Anti-Patterns

the scale before the instrument; the experiment without the guardrail's veto; the economics review skipped in the growth quarter; the loop owned by the marketing, away from the roadmap; the postmortem that names no number; the handoff that arrives as a surprise.

## Decision Heuristic

Before any claim or recommendation, ask:

1. Can the dashboard tell the engine's state in one screen, right now?
2. What did the last experiment find, and was the finding folded back?
3. When did the economics review last run, and what did it decide?
4. Which moment is the roadmap creating that the loop has not claimed?
5. Is the next transition's postmortem template ready, with its numbers defined?

If a claim cannot survive all of these questions, it is not in the artifact.

## Final Principle

The goal is not a bigger loop. It is an engine with a nervous system, a rhythm, a decision cadence, and a designed end — so the growth it produces is produced knowingly, and the handoff it makes is a handoff, not a collapse.
