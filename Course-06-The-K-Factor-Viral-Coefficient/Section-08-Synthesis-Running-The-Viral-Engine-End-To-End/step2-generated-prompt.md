# Step 2 — Generated Prompt (Result of the Reusable Prompt)

```
Develop a comprehensive, professional-level learning module on **“Synthesis: Running the Viral Engine End to End”** as part of an advanced course on the mathematics of product-led viral growth — the referral loop, its coefficient, and the economics of compounding. The module should how the loop is operated as a standing system — the dashboard, the experiments, the economics, the product sync — and how its life is told in the postmortem.

The material should teach the operations layer: the standing dashboard, the experiment pipeline, the economics review, the product sync, and the cadence that binds them. Go beyond the loop as a campaign with a launch and a wind-down and examine the loop's life as a system with a rhythm — the monitors, the reviews, the decisions — and the handoff to the next engine as the designed end.

Build the K-factor dashboard as the standing instrument: the k series (per cohort, per cycle, per surface, per channel), the i and the c decomposed, the quality series (the referred retention, the participation), the spam-line monitors, and the economics line (the cost per referred, the contribution) — the one screen that prices the loop's state.

Run the experiment pipeline in rotation: the i experiments (the surfaces, the frequency, the features) and the c experiments (the payload, the landing, the onboarding) in the queue, with the guardrails live and the learnings folded into the specs — the pipeline as the loop's standing work, with the allocation by the expected value of the change.

Institute the economics review on the quarterly cadence: the decomposition page, the invest/stop decision, the re-entry condition, the succession question — the review that keeps the loop's ambition inside the numbers.

Sync the loop with the product roadmap: the moments the product's evolution creates (the new feature's value moment, the new collaboration's handoff), the loop's design reviewed in the roadmap's cadence, and the referral program's team placed with the product, not with the marketing — the organizational design that keeps the loop inside the product.

Run the postmortem at the loop's transitions: the launch postmortem (the design's assumptions versus the measured k, the errors found, the spec updates), the scaling postmortem (the levers that held, the knee that arrived, the economics at scale), the plateau postmortem (the saturation's read, the quality's state, the decision), and the handoff postmortem (the engine's total contribution, the cost, the lesson to the next engine) — the four postmortems, each with the numbers and the decision they fed.

Set the operating cadence: the daily monitors (the spam-line, the open and the conversion, the anomaly alert), the weekly experiment read (the results, the allocation update, the queue), the monthly cohort review (the quality, the retention, the participation, the cost), and the quarterly economics (the decomposition, the decision) — the rhythm, and the owner per beat.

Include practical methods for working at a professional level:
* The dashboard spec: the series, the resolution, the monitors, the economics line, the alert rules
* The experiment pipeline: the queue, the allocation, the guardrails, the fold-back of the learnings
* The economics review agenda: the decomposition, the decision, the re-entry, the succession
* The product sync: the roadmap review of the loop, the moment pipeline, the team placement
* The postmortem templates: the four transitions, the numbers, the decision, the update

Include practical exercises that require the learner to:
1. Spec the K-factor dashboard for a live loop: the series, the resolution, the monitors, the economics line, the alerts — with the one-screen layout.
2. Design the experiment pipeline for one quarter: the queue (the i and the c candidates), the allocation, the guardrails, and the weekly read's agenda.
3. Run the quarterly economics review on real data: the decomposition page, the invest/stop decision, the re-entry condition, the succession note.
4. Build the product-sync mechanism: the roadmap review of the loop, the moment pipeline, the team placement — with the first quarter's agenda.
5. Write the four postmortem templates (the launch, the scaling, the plateau, the handoff): the numbers, the decision, the update — and fill one from a real case.
6. Set the operating cadence: the daily, the weekly, the monthly, the quarterly — with the owner per beat and the skip rule (what happens when a beat is missed).

For each exercise, provide the scenario, objective, constraints, expected deliverable, evaluation criteria, and an expert-quality example solution where appropriate.

Structure the material progressively, beginning with the dashboard and the pipeline, moving through the economics review and the product sync, then to the cadence, the postmortems, and the designed handoff. Establish clear conceptual distinctions between **monitor, experiment, review, sync, and postmortem** before showing how they interact.

Use professional terminology from product growth, referral marketing, and unit economics where relevant, but explain specialized terminology in clear language. Do not make the material sound like generic AI-generated advice; the register should be that of an advanced professional training module used by product growth managers, referral-program designers, and startup founders.

Research the subject using high-quality professional and academic sources where external research materially strengthens the material. Prioritize authoritative sources: the product-led-growth literature and case studies (Dropbox, Hotmail, PayPal, Airbnb, TikTok-style loops), the viral-coefficient and referral-program practice literature, and the standard growth-analytics methods. Avoid relying primarily on low-quality SEO articles, generic k factor viral coefficient blogs, or unsupported “best practice” claims. Where useful, distinguish established research findings from professional conventions and informed recommendations.

Pay particular attention to the relationship between this module and principles such as **the instrument before the scale, the guardrail as veto, the cadence as discipline, and the handoff as design**. Connect these concepts to concrete analytical and design decisions rather than discussing them only theoretically.

Create a professional framework that product growth managers, referral-program designers, and startup founders could actually use in a real project. The final material should therefore function simultaneously as a learning resource, a practical reference, and a working methodology for designing and operating referral loops and product-led growth.

Throughout the module, prioritize language that is:

* Precise without being jargon-heavy
* Empirical without being statistical theater
* Confident about what the evidence establishes
* Explicit about what the evidence does not establish
* Conditional where the findings are conditional
* Practical where the decisions are practical

Where a claim depends on context (platform, audience, topic, measurement), explicitly explain the trade-off and the boundary condition rather than presenting it as an absolute rule.

End the module with **Instrumentation checklist**, **Pipeline checklist**, **Economics checklist**, and a **Delivery gate checklist** that a professional team can use before acting on designing and operating referral loops and product-led growth.

The final result should be comprehensive enough to serve as an advanced professional training module, but organized clearly enough that a learner can study it progressively and apply each concept in designing and operating referral loops and product-led growth.
```
