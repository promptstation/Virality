# Step 2 — Generated Prompt (Result of the Reusable Prompt)

```
Develop a comprehensive, professional-level learning module on **“Optimizing c: The Invitation's Conversion and the Referred's Quality”** as part of an advanced course on the mathematics of product-led viral growth — the referral loop, its coefficient, and the economics of compounding. The module should how c is raised — the open, the landing, the referred's onboarding — and how the referred's quality is checked against the organic baseline.

The material should teach the c lever: the invitation's open, the landing's conversion, the referred's onboarding, and the quality check that keeps the conversion honest. Go beyond the conversion as a landing-page problem, separate from the invitation and the referred's first week and examine the referred cohort as the loop's product — its retention, its value, its own loop participation — against the organic baseline.

Teach the open rate as c's first gate: the payload's specificity (the value preview beats the generic ask), the timing (the send at the value moment, the channel's notification behavior), and the personalization (the relationship line, the shared context) — the open-rate levers, and their measurement per referrer tier.

Teach the landing's conversion: the first screen's job (the promise kept — the value preview, the referrer's social proof, the context of why this, why now), the value before the signup (the preview, the partial access, the saved state), and the path (the steps to the first value, under the budget) — the landing as the invitation's continuation, with its own funnel and its own audit.

Teach the referred's onboarding: the first session designed for the referred (the context of the referrer, the first-value moment matched to the invitation's promise, the second session's hook) — and the loop's closure: the referred's first moment when they can themselves refer (the referred becomes the referrer, and the cohort's loop participation is the loop's compounding).

Cover the quality dimension: the referred's retention curve versus the organic's (the gap, its direction, its causes — the reward-driven cohort, the value-driven cohort), the referred's LTV versus the acquisition cost (the incentive's cost included), and the referred's loop participation rate (the referred who refer, at what rate) — the three quality metrics, and the honesty rule: a loop whose referred under-retain the organic is buying churn with the incentive, and the k is overstated by the quality it hides.

Build the c experiment program: the A/B design (the arms — the payload, the landing, the onboarding variants; the metrics — the open, the conversion, the retention; the sample and the duration), the sequential learning (the bandit allocation across the variants, the stopping rule), and the guardrail set (the retention, the opt-out, the complaint rate) that bounds the optimization — the experiment as the c's standing work.

Include practical methods for working at a professional level:
* The open-rate analysis: per payload, per timing, per channel, per referrer tier
* The landing funnel: the screen-by-screen conversion, the value-before-signup measurement, the path audit
* The referred onboarding spec: the first session, the first-value moment, the loop-closure moment
* The quality comparison: the referred versus the organic — the retention curves, the LTV, the participation
* The experiment program: the arms, the metrics, the sample, the bandit allocation, the guardrails

Include practical exercises that require the learner to:
1. Analyze the open rate by payload, timing, channel, and referrer tier; identify the two levers with the largest headroom and the test for each.
2. Audit a referral landing: the promise-keeping, the value before the signup, the path — with the redesign of the largest drop.
3. Design the referred onboarding: the first session, the first-value moment, the loop-closure moment — with the participation metric and its target.
4. Run the quality comparison on a cohort: the referred versus the organic retention, the LTV with the incentive cost, the participation — and the honesty verdict.
5. Design the c experiment program: the arms, the metrics, the sample, the bandit allocation, the guardrails, and the stopping rule.
6. Write the dark-pattern boundary note: the conversion tactics the guardrail vetoes, the retention detector, and the ethics review's standard — as the standing policy.

For each exercise, provide the scenario, objective, constraints, expected deliverable, evaluation criteria, and an expert-quality example solution where appropriate.

Structure the material progressively, beginning with the gates of c and their levers, moving through the referred's onboarding and the quality check, then to the experiment program with its guardrails, running. Establish clear conceptual distinctions between **open, landing conversion, onboarding, referred quality, and loop closure** before showing how they interact.

Use professional terminology from product growth, referral marketing, and unit economics where relevant, but explain specialized terminology in clear language. Do not make the material sound like generic AI-generated advice; the register should be that of an advanced professional training module used by product growth managers, referral-program designers, and startup founders.

Research the subject using high-quality professional and academic sources where external research materially strengthens the material. Prioritize authoritative sources: the product-led-growth literature and case studies (Dropbox, Hotmail, PayPal, Airbnb, TikTok-style loops), the viral-coefficient and referral-program practice literature, and the standard growth-analytics methods. Avoid relying primarily on low-quality SEO articles, generic k factor viral coefficient blogs, or unsupported “best practice” claims. Where useful, distinguish established research findings from professional conventions and informed recommendations.

Pay particular attention to the relationship between this module and principles such as **the gate-by-gate order, the promise kept, the retention honesty, and the guardrail veto**. Connect these concepts to concrete analytical and design decisions rather than discussing them only theoretically.

Create a professional framework that product growth managers, referral-program designers, and startup founders could actually use in a real project. The final material should therefore function simultaneously as a learning resource, a practical reference, and a working methodology for designing and operating referral loops and product-led growth.

Throughout the module, prioritize language that is:

* Precise without being jargon-heavy
* Empirical without being statistical theater
* Confident about what the evidence establishes
* Explicit about what the evidence does not establish
* Conditional where the findings are conditional
* Practical where the decisions are practical

Where a claim depends on context (platform, audience, topic, measurement), explicitly explain the trade-off and the boundary condition rather than presenting it as an absolute rule.

End the module with **Gate optimization checklist**, **Onboarding checklist**, **Quality check checklist**, and a **Delivery gate checklist** that a professional team can use before acting on designing and operating referral loops and product-led growth.

The final result should be comprehensive enough to serve as an advanced professional training module, but organized clearly enough that a learner can study it progressively and apply each concept in designing and operating referral loops and product-led growth.
```
