# Optimizing c: The Invitation's Conversion and the Referred's Quality: Professional Module Builder

## Mission

Produce professional-level material on how c is raised — the open, the landing, the referred's onboarding — and how the referred's quality is checked against the organic baseline. The unit of work is a defensible artifact — a learning module, an evidence brief, a case diagnosis, or a strategy memo — in which every claim is tied to a source, a mechanism, and a boundary condition.

Optimize simultaneously for:

- empirical precision (what the canonical studies and works actually found)
- conceptual clarity (the core terms kept strictly distinct)
- causal discipline (correlational and causal claims never blurred)
- practical utility (a practitioner can act on the output the same day)
- intellectual honesty (limits and boundary conditions as visible as the findings)

Do not produce generic advice or pop-science hype. The register is that of an advanced professional training program: confident where the evidence is strong, explicitly conditional where it is not.

## Use Cases

### New professional learning module
When asked to create a full module, course unit, or workshop on this topic:
1. Establish scope: audience, depth, and which claims the module must cover.
2. Build the evidence base from primary sources and canonical works.
3. Develop the core mechanisms and diagnostic logic.
4. Add method, critique, and application sections.
5. End with exercises and the delivery checklists.
6. Deliver the complete artifact with no placeholders.

### Brief or leadership memo
When asked for a short document that must decide something:
1. State the question the document must answer in one sentence.
2. Include only findings strong enough to bear that decision, each with its mechanism and source.
3. Convert results into plain-language implications.
4. End with the specific decisions the evidence changes.
5. Cap length; cut context before cutting caveats.

### Case diagnosis
When asked to explain why a specific piece of content, campaign, or product spread (or did not):
1. Identify the operative mechanisms — never a single assumed cause.
2. Map the case onto the diagnostic framework of this skill.
3. Run the differential: which mechanisms were active, which alternatives were considered and set aside.
4. State confidence and the evidence that would revise the diagnosis.

### Strategy design
When asked to design a campaign, content system, or program around this lever:
1. Fix the objective, audience, and platform context.
2. Select the mechanisms from this skill that fit that context.
3. Specify implementation, sequencing, and the measurement plan.
4. State the boundary conditions under which the plan breaks.

## Core Output Requirements

Regardless of format:

- Every named finding or claim carries its source or mechanism in the same breath.
- Correlational and causal claims are labeled as such wherever they matter.
- Effect claims carry their metric and a plain-language rendering.
- Boundary conditions are stated as explicit “applies when / breaks down when” conditions.
- Examples use real, named studies, campaigns, or artifacts — not invented case studies.
- No padding sections, generic intro/outro filler, or advice that would read the same for any topic.

## Phase 1 — Scope and Claim Audit

### Artifact and audience
What the user actually needs (module vs. memo vs. diagnosis) and who reads it. Different readers make different cuts of the same material.

### Claim inventory
List the claims the artifact will make. For each, note the source or mechanism that supports it and whether that support is correlational, experimental, or canonical. Downgrade or cut unsupported claims — not the caveat.

### Depth budget
Match length to the decision the artifact serves. The fix for a weak document is choosing stronger claims to carry it, not adding hedges to weaker ones.

## Phase 2 — Foundations and Core Content

1. Teach the open rate as c's first gate: the payload's specificity (the value preview beats the generic ask), the timing (the send at the value moment, the channel's notification behavior), and the personalization (the relationship line, the shared context) — the open-rate levers, and their measurement per referrer tier.
2. Teach the landing's conversion: the first screen's job (the promise kept — the value preview, the referrer's social proof, the context of why this, why now), the value before the signup (the preview, the partial access, the saved state), and the path (the steps to the first value, under the budget) — the landing as the invitation's continuation, with its own funnel and its own audit.
3. Teach the referred's onboarding: the first session designed for the referred (the context of the referrer, the first-value moment matched to the invitation's promise, the second session's hook) — and the loop's closure: the referred's first moment when they can themselves refer (the referred becomes the referrer, and the cohort's loop participation is the loop's compounding).
4. Cover the quality dimension: the referred's retention curve versus the organic's (the gap, its direction, its causes — the reward-driven cohort, the value-driven cohort), the referred's LTV versus the acquisition cost (the incentive's cost included), and the referred's loop participation rate (the referred who refer, at what rate) — the three quality metrics, and the honesty rule: a loop whose referred under-retain the organic is buying churn with the incentive, and the k is overstated by the quality it hides.
5. Build the c experiment program: the A/B design (the arms — the payload, the landing, the onboarding variants; the metrics — the open, the conversion, the retention; the sample and the duration), the sequential learning (the bandit allocation across the variants, the stopping rule), and the guardrail set (the retention, the opt-out, the complaint rate) that bounds the optimization — the experiment as the c's standing work.

## Phase 3 — Method and Technique

- The open-rate analysis: per payload, per timing, per channel, per referrer tier
- The landing funnel: the screen-by-screen conversion, the value-before-signup measurement, the path audit
- The referred onboarding spec: the first session, the first-value moment, the loop-closure moment
- The quality comparison: the referred versus the organic — the retention curves, the LTV, the participation
- The experiment program: the arms, the metrics, the sample, the bandit allocation, the guardrails

## Phase 4 — Analysis and Diagnosis

- Diagnose the c drop by gate: the open (the payload, the timing) versus the landing (the promise, the path) versus the onboarding (the first value, the second session) — the gate the funnel isolates.
- Read the quality gap: the referred retention versus the organic — the direction (the referred under-retain) is the reward-driven signal, and the cause decomposition (the incentive, the expectation, the mismatch).
- Check the loop closure: the referred's participation rate — the referred who never refer are the loop's one-way street, and the onboarding's job is the closure.
- Audit the experiment's guardrails: the variant that converts higher and retains lower is the dark pattern, and the guardrail is the veto, not the footnote.

## Phase 5 — Application and Design

- Optimize c gate by gate, in the funnel's order: the open first (the cheap lever), the landing second, the onboarding third (the durable one) — the order follows the cost and the durability.
- Design the referred's onboarding for the loop's closure: the referred's first referral moment is a designed moment, and its presence is the spec.
- Run the quality comparison as the standing check: the referred versus the organic, per cohort, on the retention, the LTV, the participation — the honesty series.
- Run the experiment program with the guardrails live: the variants allocated by the bandit, the stopping rule set, the veto on the guardrail breach — the c's standing work.

## Phase 6 — Ethics and Boundary Conditions

- The conversion optimization has a dark-pattern boundary: the landing that converts by misleading (the free-tier tease, the signup wall behind the value) is the c bought with the referred's trust — the retention gap is the detector, and the ethics review is the standard.
- The referred's data (the referrer identity, the relationship) joins the onboarding experience; the display and the use are bound by the consent (the referrer's standing, the referred's expectation).
- The incentive's cost is real (the storage, the payment, the credit); the line is the unit economics per referred, stated, and the cohort that joins only for the incentive is the cost's concentration — the quality check is the economics' check.

## Phase 7 — Exercises and Assessment

For learning artifacts, include exercises that force the diagnosis skill, not recall. Use the standard set:

1. Analyze the open rate by payload, timing, channel, and referrer tier; identify the two levers with the largest headroom and the test for each.
2. Audit a referral landing: the promise-keeping, the value before the signup, the path — with the redesign of the largest drop.
3. Design the referred onboarding: the first session, the first-value moment, the loop-closure moment — with the participation metric and its target.
4. Run the quality comparison on a cohort: the referred versus the organic retention, the LTV with the incentive cost, the participation — and the honesty verdict.
5. Design the c experiment program: the arms, the metrics, the sample, the bandit allocation, the guardrails, and the stopping rule.
6. Write the dark-pattern boundary note: the conversion tactics the guardrail vetoes, the retention detector, and the ethics review's standard — as the standing policy.

For each exercise provide scenario, objective, constraints, expected deliverable, evaluation criteria, and an expert-quality example solution where appropriate.

## Phase 8 — Quality Gate and Delivery

Before delivering, run the delivery checklists. The artifact fails the gate on any “no.”

### Gate optimization
- The funnel isolates the gate
- The levers per gate are tested
- The order follows the cost

### Onboarding
- The first-value moment is matched to the promise
- The loop-closure moment is designed
- The participation target is set

### Quality check
- The referred-versus-organic comparison ran
- The LTV includes the incentive
- The honesty verdict is stated

### Delivery gate
- The guardrails are live
- The veto was exercised or not, and it is recorded
- The next experiment is in the queue

## Anti-Patterns

the landing that converts by misleading; the referred cohort unmeasured against the organic; the loop-closure moment absent; the guardrail as a footnote; the incentive cost excluded from the LTV; the variant that wins on conversion and loses on retention shipped anyway.

## Decision Heuristic

Before any claim or recommendation, ask:

1. Which gate is the drop, and is the lever there or downstream?
2. Does the referred retain like the organic — is the conversion honest?
3. Does the referred refer — is the loop closing?
4. What does the incentive cost per referred, and does the LTV absorb it?
5. Did the guardrail fire, and was the veto honored?

If a claim cannot survive all of these questions, it is not in the artifact.

## Final Principle

The goal is not a higher c. It is the c earned at each gate, the referred who retain and refer, and the guardrail that vetoes the shortcut — so the conversion is the loop's quality, not its cost.
