# Step 2 — Generated Prompt (Result of the Reusable Prompt)

```
Develop a comprehensive, professional-level learning module on **“Foundations of Social Transmission: Why People Share”** as part of an advanced course on the science of why ideas, products, and messages spread socially. The module should the social mechanics of transmission — why people share, what 'spreading' actually measures, and how content-driven spread differs from context-driven spread.

The material should teach the motives behind sharing and the way transmission is measured before any lever is applied. Go beyond the assumption that great content simply goes viral on its own and examine the empirical base that separates what content does from what context does.

Explain the four social motives that drive sharing — to look good (social currency), to help others (practical value), to feel something (emotion), and to stay connected (social maintenance) — and show how every STEPPS lever maps onto one of them.

Teach the difference between content-driven and context-driven spread using the field evidence: the same content performs differently with and without placement, promotion, and timing, and neither factor alone explains virality.

Define the measurement layer — share counts, unique reach, and transmission velocity — and explain why raw counts mislead without denominators and time windows.

Introduce the idea of a transmission chain: who sees the content, who shares it, who they reach — the network view that the later courses formalize.

Teach how to read a piece of content's spread history as data: the shape of a share curve (spike, plateau, tail) tells you which lever is doing the work.

Include practical methods for working at a professional level:
* Collecting share and reach data from a platform or CMS
* Distinguishing organic from promoted share events
* Computing transmission velocity and reach-per-share
* Reading a share curve to identify the driving lever
* Building a transmission baseline for a brand's historical content

Include practical exercises that require the learner to:
1. Write a one-page analysis of a piece of content that spread: separate the content-driven from the context-driven contribution and identify the dominant sharing motive.
2. Build a transmission baseline for a brand: collect three months of share data, compute velocity and reach-per-share, and identify the top three historical performers by motive.
3. Given five candidate content ideas, assign each a primary sharing motive and predict its transmission curve shape (spike vs. plateau) with justification.
4. Design the measurement plan for a new content project: metrics, denominators, time windows, and the decision rule for whether the lever worked.
5. Take a content piece that underperformed and produce a differential diagnosis: motive mismatch, curve shape, denominator, or context.
6. Draft a client memo arguing that a campaign's 'viral' success was context-driven, with the evidence and the implication for the next campaign's budget.

For each exercise, provide the scenario, objective, constraints, expected deliverable, evaluation criteria, and an expert-quality example solution where appropriate.

Structure the material progressively, beginning with why people share and how transmission is measured, moving through the motives and the content-versus-context distinction, then to motivated content design and evaluation. Establish clear conceptual distinctions between **sharing motive, transmission outcome, content-driven spread, and context-driven spread** before showing how they interact.

Use professional terminology from marketing science, social psychology, and communication research where relevant, but explain specialized terminology in clear language. Do not make the material sound like generic AI-generated advice; the register should be that of an advanced professional training module used by content strategists, brand marketers, and communication researchers.

Research the subject using high-quality professional and academic sources where external research materially strengthens the material. Prioritize authoritative sources: Jonah Berger's research program (Journal of Consumer Research, Journal of Marketing Research, and the book 'Contagious'), the word-of-mouth and social-contagion literature, and replication studies of the six levers. Avoid relying primarily on low-quality SEO articles, generic stepps framework blogs, or unsupported “best practice” claims. Where useful, distinguish established research findings from professional conventions and informed recommendations.

Pay particular attention to the relationship between this module and principles such as **social identity, action readiness, denominator discipline, and curve reading**. Connect these concepts to concrete analytical and design decisions rather than discussing them only theoretically.

Create a professional framework that content strategists, brand marketers, and communication researchers could actually use in a real project. The final material should therefore function simultaneously as a learning resource, a practical reference, and a working methodology for content and communication strategy.

Throughout the module, prioritize language that is:

* Precise without being jargon-heavy
* Empirical without being statistical theater
* Confident about what the evidence establishes
* Explicit about what the evidence does not establish
* Conditional where the findings are conditional
* Practical where the decisions are practical

Where a claim depends on context (platform, audience, topic, measurement), explicitly explain the trade-off and the boundary condition rather than presenting it as an absolute rule.

End the module with **Sharing-motive audit checklist**, **Transmission measurement plan checklist**, **Content-versus-context diagnosis checklist**, and a **Delivery gate checklist** that a professional team can use before acting on content and communication strategy.

The final result should be comprehensive enough to serve as an advanced professional training module, but organized clearly enough that a learner can study it progressively and apply each concept in content and communication strategy.
```
