# Step 2 — Generated Prompt (Result of the Reusable Prompt)

```
Develop a comprehensive, professional-level learning module on **“Emotion and Arousal as a Lever”** as part of an advanced course on the science of why ideas, products, and messages spread socially. The module should how evoked emotional arousal — specifically high-arousal emotion — drives transmission, and how to target it deliberately within a content lever.

The material should teach the arousal finding and its operational use: choosing the target emotion, directing the creative to evoke it, and measuring the result. Go beyond the folk rules that 'happy content spreads' or that 'bad news travels fastest' and examine the field and experimental evidence that arousal — not valence alone — is the driver.

State the core finding precisely: high-arousal content — awe, anger, anxiety, amusement — transmits more than low-arousal content, and positive high-arousal is more viral on average than negative, with the mechanism being activation, not positivity.

Teach target-emotion selection: the brand-safe default is high-arousal positive (awe, excitement); anger and anxiety carry power and brand risk, and the choice must be made deliberately, not by drift.

Show the directing levers for arousal — stakes, specificity, sensory detail, urgency, contradiction, personal relevance — each tied to the activation mechanism.

Teach the measurement of evoked emotion: self-report scales, text coding of the final content, and the validation loop from evoked emotion to share lift.

Diagnose the flat-content failure: production quality without evoked arousal, the most common reason polished content fails to transmit.

Include practical methods for working at a professional level:
* Self-report emotion sampling (valence–arousal ratings from a test audience)
* Lexicon-based emotion coding of scripts and captions
* A/B testing arousal-framed versus neutral versions of the same story
* Tracking evoked-emotion scores against share velocity
* Building an emotion target into the creative brief (named emotion, valence sign, intensity)

Include practical exercises that require the learner to:
1. Produce the emotion profile of a piece of content that underperformed: evoked emotion, valence, arousal, and the share data it predicts.
2. Design the target-emotion spec for a campaign: named emotion, valence, intensity, the directing levers that will carry it, and the risk note.
3. Rewrite a neutral product story into three versions — awe, excitement, urgency — using only the arousal levers, and predict the ranking.
4. Build the pretest: 30 respondents, valence–arousal scales, and the decision rule for whether the emotion target was hit.
5. A/B test an arousal-framed versus neutral version of the same asset; interpret the share-velocity differential and the mediation by evoked arousal.
6. Write the ethics note for a campaign proposing an anger-driven message: the power, the risk, the alternatives, and the conditions under which it would be justified.

For each exercise, provide the scenario, objective, constraints, expected deliverable, evaluation criteria, and an expert-quality example solution where appropriate.

Structure the material progressively, beginning with the arousal finding and its mechanism, moving through target-emotion selection, directing, and measurement, then to an emotion-targeted campaign validated from pretest to post. Establish clear conceptual distinctions between **evoked emotion, displayed emotion, valence, arousal, and emotional risk** before showing how they interact.

Use professional terminology from marketing science, social psychology, and communication research where relevant, but explain specialized terminology in clear language. Do not make the material sound like generic AI-generated advice; the register should be that of an advanced professional training module used by content strategists, brand marketers, and communication researchers.

Research the subject using high-quality professional and academic sources where external research materially strengthens the material. Prioritize authoritative sources: Jonah Berger's research program (Journal of Consumer Research, Journal of Marketing Research, and the book 'Contagious'), the word-of-mouth and social-contagion literature, and replication studies of the six levers. Avoid relying primarily on low-quality SEO articles, generic stepps framework blogs, or unsupported “best practice” claims. Where useful, distinguish established research findings from professional conventions and informed recommendations.

Pay particular attention to the relationship between this module and principles such as **activation mechanism, evoked-versus-displayed emotion, valence risk, and pretest validation**. Connect these concepts to concrete analytical and design decisions rather than discussing them only theoretically.

Create a professional framework that content strategists, brand marketers, and communication researchers could actually use in a real project. The final material should therefore function simultaneously as a learning resource, a practical reference, and a working methodology for content and communication strategy.

Throughout the module, prioritize language that is:

* Precise without being jargon-heavy
* Empirical without being statistical theater
* Confident about what the evidence establishes
* Explicit about what the evidence does not establish
* Conditional where the findings are conditional
* Practical where the decisions are practical

Where a claim depends on context (platform, audience, topic, measurement), explicitly explain the trade-off and the boundary condition rather than presenting it as an absolute rule.

End the module with **Emotion target spec checklist**, **Pretest plan checklist**, **Directing audit checklist**, and a **Delivery gate checklist** that a professional team can use before acting on content and communication strategy.

The final result should be comprehensive enough to serve as an advanced professional training module, but organized clearly enough that a learner can study it progressively and apply each concept in content and communication strategy.
```
