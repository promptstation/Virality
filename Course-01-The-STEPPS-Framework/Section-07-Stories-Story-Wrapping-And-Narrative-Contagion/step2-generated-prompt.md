# Step 2 — Generated Prompt (Result of the Reusable Prompt)

```
Develop a comprehensive, professional-level learning module on **“Stories: Story Wrapping and Narrative Contagion”** as part of an advanced course on the science of why ideas, products, and messages spread socially. The module should how narrative transport makes embedded ideas contagious, and how to wrap a message inside a story structure that is shared for the story itself.

The material should teach the story-wrap logic: the audience shares the story, and the message travels inside it — and how to build and audit that wrap. Go beyond the assumption that a good message plus good design will transmit and examine the narrative-transport evidence and the difference between stories that carry and stories that decorate.

Explain narrative transport: an audience absorbed in a story lowers its resistance to the embedded idea, and the story — not the idea — is what gets shared.

Teach the wrap structure — situation, character, tension, turn, resolution — and why the message must be load-bearing: the story should fail to land if the message is removed.

Show the product-reveal craft: the message appears as the story's answer, tool, or consequence — never as an interruption — with the failure pattern of the decorated story (story first, pitch stapled on).

Teach the story/anecdote distinction: a story has a turn and a resolution that changes the situation; an anecdote illustrates; only the former transmits the embedded idea.

Evaluate wrap quality with the removal test: delete the message from the story and check whether the story still works — if it does, the message was decoration.

Include practical methods for working at a professional level:
* Building a story from a factual case (situation → character → tension → turn → resolution)
* The removal test for load-bearing messages
* Auditing a script for transport markers (specificity, character want, stakes, turn)
* Testing story versus fact-list versions of the same message for transmission
* Mapping the reveal point (when the message appears) across a series of assets

Include practical exercises that require the learner to:
1. Wrap a given message in a full story (situation, character, tension, turn, resolution) and run the removal test on it.
2. Audit three existing assets for wrap quality: transport markers, reveal point, load-bearing-ness — with the score.
3. Rewrite a stapled pitch (story + pitch) as a true wrap, moving the message to the turn.
4. Build a story bank: one message, three wraps, three audiences — with the audience fit for each.
5. A/B test a story version versus a fact-list version of the same message; interpret the transmission and recall differentials.
6. Write the ethics review for a story that uses a real case: the consent, the accuracy, the cherry-picking check, and the line that would make it propaganda.

For each exercise, provide the scenario, objective, constraints, expected deliverable, evaluation criteria, and an expert-quality example solution where appropriate.

Structure the material progressively, beginning with narrative transport and the wrap concept, moving through structure, reveal craft, and evaluation, then to a story bank with validated wraps and an ethics gate. Establish clear conceptual distinctions between **story, anecdote, wrap, reveal point, and load-bearing message** before showing how they interact.

Use professional terminology from marketing science, social psychology, and communication research where relevant, but explain specialized terminology in clear language. Do not make the material sound like generic AI-generated advice; the register should be that of an advanced professional training module used by content strategists, brand marketers, and communication researchers.

Research the subject using high-quality professional and academic sources where external research materially strengthens the material. Prioritize authoritative sources: Jonah Berger's research program (Journal of Consumer Research, Journal of Marketing Research, and the book 'Contagious'), the word-of-mouth and social-contagion literature, and replication studies of the six levers. Avoid relying primarily on low-quality SEO articles, generic stepps framework blogs, or unsupported “best practice” claims. Where useful, distinguish established research findings from professional conventions and informed recommendations.

Pay particular attention to the relationship between this module and principles such as **narrative transport, load-bearing messages, the removal test, and case truthfulness**. Connect these concepts to concrete analytical and design decisions rather than discussing them only theoretically.

Create a professional framework that content strategists, brand marketers, and communication researchers could actually use in a real project. The final material should therefore function simultaneously as a learning resource, a practical reference, and a working methodology for content and communication strategy.

Throughout the module, prioritize language that is:

* Precise without being jargon-heavy
* Empirical without being statistical theater
* Confident about what the evidence establishes
* Explicit about what the evidence does not establish
* Conditional where the findings are conditional
* Practical where the decisions are practical

Where a claim depends on context (platform, audience, topic, measurement), explicitly explain the trade-off and the boundary condition rather than presenting it as an absolute rule.

End the module with **Wrap structure checklist**, **Removal test checklist**, **Transport audit checklist**, and a **Delivery gate checklist** that a professional team can use before acting on content and communication strategy.

The final result should be comprehensive enough to serve as an advanced professional training module, but organized clearly enough that a learner can study it progressively and apply each concept in content and communication strategy.
```
