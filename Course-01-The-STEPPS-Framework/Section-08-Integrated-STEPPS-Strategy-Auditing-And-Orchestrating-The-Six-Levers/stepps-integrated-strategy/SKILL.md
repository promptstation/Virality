# Integrated STEPPS Strategy: Auditing and Orchestrating the Six Levers: Professional Module Builder

## Mission

Produce professional-level material on how the six STEPPS levers work as a system — audit, weighting, pipeline, and measurement — so that transmission becomes a designed outcome rather than a lucky event. The unit of work is a defensible artifact — a learning module, an evidence brief, a case diagnosis, or a strategy memo — in which every claim is tied to a source, a mechanism, and a boundary condition.

Optimize simultaneously for:

- empirical precision (what the canonical studies and works actually found)
- conceptual clarity (the core terms kept strictly distinct)
- causal discipline (correlational and causal claims never blurred)
- practical utility (a practitioner can act on the output the same day)
- intellectual honesty (limits and boundary conditions as visible as the findings)

Do not produce generic advice or pop-science hype. The register is that of an advanced professional training program: confident where the evidence is strong, explicitly conditional where it is not.

## Use Cases

### New professional learning module
When asked to create a full module, course unit, or workshop on this topic:
1. Establish scope: audience, depth, and which claims the module must cover.
2. Build the evidence base from primary sources and canonical works.
3. Develop the core mechanisms and diagnostic logic.
4. Add method, critique, and application sections.
5. End with exercises and the delivery checklists.
6. Deliver the complete artifact with no placeholders.

### Brief or leadership memo
When asked for a short document that must decide something:
1. State the question the document must answer in one sentence.
2. Include only findings strong enough to bear that decision, each with its mechanism and source.
3. Convert results into plain-language implications.
4. End with the specific decisions the evidence changes.
5. Cap length; cut context before cutting caveats.

### Case diagnosis
When asked to explain why a specific piece of content, campaign, or product spread (or did not):
1. Identify the operative mechanisms — never a single assumed cause.
2. Map the case onto the diagnostic framework of this skill.
3. Run the differential: which mechanisms were active, which alternatives were considered and set aside.
4. State confidence and the evidence that would revise the diagnosis.

### Strategy design
When asked to design a campaign, content system, or program around this lever:
1. Fix the objective, audience, and platform context.
2. Select the mechanisms from this skill that fit that context.
3. Specify implementation, sequencing, and the measurement plan.
4. State the boundary conditions under which the plan breaks.

## Core Output Requirements

Regardless of format:

- Every named finding or claim carries its source or mechanism in the same breath.
- Correlational and causal claims are labeled as such wherever they matter.
- Effect claims carry their metric and a plain-language rendering.
- Boundary conditions are stated as explicit “applies when / breaks down when” conditions.
- Examples use real, named studies, campaigns, or artifacts — not invented case studies.
- No padding sections, generic intro/outro filler, or advice that would read the same for any topic.

## Phase 1 — Scope and Claim Audit

### Artifact and audience
What the user actually needs (module vs. memo vs. diagnosis) and who reads it. Different readers make different cuts of the same material.

### Claim inventory
List the claims the artifact will make. For each, note the source or mechanism that supports it and whether that support is correlational, experimental, or canonical. Downgrade or cut unsupported claims — not the caveat.

### Depth budget
Match length to the decision the artifact serves. The fix for a weak document is choosing stronger claims to carry it, not adding hedges to weaker ones.

## Phase 2 — Foundations and Core Content

1. Teach the STEPPS audit: score each asset 0–5 on each lever, identify the dominant lever, and flag assets that attempt all six and achieve none.
2. Show the weighting logic: the dominant lever is set by audience (who shares what), platform (what the surface rewards), and goal (reach, conversion, or norm change).
3. Build the content engine: the pipeline in which every asset enters with a named lever, a measurement plan, and a follow-up asset, and exits with a post-mortem against the lever's KPI.
4. Teach lever interaction: story carries emotion and currency; triggers re-activate value; visibility amplifies the observed result — and the conflicts (a utility asset does not need a story wrap; an awe campaign does not need a checklist).
5. Run the synthesis: a complete campaign, from brief (lever choice, weighting) through production (wrap, directing, affordances) to post-mortem (KPI versus prediction) and the next brief.

## Phase 3 — Method and Technique

- The six-lever audit scorecard (0–5 per lever, dominant lever named)
- Weighting matrix: audience × platform × goal
- The content-engine pipeline (intake, lever assignment, measurement plan, follow-up, post-mortem)
- KPI mapping per lever (currency → share rate, triggers → cued recall, emotion → arousal pretest, utility → forwarding rate, visibility → uptake, story → transmission)
- The post-mortem template: prediction versus result, per lever

## Phase 4 — Analysis and Diagnosis

- Diagnose a failing portfolio by lever profile: which lever was absent, which was over-claimed.
- Detect the all-six asset and the one-lever monoculture, and prescribe the correction.
- Verify the weighting: the dominant lever should match the audience's sharing identity, not the brand's preference.
- Read the post-mortem honestly: the KPI that missed identifies the lever that was mis-scored, not the audience's failure.

## Phase 5 — Application and Design

- Run every brief through the audit before production — no asset enters the pipeline without a named dominant lever.
- Set the KPI per lever before spend, and the post-mortem rule: what result justifies repeating the play.
- Build the portfolio mix: a quarter's calendar balanced across levers, not stacked on the brand's favorite.
- Feed the post-mortems back into the weighting matrix — the system learns its own audience.

## Phase 6 — Ethics and Boundary Conditions

- Lever stacking shades into over-engineering: a campaign optimized for six levers is a campaign that pleases none.
- KPI gaming (optimizing for the metric, not the motive) reproduces the manipulation the individual-lever ethics rules forbid.
- The system is only as honest as its post-mortems; a pipeline that never records a miss stops learning.

## Phase 7 — Exercises and Assessment

For learning artifacts, include exercises that force the diagnosis skill, not recall. Use the standard set:

1. Audit a portfolio of ten assets with the six-lever scorecard; produce the dominant-lever map and the two biggest gaps.
2. Build the weighting matrix for a defined audience, platform, and goal; defend the dominant-lever choice with the audience evidence.
3. Design the content-engine pipeline: intake criteria, lever assignment, KPI mapping, follow-up rule, post-mortem template.
4. Plan a quarter's calendar balanced across levers, with each asset's lever, KPI, and follow-up named.
5. Run the full synthesis on one campaign: brief → production decisions → measurement plan → a simulated post-mortem against the prediction.
6. Write the system's learning rule: how post-mortem results change next quarter's weighting, with one worked example of a missed KPI.

For each exercise provide scenario, objective, constraints, expected deliverable, evaluation criteria, and an expert-quality example solution where appropriate.

## Phase 8 — Quality Gate and Delivery

Before delivering, run the delivery checklists. The artifact fails the gate on any “no.”

### Audit completion
- Every asset has a six-lever score
- The dominant lever is named
- The all-six and the zero-lever assets are flagged

### Weighting decision
- Audience, platform, and goal are fixed
- The dominant lever matches the audience's sharing identity
- The portfolio mix is balanced, not stacked

### Engine operation
- KPI is set per asset before spend
- The follow-up asset is defined
- The post-mortem is scheduled

### Delivery gate
- The post-mortem compares prediction to result
- The weighting matrix was updated from the results
- Next quarter's calendar is drafted

## Anti-Patterns

the all-six asset; one-lever monoculture; KPI optimization without motive; post-mortems that never record a miss; weighting by brand preference; the audit as a formality.

## Decision Heuristic

Before any claim or recommendation, ask:

1. What is the dominant lever, and does it match who is actually sharing?
2. Which lever is this asset pretending to be that it is not?
3. Is the KPI the lever's KPI, set before spend?
4. What did the last post-mortem change, and is it visible in this brief?
5. If this portfolio ran for a year, what would the mix tell about the audience?

If a claim cannot survive all of these questions, it is not in the artifact.

## Final Principle

The goal is not a viral hit. It is a system that knows, for every asset it ships, which lever is doing the work, what it predicted, and what it learned — so that transmission becomes a practice instead of a prayer.
