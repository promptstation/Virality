# Course 01 — The STEPPS Framework (Researched Curriculum Outline)

Source: `uploads/Virality.txt`, entry 1. Section count: 8 (compressed to ≤15). Progression: beginner → expert.

**Foundations of Social Transmission: Why People Share**

**Summary:** The ground floor of the STEPPS course: what sharing is, why it matters, and how virality actually works. People share content for social reasons — to look good, to help, to feel something, to stay connected — not because the content is 'viral' by decree. The section distinguishes content-driven spread (the message itself does the work) from context-driven spread (placement, timing, and promotion do the work), and introduces the measurement of social transmission (shares, reach, and velocity) as the dependent variable for everything that follows.

**Absorbed Skill:** Explain the social motives that drive sharing and distinguish content-driven from context-driven spread; Define and measure social transmission outcomes (shares, reach, velocity) so later levers can be evaluated against them.

**Social Currency: Making Content Worth Sharing**

**Summary:** The first STEPPS lever: people share what makes them look good. Social currency is the conversational status a piece of content confers on its sharer — insider access, exclusivity, cleverness, or sophistication. The section covers conversational fit (what people talk about becomes what people share), the design of insider knowledge, aspirational content, and data insights, and why content that is impressive to the audience but not to the sharer fails. It draws on the field evidence that people prefer to share information that enhances their image, and on the classic finding that exclusive, hard-to-get information is disproportionately shared.

**Absorbed Skill:** Design content that confers status, insider access, or exclusivity on the sharer; Diagnose why content fails to elevate the sharer and redesign it for conversational fit.

**Triggers: Building Persistent Top-of-Mind Cues**

**Summary:** The second STEPPS lever: environmental cues — triggers — that bring a brand, product, or idea to mind and prompt action. The section covers how triggers work (a cue activates an association; the association must be strong and current), how to find triggers in a category's daily life, how to build the association through repeated pairing, and why trigger power decays as the association fades without reinforcement. It draws on the field evidence that products mentioned in frequently shared content benefit from cue exposure, and on the classic cue-exposure experiments, and it distinguishes fleeting triggers (news, holidays) from persistent ones (daily routines).

**Absorbed Skill:** Identify the strongest environmental triggers for a category and build repeated cue–brand pairings around them; Measure trigger persistence and design reinforcement so the association does not decay.

**Emotion and Arousal as a Lever**

**Summary:** The third STEPPS lever, and the bridge to Course 2: high-arousal emotions drive sharing. The section teaches the core finding (content evoking awe, anger, anxiety, and amusement is more shared; low-arousal contentment and sadness suppress transmission), how to choose the target emotion for a content project, and how to write and direct for arousal — stakes, specificity, sensory detail, urgency — without crossing into manipulation. It covers the valence boundary (positive high-arousal is brand-safe; negative high-arousal is powerful and risky) and the measurement of evoked emotion (self-report, text coding) as the lever's KPI.

**Absorbed Skill:** Select a target high-arousal emotion for a content project and direct the creative to evoke it; Measure evoked emotion and validate that the arousal design produced the intended share lift.

**Practical Value: Content People Forward to Help Others**

**Summary:** The fourth STEPPS lever: useful content is shared to help specific people. The section covers the research showing that content perceived as practically useful is more likely to be shared — and that the usefulness drives forwarding to close others (family, friends) rather than broadcasting — how to design genuinely useful content (how-to, tools, templates, calculators, checklists), the difference between utility for the reader and utility worth forwarding, and why 'value content' is the most underrated transmission lever for B2B and professional audiences.

**Absorbed Skill:** Design content whose practical value is high enough to be forwarded to specific people; Diagnose utility-deficit content and redesign it around a concrete, nameable use case.

**Public, Private, and Persuasion: What Is Visible Shapes What Happens**

**Summary:** The fifth STEPPS lever: the visibility of behavior drives behavior. The section teaches the distinction between loud (public, visible) and subtle (private, contextual) influence, the research showing that making a behavior or product visible increases its uptake — public displays, defaults, and ambient cues — and how to apply it: making usage visible, designing default choices, using environmental cues, and understanding when subtlety (the unobtrusive nudge) is more effective than visibility. It covers the boundary: public pressure works on observable behaviors and backfires on identity-sensitive ones.

**Absorbed Skill:** Design for the visibility of behavior — public displays, defaults, ambient cues — to increase adoption and sharing; Choose between loud (public) and subtle (private) influence tactics based on the behavior's sensitivity.

**Stories: Story Wrapping and Narrative Contagion**

**Summary:** The sixth and final STEPPS lever: ideas are contagious when wrapped in stories. The section teaches the concept of story wrapping — embedding the message, product, or call-to-action inside a narrative that is shared for the story, not for the pitch — why narrative transport makes audiences receptive, the structure of a story that carries (situation, character, tension, turn, resolution), and how to evaluate whether a story is doing the wrapping or merely decorating. It covers the research showing that stories increase transmission of the embedded idea, the difference between story and anecdote, and the craft of the embedded product reveal.

**Absorbed Skill:** Wrap a message or product inside a transportive story structure that is shared for the narrative; Evaluate story-wrap quality — whether the story carries the message or merely contains it.

**Integrated STEPPS Strategy: Auditing and Orchestrating the Six Levers**

**Summary:** The capstone: using all six levers as a system. The section teaches the STEPPS audit — scoring existing and proposed content across social currency, triggers, emotion, public/subtle, practical value, and story — how to weight the levers by audience, platform, and goal, how to build a content engine (a pipeline that assigns each asset a primary lever, a measurement plan, and a follow-up), and the synthesis of the six levers in a complete campaign, from brief to post-mortem.

**Absorbed Skill:** Audit content across the six STEPPS levers and prescribe the dominant lever for each asset; Build and run a STEPPS content engine: pipeline, weighting, measurement, and post-mortem.
