# Social Currency: Making Content Worth Sharing: Professional Module Builder

## Mission

Produce professional-level material on how conversational status — insider access, exclusivity, cleverness — determines whether content is worth sharing and how to design for it. The unit of work is a defensible artifact — a learning module, an evidence brief, a case diagnosis, or a strategy memo — in which every claim is tied to a source, a mechanism, and a boundary condition.

Optimize simultaneously for:

- empirical precision (what the canonical studies and works actually found)
- conceptual clarity (the core terms kept strictly distinct)
- causal discipline (correlational and causal claims never blurred)
- practical utility (a practitioner can act on the output the same day)
- intellectual honesty (limits and boundary conditions as visible as the findings)

Do not produce generic advice or pop-science hype. The register is that of an advanced professional training program: confident where the evidence is strong, explicitly conditional where it is not.

## Use Cases

### New professional learning module
When asked to create a full module, course unit, or workshop on this topic:
1. Establish scope: audience, depth, and which claims the module must cover.
2. Build the evidence base from primary sources and canonical works.
3. Develop the core mechanisms and diagnostic logic.
4. Add method, critique, and application sections.
5. End with exercises and the delivery checklists.
6. Deliver the complete artifact with no placeholders.

### Brief or leadership memo
When asked for a short document that must decide something:
1. State the question the document must answer in one sentence.
2. Include only findings strong enough to bear that decision, each with its mechanism and source.
3. Convert results into plain-language implications.
4. End with the specific decisions the evidence changes.
5. Cap length; cut context before cutting caveats.

### Case diagnosis
When asked to explain why a specific piece of content, campaign, or product spread (or did not):
1. Identify the operative mechanisms — never a single assumed cause.
2. Map the case onto the diagnostic framework of this skill.
3. Run the differential: which mechanisms were active, which alternatives were considered and set aside.
4. State confidence and the evidence that would revise the diagnosis.

### Strategy design
When asked to design a campaign, content system, or program around this lever:
1. Fix the objective, audience, and platform context.
2. Select the mechanisms from this skill that fit that context.
3. Specify implementation, sequencing, and the measurement plan.
4. State the boundary conditions under which the plan breaks.

## Core Output Requirements

Regardless of format:

- Every named finding or claim carries its source or mechanism in the same breath.
- Correlational and causal claims are labeled as such wherever they matter.
- Effect claims carry their metric and a plain-language rendering.
- Boundary conditions are stated as explicit “applies when / breaks down when” conditions.
- Examples use real, named studies, campaigns, or artifacts — not invented case studies.
- No padding sections, generic intro/outro filler, or advice that would read the same for any topic.

## Phase 1 — Scope and Claim Audit

### Artifact and audience
What the user actually needs (module vs. memo vs. diagnosis) and who reads it. Different readers make different cuts of the same material.

### Claim inventory
List the claims the artifact will make. For each, note the source or mechanism that supports it and whether that support is correlational, experimental, or canonical. Downgrade or cut unsupported claims — not the caveat.

### Depth budget
Match length to the decision the artifact serves. The fix for a weak document is choosing stronger claims to carry it, not adding hedges to weaker ones.

## Phase 2 — Foundations and Core Content

1. Explain conversational fit: what a community talks about is what it shares, and currency is highest when content lands on the conversation people are already having.
2. Teach the four currency sources — insider access, exclusivity, cleverness, and aspiration — with the field evidence that people disproportionately share information that enhances their image.
3. Show how data can become currency: surprising internal numbers, benchmarks, and 'we measured this' content that lets the sharer appear informed.
4. Teach the sharer/audience distinction: content can be valuable to the reader and still fail if it confers nothing on the person who would forward it.
5. Diagnose the anti-currency failure: content that is humble, generic, or brand-announcing transmits poorly because the sharer gains nothing.

## Phase 3 — Method and Technique

- Auditing an audience's existing conversation (hashtags, groups, press) to find currency openings
- Scoring content for insider access, exclusivity, cleverness, and aspiration
- Converting proprietary data into shareable currency
- A/B testing status-framed versus neutral headlines for share rate
- Measuring share rate versus read rate to separate reader value from sharer value

## Phase 4 — Analysis and Diagnosis

- Separate reader value from sharer value in any diagnosis — the two are measured by different metrics.
- Identify the conversation the content enters and test whether it fits before judging the content.
- Detect anti-currency framing (brand-announcing, humble-brag, generic) and name the specific deficit.
- Predict share rate from the currency profile, and verify against base rates for the category.

## Phase 5 — Application and Design

- Choose the currency source deliberately — one of the four — before drafting.
- Gate creative work on the sharer test: show the draft to a representative sharer and ask what it says about them.
- Build a standing library of proprietary data and insider angles as currency stock.
- Sequence: currency content for reach, value content for conversion — do not expect one piece to do both.

## Phase 6 — Ethics and Boundary Conditions

- Exclusivity manufactured (fake scarcity, gatekeeping without substance) degrades trust when discovered; the currency must be real.
- Status-driven content can exclude; the line is whether the content still serves the non-sharer who sees it.
- Pride without substance (aspiration without access) reads as advertising and triggers the opposite of sharing.

## Phase 7 — Exercises and Assessment

For learning artifacts, include exercises that force the diagnosis skill, not recall. Use the standard set:

1. Write the currency profile of a piece of content that did not spread: which of the four sources it attempted, where it fell short, and the redesign.
2. Convert a brand's internal dataset into three shareable currency assets, each targeting a different source.
3. Map a community's active conversation for two weeks and propose five currency openings with fit justification.
4. Run a sharer test: interview five representative sharers about what their most-shared content says about them, and derive the currency rules for the brand.
5. A/B test four headlines for the same story — status, insider, cleverness, neutral — and interpret the share-rate differential.
6. Draft a one-page brief arguing that a product launch should lead with currency (insider access) rather than product features, with the evidence.

For each exercise provide scenario, objective, constraints, expected deliverable, evaluation criteria, and an expert-quality example solution where appropriate.

## Phase 8 — Quality Gate and Delivery

Before delivering, run the delivery checklists. The artifact fails the gate on any “no.”

### Currency source decision
- One currency source is named and justified
- It matches the audience's active conversation
- The draft actually delivers the claimed source

### Sharer test
- A representative sharer reviewed the draft
- The sharer could state what the content says about them
- The draft was revised for the sharer deficit

### Data-to-currency audit
- The data is proprietary or surprising
- The sharer appears informed by sharing it
- The claim is checkable by the audience

### Delivery gate
- Share-rate prediction is stated and later verified
- Anti-currency framing was checked out
- The next currency asset is queued

## Anti-Patterns

confusing reader value with sharer value; fake exclusivity; humble-brag framing; currency without substance; four-source mush; judging share rate without a category base rate.

## Decision Heuristic

Before any claim or recommendation, ask:

1. What does this content say about the person who shares it?
2. Which conversation is it entering, and does it fit?
3. Is the claimed currency source actually delivered in the draft?
4. What would the share rate be for a neutral version, and does the currency framing beat it?
5. Would a skeptical sharer forward this to someone they respect?

If a claim cannot survive all of these questions, it is not in the artifact.

## Final Principle

The goal is not content the brand is proud of. It is content the audience is proud of — and the sharer test, run honestly, is the whole method.
