# Step 2 — Generated Prompt (Result of the Reusable Prompt)

```
Develop a comprehensive, professional-level learning module on **“Synthesis: Network Strategy for Virality”** as part of an advanced course on the structure of social networks and how that structure determines who spreads what, where, and how fast. The module should how the network's architecture — its shape, its communities, its verified spreaders — becomes the strategy: the play, the seeds, the timing, and the structural measurement.

The material should teach the integration layer: the atlas to the play, the verified list to the budget, the dynamics to the timing, and the audit to the standing asset. Go beyond the campaign planned on demographics and hopes, with the network as a backdrop and examine how the strategy's success is measured in structural terms (the crossing, the community, the tail) as well as in reach terms.

Teach the strategy sequence from the atlas: read the network (the shape, the communities, the bridges, the tail), choose the play (the community play — win the cluster; the crossing play — own the bridge or the hole-holder; the hub ignition — light the tail and let the percolation do the rest), and let the play choose the seed design and the measurement.

Design the seed set from the verified list: the role of each seed in the play (the ignition seed on the hub, the bridge seed on the crossing, the sustain seed inside the community), the budget allocation with the risk bound, and the sequence (the ignition first, the crossing on its window, the sustain after the crossing holds).

Time against the network's dynamics: the activity windows of the seed nodes, the community rhythms (the community's peak hours, the recurring topics), and the global windows (the platform's traffic peaks, the competing events) — the timing is a network property, not a calendar property.

Measure the structural outcome: the crossing achieved (the content reached the target communities, not just the home one), the community won (the inside-loop density and the sustained engagement), and the tail captured (the share of the propagation carried by the verified tail) — alongside the reach metrics, as the pair that tells the full story.

Run the post-campaign structural audit: the adoption graph versus the pre-campaign atlas (which communities were crossed, which bridges carried, which tail nodes fired), the update to the versioned graph (the new edges, the changed densities), and the lesson to the next report (the play's hit rate, the seed list's verification update, the timing's accuracy).

Include practical methods for working at a professional level:
* The atlas read: the one-page structural summary that frames the play choice
* The play decision record: the plays considered, the chosen one, the rejected ones with their reasons
* The seed design sheet: the role per seed, the budget, the sequence, the risk bound
* The timing plan: the windows per layer, the competing events, the go/no-go checkpoints
* The structural-audit protocol: the adoption graph, the atlas diff, the tail accounting, the lesson record

Include practical exercises that require the learner to:
1. Produce the atlas read for a target network and the play decision record: the plays considered, the choice, the rejections.
2. Design the seed sheet for the chosen play: the role per seed, the budget with the risk bound, the sequence with the checkpoints.
3. Build the timing plan: the windows per layer, the competing events, and the go/no-go logic per checkpoint.
4. Design the structural measurement: the crossing metric, the community-sustain metric, the tail accounting — each with its data source and its threshold.
5. Run the post-campaign structural audit on a finished campaign: the adoption graph, the atlas diff, the tail hit rate, and the lesson record.
6. Write the handoff to the next campaign: the updated asset, the updated verification list, the open questions — and the first decision the next read faces.

For each exercise, provide the scenario, objective, constraints, expected deliverable, evaluation criteria, and an expert-quality example solution where appropriate.

Structure the material progressively, beginning with the atlas read and the play choice, moving through the seed design, the timing, and the structural measurement, then to the audit, the update, and the compounding handoff. Establish clear conceptual distinctions between **play, seed role, window, and structural outcome** before showing how they interact.

Use professional terminology from graph theory, network science, and social network analysis where relevant, but explain specialized terminology in clear language. Do not make the material sound like generic AI-generated advice; the register should be that of an advanced professional training module used by growth analysts, social researchers, and data scientists working with network data.

Research the subject using high-quality professional and academic sources where external research materially strengthens the material. Prioritize authoritative sources: the network-science literature (Watts and Strogatz 1998, Barabási and Albert 1999, the Watts small-world and Barabási scale-free papers), the centrality and community-detection literature, the diffusion-on-networks models (independent cascade, linear threshold), and the standard network-analysis toolkits. Avoid relying primarily on low-quality SEO articles, generic graph theory networks blogs, or unsupported “best practice” claims. Where useful, distinguish established research findings from professional conventions and informed recommendations.

Pay particular attention to the relationship between this module and principles such as **the atlas as the start, the play as the logic, the structural pair as the truth, and the audit as the compounding**. Connect these concepts to concrete analytical and design decisions rather than discussing them only theoretically.

Create a professional framework that growth analysts, social researchers, and data scientists working with network data could actually use in a real project. The final material should therefore function simultaneously as a learning resource, a practical reference, and a working methodology for targeting, seeding, and analyzing spread on social networks.

Throughout the module, prioritize language that is:

* Precise without being jargon-heavy
* Empirical without being statistical theater
* Confident about what the evidence establishes
* Explicit about what the evidence does not establish
* Conditional where the findings are conditional
* Practical where the decisions are practical

Where a claim depends on context (platform, audience, topic, measurement), explicitly explain the trade-off and the boundary condition rather than presenting it as an absolute rule.

End the module with **Strategy record checklist**, **Seed and timing checklist**, **Measurement pair checklist**, and a **Delivery gate checklist** that a professional team can use before acting on targeting, seeding, and analyzing spread on social networks.

The final result should be comprehensive enough to serve as an advanced professional training module, but organized clearly enough that a learner can study it progressively and apply each concept in targeting, seeding, and analyzing spread on social networks.
```
