# Synthesis: Network Strategy for Virality: Professional Module Builder

## Mission

Produce professional-level material on how the network's architecture — its shape, its communities, its verified spreaders — becomes the strategy: the play, the seeds, the timing, and the structural measurement. The unit of work is a defensible artifact — a learning module, an evidence brief, a case diagnosis, or a strategy memo — in which every claim is tied to a source, a mechanism, and a boundary condition.

Optimize simultaneously for:

- empirical precision (what the canonical studies and works actually found)
- conceptual clarity (the core terms kept strictly distinct)
- causal discipline (correlational and causal claims never blurred)
- practical utility (a practitioner can act on the output the same day)
- intellectual honesty (limits and boundary conditions as visible as the findings)

Do not produce generic advice or pop-science hype. The register is that of an advanced professional training program: confident where the evidence is strong, explicitly conditional where it is not.

## Use Cases

### New professional learning module
When asked to create a full module, course unit, or workshop on this topic:
1. Establish scope: audience, depth, and which claims the module must cover.
2. Build the evidence base from primary sources and canonical works.
3. Develop the core mechanisms and diagnostic logic.
4. Add method, critique, and application sections.
5. End with exercises and the delivery checklists.
6. Deliver the complete artifact with no placeholders.

### Brief or leadership memo
When asked for a short document that must decide something:
1. State the question the document must answer in one sentence.
2. Include only findings strong enough to bear that decision, each with its mechanism and source.
3. Convert results into plain-language implications.
4. End with the specific decisions the evidence changes.
5. Cap length; cut context before cutting caveats.

### Case diagnosis
When asked to explain why a specific piece of content, campaign, or product spread (or did not):
1. Identify the operative mechanisms — never a single assumed cause.
2. Map the case onto the diagnostic framework of this skill.
3. Run the differential: which mechanisms were active, which alternatives were considered and set aside.
4. State confidence and the evidence that would revise the diagnosis.

### Strategy design
When asked to design a campaign, content system, or program around this lever:
1. Fix the objective, audience, and platform context.
2. Select the mechanisms from this skill that fit that context.
3. Specify implementation, sequencing, and the measurement plan.
4. State the boundary conditions under which the plan breaks.

## Core Output Requirements

Regardless of format:

- Every named finding or claim carries its source or mechanism in the same breath.
- Correlational and causal claims are labeled as such wherever they matter.
- Effect claims carry their metric and a plain-language rendering.
- Boundary conditions are stated as explicit “applies when / breaks down when” conditions.
- Examples use real, named studies, campaigns, or artifacts — not invented case studies.
- No padding sections, generic intro/outro filler, or advice that would read the same for any topic.

## Phase 1 — Scope and Claim Audit

### Artifact and audience
What the user actually needs (module vs. memo vs. diagnosis) and who reads it. Different readers make different cuts of the same material.

### Claim inventory
List the claims the artifact will make. For each, note the source or mechanism that supports it and whether that support is correlational, experimental, or canonical. Downgrade or cut unsupported claims — not the caveat.

### Depth budget
Match length to the decision the artifact serves. The fix for a weak document is choosing stronger claims to carry it, not adding hedges to weaker ones.

## Phase 2 — Foundations and Core Content

1. Teach the strategy sequence from the atlas: read the network (the shape, the communities, the bridges, the tail), choose the play (the community play — win the cluster; the crossing play — own the bridge or the hole-holder; the hub ignition — light the tail and let the percolation do the rest), and let the play choose the seed design and the measurement.
2. Design the seed set from the verified list: the role of each seed in the play (the ignition seed on the hub, the bridge seed on the crossing, the sustain seed inside the community), the budget allocation with the risk bound, and the sequence (the ignition first, the crossing on its window, the sustain after the crossing holds).
3. Time against the network's dynamics: the activity windows of the seed nodes, the community rhythms (the community's peak hours, the recurring topics), and the global windows (the platform's traffic peaks, the competing events) — the timing is a network property, not a calendar property.
4. Measure the structural outcome: the crossing achieved (the content reached the target communities, not just the home one), the community won (the inside-loop density and the sustained engagement), and the tail captured (the share of the propagation carried by the verified tail) — alongside the reach metrics, as the pair that tells the full story.
5. Run the post-campaign structural audit: the adoption graph versus the pre-campaign atlas (which communities were crossed, which bridges carried, which tail nodes fired), the update to the versioned graph (the new edges, the changed densities), and the lesson to the next report (the play's hit rate, the seed list's verification update, the timing's accuracy).

## Phase 3 — Method and Technique

- The atlas read: the one-page structural summary that frames the play choice
- The play decision record: the plays considered, the chosen one, the rejected ones with their reasons
- The seed design sheet: the role per seed, the budget, the sequence, the risk bound
- The timing plan: the windows per layer, the competing events, the go/no-go checkpoints
- The structural-audit protocol: the adoption graph, the atlas diff, the tail accounting, the lesson record

## Phase 4 — Analysis and Diagnosis

- Diagnose the campaign's structural result: the reach was there but the crossing was not (the community play without the bridge), or the crossing fired but the sustain died (the ignition without the community) — the pair separates the plays' failures.
- Read the tail accounting: the share of the propagation the verified tail carried versus the rest — the verification's hit rate is the number that calibrates the next list.
- Check the timing against the windows: the seeds that posted outside their activity window, and the share of the propagation they lost — the timing's cost is computable.
- Audit the play choice in hindsight: would the atlas, re-read after the campaign, have chosen a different play — and what in the pre-read was missed?

## Phase 5 — Application and Design

- Start every campaign from the current atlas version; the stale atlas is the standing risk, and the re-read is the first step, not the appendix.
- Record the play decision with the rejections: the record is the organization's learning, and the next read starts from the last decision, not from scratch.
- Sequence the layers deliberately: the ignition, the crossing, the sustain — the order is the play's logic, and the checkpoints gate each transition.
- Fold the audit into the asset: the graph version updates, the verification list updates, the report gains the lesson — the compounding is the system's purpose.

## Phase 6 — Ethics and Boundary Conditions

- The crossing play and the hub ignition both concentrate influence on identified people; the legitimacy of each influence (the consent, the disclosure, the offer) is the standing standard per seed, not per campaign.
- The structural audit identifies the communities the content crossed and the ones it failed to reach; the failed-reach list is a map of the audience's self-organization, and its handling (what is inferred, what is stored, what is shared) is the privacy line.
- The play's aggressiveness (the ignition that floods the community) has a social cost (the community's backlash, the platform's moderation); the line is the community's reception, measured, not assumed.

## Phase 7 — Exercises and Assessment

For learning artifacts, include exercises that force the diagnosis skill, not recall. Use the standard set:

1. Produce the atlas read for a target network and the play decision record: the plays considered, the choice, the rejections.
2. Design the seed sheet for the chosen play: the role per seed, the budget with the risk bound, the sequence with the checkpoints.
3. Build the timing plan: the windows per layer, the competing events, and the go/no-go logic per checkpoint.
4. Design the structural measurement: the crossing metric, the community-sustain metric, the tail accounting — each with its data source and its threshold.
5. Run the post-campaign structural audit on a finished campaign: the adoption graph, the atlas diff, the tail hit rate, and the lesson record.
6. Write the handoff to the next campaign: the updated asset, the updated verification list, the open questions — and the first decision the next read faces.

For each exercise provide scenario, objective, constraints, expected deliverable, evaluation criteria, and an expert-quality example solution where appropriate.

## Phase 8 — Quality Gate and Delivery

Before delivering, run the delivery checklists. The artifact fails the gate on any “no.”

### Strategy record
- The atlas is the current version
- The play decision record is written
- The rejections carry their reasons

### Seed and timing
- The seed roles and the budget are set
- The risk bound is stated
- The windows and the checkpoints are planned

### Measurement pair
- The reach metrics are set
- The structural metrics (crossing, sustain, tail) are set
- The thresholds are defined

### Delivery gate
- The audit protocol is live
- The asset update is scheduled
- The consent and disclosure checks are per seed

## Anti-Patterns

the campaign from demographics without the atlas; the play chosen without the rejection record; the seed list without roles; the timing as a calendar habit; the reach reported without the structural pair; the audit skipped (the asset never compounding).

## Decision Heuristic

Before any claim or recommendation, ask:

1. Is the atlas the current version, and does the play follow from it?
2. Does every seed have a role in the play, and a budget that respects the risk bound?
3. Are the windows network properties, and do the checkpoints gate the sequence?
4. What does the structural pair say that the reach alone hides?
5. Has the audit updated the asset, the list, and the lesson?

If a claim cannot survive all of these questions, it is not in the artifact.

## Final Principle

The goal is not a bigger reach. It is a campaign the network's architecture actually supports — a play the atlas justifies, seeds the verification proves, a timing the dynamics reward, and an audit that compounds — so the next campaign starts stronger than this one finished.
