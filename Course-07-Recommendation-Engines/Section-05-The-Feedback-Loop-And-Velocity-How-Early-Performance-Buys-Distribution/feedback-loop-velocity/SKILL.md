# The Feedback Loop and Velocity: How Early Performance Buys Distribution: Professional Module Builder

## Mission

Produce professional-level material on how the early performance buys the distribution — the test-and-expand, the velocity, the bandit logic, and the loop's dynamics — and how to design for the first window. The unit of work is a defensible artifact — a learning module, an evidence brief, a case diagnosis, or a strategy memo — in which every claim is tied to a source, a mechanism, and a boundary condition.

Optimize simultaneously for:

- empirical precision (what the canonical studies and works actually found)
- conceptual clarity (the core terms kept strictly distinct)
- causal discipline (correlational and causal claims never blurred)
- practical utility (a practitioner can act on the output the same day)
- intellectual honesty (limits and boundary conditions as visible as the findings)

Do not produce generic advice or pop-science hype. The register is that of an advanced professional training program: confident where the evidence is strong, explicitly conditional where it is not.

## Use Cases

### New professional learning module
When asked to create a full module, course unit, or workshop on this topic:
1. Establish scope: audience, depth, and which claims the module must cover.
2. Build the evidence base from primary sources and canonical works.
3. Develop the core mechanisms and diagnostic logic.
4. Add method, critique, and application sections.
5. End with exercises and the delivery checklists.
6. Deliver the complete artifact with no placeholders.

### Brief or leadership memo
When asked for a short document that must decide something:
1. State the question the document must answer in one sentence.
2. Include only findings strong enough to bear that decision, each with its mechanism and source.
3. Convert results into plain-language implications.
4. End with the specific decisions the evidence changes.
5. Cap length; cut context before cutting caveats.

### Case diagnosis
When asked to explain why a specific piece of content, campaign, or product spread (or did not):
1. Identify the operative mechanisms — never a single assumed cause.
2. Map the case onto the diagnostic framework of this skill.
3. Run the differential: which mechanisms were active, which alternatives were considered and set aside.
4. State confidence and the evidence that would revise the diagnosis.

### Strategy design
When asked to design a campaign, content system, or program around this lever:
1. Fix the objective, audience, and platform context.
2. Select the mechanisms from this skill that fit that context.
3. Specify implementation, sequencing, and the measurement plan.
4. State the boundary conditions under which the plan breaks.

## Core Output Requirements

Regardless of format:

- Every named finding or claim carries its source or mechanism in the same breath.
- Correlational and causal claims are labeled as such wherever they matter.
- Effect claims carry their metric and a plain-language rendering.
- Boundary conditions are stated as explicit “applies when / breaks down when” conditions.
- Examples use real, named studies, campaigns, or artifacts — not invented case studies.
- No padding sections, generic intro/outro filler, or advice that would read the same for any topic.

## Phase 1 — Scope and Claim Audit

### Artifact and audience
What the user actually needs (module vs. memo vs. diagnosis) and who reads it. Different readers make different cuts of the same material.

### Claim inventory
List the claims the artifact will make. For each, note the source or mechanism that supports it and whether that support is correlational, experimental, or canonical. Downgrade or cut unsupported claims — not the caveat.

### Depth budget
Match length to the decision the artifact serves. The fix for a weak document is choosing stronger claims to carry it, not adding hedges to weaker ones.

## Phase 2 — Foundations and Core Content

1. Teach the two-stage test: the content's seed distribution (the small audience — the follows, the interest pool, the exploration slice), the performance read (the early metrics against the seed's expected), and the expansion decision (the promotion to the larger pool, the halt) — the documented pattern across the short-video platforms, and the stages' dynamics.
2. Teach the velocity metrics: the early engagement per unit of the seed (the rate, not the count — the normalized signal), the completion in the first window, the re-watch, the share, and the early comment — the metrics the read uses, the window per platform, and the normalization that makes the signal comparable.
3. Teach the bandit logic: the exploration-exploitation problem (the allocation across the candidates — the known good versus the untested), the formal models (the UCB, the Thompson sampling — the confidence and the posterior as the allocation's logic), and the platform's informal version (the exploration slice, the test pool) — the logic, and the strategy's read of it (the new content's exploration budget, the decaying with the age).
4. Cover the loop's dynamics: the amplification (the strong content's expanding reach, the engagement that funds the next expansion), the starvation (the weak content's halt, the seed's exhaustion without the expansion), and the instability (the feedback's runaway — the viral spike, the correction — and the quality layer's check on the runaway) — the three dynamics, the evidence, and the platform's stabilizers.
5. Teach the first window as the auction: the content's early design — the hook (the first seconds' holding power), the completion design (the structure that earns the finish), the early engagement seed (the first comments' prompt, the reply's presence), and the posting context (the time, the series' position) — the entry designed against the read's metrics, and the window's honesty (the first window's signal, not the verdict — the correction, the late bloom, the quality layer).

## Phase 3 — Method and Technique

- The two-stage map per platform: the seed's composition, the read's metrics, the expansion's trigger, the documented status
- The velocity computation: the per-seed normalization, the window, the comparable index
- The bandit read: the exploration allocation's inference, the age decay, the content's exploration budget
- The loop diagnostics: the amplification's signature, the starvation's signature, the runaway's signature, in the reach data
- The first-window spec: the hook, the completion, the seed prompt, the context — with the design rule per element

## Phase 4 — Analysis and Diagnosis

- Diagnose the reach outcome by the loop's dynamic: the amplification that arrived (the expansion's trigger met), the starvation (the read missed, the halt), the flatline (the signal absent — the seed exhausted without the read) — the dynamic the data shows.
- Read the velocity's normalization: the count without the seed's size is the vanity; the per-seed rate is the signal, and the window's definition is the claim.
- Check the quality layer on the runaway: the viral spike with the negative signal (the engagement the trust refunds) — the loop's correction, and the content's exposure to it.
- Separate the late bloom from the starvation: the content whose signal arrived after the window (the series' effect, the trend's arrival) — the correction the loop's dynamics allow, and its rarity.

## Phase 5 — Application and Design

- Design the first window as the auction entry: the hook against the completion metric, the structure against the finish, the seed prompt against the early comment — the spec per element, from the read's metrics.
- Time the posting to the context: the audience's active window, the series' position, the trend's state — the context's input to the read, controlled.
- Manage the seed deliberately: the follow structure, the community's presence in the first window (the reply, the early comment) — the seed's engagement, designed.
- Read the outcome against the loop's dynamics, not the count: the expansion's state, the quality layer's read, the next content's spec update — the loop as the standing instrument.

## Phase 6 — Ethics and Boundary Conditions

- The early engagement seed (the first comment's prompt) can be the engagement bait; the line is the prompt's value (the question the content answers, not the trap it sets) and the reply's honesty.
- The velocity's auction rewards the early reaction; the strategy that engineers the reaction (the controversy in the first seconds) is the reaction bought with the audience's goodwill — the negative signal is its detector, and the retention its refund.
- The runaway's correction is the platform's design; the strategy's contribution to the runaway (the amplification's fuel) is the share of the instability it owns, and the quality layer's check is its standard.

## Phase 7 — Exercises and Assessment

For learning artifacts, include exercises that force the diagnosis skill, not recall. Use the standard set:

1. Map the two-stage test for two platforms: the seed's composition, the read's metrics, the expansion's trigger — with the documentation status per element.
2. Compute the velocity index for a content set: the per-seed normalization, the window, the comparable ranking — and the diagnosis of the bottom decile.
3. Infer the exploration allocation from a platform's behavior: the new content's seed pattern, the age decay, and the exploration budget's estimate.
4. Run the loop diagnostics on a reach dataset: the amplification, the starvation, the flatline signatures — with the case per dynamic.
5. Write the first-window spec for a content type: the hook, the completion, the seed prompt, the context — with the design rule and the metric per element.
6. Write the ethics note for the auction strategy: the seed prompt's line, the reaction's honesty, the runaway's share — as the standing policy.

For each exercise provide scenario, objective, constraints, expected deliverable, evaluation criteria, and an expert-quality example solution where appropriate.

## Phase 8 — Quality Gate and Delivery

Before delivering, run the delivery checklists. The artifact fails the gate on any “no.”

### Two-stage map
- The seed, the read, and the expansion are documented
- The window is defined
- The normalization is stated

### Velocity
- The per-seed rate is computed
- The comparable index is ranked
- The bottom decile is diagnosed

### First window
- The hook, the completion, and the seed prompt are specified
- The context is timed
- The metrics are per element

### Delivery gate
- The loop diagnostics are run on the outcome
- The quality layer's read is recorded
- The next content's spec is updated

## Anti-Patterns

the count read as the signal; the window undefined; the seed ignored (the post into the void); the first comment's trap; the runaway fuelled without the quality read; the late bloom assumed, not checked.

## Decision Heuristic

Before any claim or recommendation, ask:

1. What is the per-seed rate in the first window, and how does it rank?
2. Which stage of the two-stage test is this content at, and what does the read say?
3. Is the hook earning the completion, and is the completion earning the re-watch?
4. What does the loop's dynamic show — the amplification, the starvation, or the flatline?
5. Does the quality layer's read clear the engagement the velocity reports?

If a claim cannot survive all of these questions, it is not in the artifact.

## Final Principle

The goal is not the viral spike. It is the first window designed against the read, the loop read against its dynamics, and the quality layer held over both — so the distribution the content earns is earned on the signal, not on the seed's luck.
