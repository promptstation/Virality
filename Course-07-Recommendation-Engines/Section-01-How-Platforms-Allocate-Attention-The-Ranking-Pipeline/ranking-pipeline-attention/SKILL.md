# How Platforms Allocate Attention: The Ranking Pipeline: Professional Module Builder

## Mission

Produce professional-level material on how the feed is made — the recall, the ranking, the impression, and the objective — with the documented perimeter as the strategy's ground. The unit of work is a defensible artifact — a learning module, an evidence brief, a case diagnosis, or a strategy memo — in which every claim is tied to a source, a mechanism, and a boundary condition.

Optimize simultaneously for:

- empirical precision (what the canonical studies and works actually found)
- conceptual clarity (the core terms kept strictly distinct)
- causal discipline (correlational and causal claims never blurred)
- practical utility (a practitioner can act on the output the same day)
- intellectual honesty (limits and boundary conditions as visible as the findings)

Do not produce generic advice or pop-science hype. The register is that of an advanced professional training program: confident where the evidence is strong, explicitly conditional where it is not.

## Use Cases

### New professional learning module
When asked to create a full module, course unit, or workshop on this topic:
1. Establish scope: audience, depth, and which claims the module must cover.
2. Build the evidence base from primary sources and canonical works.
3. Develop the core mechanisms and diagnostic logic.
4. Add method, critique, and application sections.
5. End with exercises and the delivery checklists.
6. Deliver the complete artifact with no placeholders.

### Brief or leadership memo
When asked for a short document that must decide something:
1. State the question the document must answer in one sentence.
2. Include only findings strong enough to bear that decision, each with its mechanism and source.
3. Convert results into plain-language implications.
4. End with the specific decisions the evidence changes.
5. Cap length; cut context before cutting caveats.

### Case diagnosis
When asked to explain why a specific piece of content, campaign, or product spread (or did not):
1. Identify the operative mechanisms — never a single assumed cause.
2. Map the case onto the diagnostic framework of this skill.
3. Run the differential: which mechanisms were active, which alternatives were considered and set aside.
4. State confidence and the evidence that would revise the diagnosis.

### Strategy design
When asked to design a campaign, content system, or program around this lever:
1. Fix the objective, audience, and platform context.
2. Select the mechanisms from this skill that fit that context.
3. Specify implementation, sequencing, and the measurement plan.
4. State the boundary conditions under which the plan breaks.

## Core Output Requirements

Regardless of format:

- Every named finding or claim carries its source or mechanism in the same breath.
- Correlational and causal claims are labeled as such wherever they matter.
- Effect claims carry their metric and a plain-language rendering.
- Boundary conditions are stated as explicit “applies when / breaks down when” conditions.
- Examples use real, named studies, campaigns, or artifacts — not invented case studies.
- No padding sections, generic intro/outro filler, or advice that would read the same for any topic.

## Phase 1 — Scope and Claim Audit

### Artifact and audience
What the user actually needs (module vs. memo vs. diagnosis) and who reads it. Different readers make different cuts of the same material.

### Claim inventory
List the claims the artifact will make. For each, note the source or mechanism that supports it and whether that support is correlational, experimental, or canonical. Downgrade or cut unsupported claims — not the caveat.

### Depth budget
Match length to the decision the artifact serves. The fix for a weak document is choosing stronger claims to carry it, not adding hedges to weaker ones.

## Phase 2 — Foundations and Core Content

1. Explain the candidate generation (recall): the universe of candidate content narrowed by the signals the platform has on hand (the follows, the past engagement, the topic tags, the trending pool, the social graph) — the stage that decides what is eligible, and the failure mode (the content that is never a candidate is never ranked).
2. Explain the ranking: the model that scores each candidate for this user at this moment — the predicted engagement (the watch, the click, the like, the completion), the user's history, the content's features, the context (the time, the device, the session) — the stage that decides the order, and the evidence for its inputs (the documented signals, the learned papers' analogs).
3. Explain the impression: the final selection from the ranked list with the constraints — the diversity (the topic spread, the source spread), the freshness (the new content's allocation), the policy (the safety, the quality filters), and the strategic slots (the promoted content, the follow-feed blend) — the stage that decides what actually appears.
4. Teach the objective: the platform's optimized quantity (the predicted user value in the platform's definition — the watch time, the engagement, the return) and the tension in the objective (the user's stated preference versus the measured behavior, the short-term engagement versus the long-term value, the platform's documented welfare goals) — the objective is the variable, and the strategy is the alignment with it.
5. Frame the known versus the inferred: the documented signals (the platforms' help pages, the transparency reports, the creator guidance), the academic reconstructions (the published studies of the feed's behavior), and the creator evidence (the measured A/B results) — the three layers, and the rule that the strategy is built on the first two, tested with the third, and never on the rumor.

## Phase 3 — Method and Technique

- The pipeline diagram for a target platform: the stages, the documented signals per stage, the inferred ones marked
- The objective read: the platform's documented value definition, the tension notes, the strategy alignment
- The eligibility check: is the content a candidate (the tags, the follows, the topic pool), or is it outside the recall
- The documented-perimeter inventory: the help pages, the transparency materials, the creator guidance — the citable list
- The creator-evidence protocol: the A/B design that tests a pipeline hypothesis on the real account

## Phase 4 — Analysis and Diagnosis

- Diagnose the reach failure by stage: the eligibility (never a candidate), the ranking (a candidate, not scored high), the impression (ranked, filtered out) — the stage the evidence isolates.
- Read the objective's tension in a case: the content the audience wants versus the content the objective rewards — the gap is the strategy's space, and the platform's welfare goals are the direction of travel.
- Check the inference's status: a pipeline claim used in the strategy without its documentation or evidence status is a rumor with a plan attached.
- Separate the platform's documented change (the stated algorithm update) from the community's interpretation of it — the change log is the citable record.

## Phase 5 — Application and Design

- Build the strategy on the documented perimeter: the signals the platform states it uses are the levers with a citation, and the rest are hypotheses to test.
- Design for the eligibility first: the tags, the metadata, the follow structure — the recall's inputs are the cheap levers, and the content that is never a candidate is the strategy's dead loss.
- Test the pipeline hypotheses with the account's A/B: the documented signal changed, the outcome measured — the creator evidence as the strategy's instrument.
- Track the platform's change log as a standing input: the stated updates, the transparency notes, the policy shifts — the perimeter moves, and the strategy re-aligns.

## Phase 6 — Ethics and Boundary Conditions

- The pipeline's inputs include the user's behavior and, in some designs, the user's sensitive attributes; the platform's handling is the platform's responsibility, but the strategy's use of the behavioral signals (the targeting of the vulnerable state) carries the strategist's share of the line.
- The ranking's objective is the platform's choice; the strategy's alignment with it is not an endorsement of it — the distinction between optimizing within the system and arguing for the system is the professional's standing.
- The creator evidence (the A/B on the real account) uses the account's audience; the experiment's consent (the audience's exposure to the variants) is the design's constraint, not its detail.

## Phase 7 — Exercises and Assessment

For learning artifacts, include exercises that force the diagnosis skill, not recall. Use the standard set:

1. Build the pipeline diagram for one platform: the three stages, the documented signals per stage, the inferred ones marked as such — with the citations for the documented layer.
2. Write the objective read for the platform: the documented value definition, the tensions, and the strategy's alignment statement.
3. Run the eligibility check on ten pieces of content: the tags, the metadata, the follow structure — with the diagnosis of the ones outside the recall.
4. Design the creator-evidence A/B for one pipeline hypothesis (e.g., the completion-rate effect on the re-recommendation): the variants, the metric, the sample, the window.
5. Produce the documented-perimeter inventory: the help pages, the transparency materials, the creator guidance — as the citable list the team uses instead of the rumor.
6. Write the change-log tracking spec: the sources, the cadence, the re-alignment rule when the perimeter moves.

For each exercise provide scenario, objective, constraints, expected deliverable, evaluation criteria, and an expert-quality example solution where appropriate.

## Phase 8 — Quality Gate and Delivery

Before delivering, run the delivery checklists. The artifact fails the gate on any “no.”

### Pipeline diagram
- The three stages are drawn
- The documented signals are cited
- The inferred ones are marked

### Objective read
- The value definition is from the documentation
- The tensions are named
- The alignment is stated

### Evidence protocol
- The A/B design is specified
- The metric and the window are set
- The consent constraint is noted

### Delivery gate
- The change-log tracking is live
- The re-alignment rule is written
- The next hypothesis is in the queue

## Anti-Patterns

the rumor as the strategy; the single-stage diagnosis (everything is 'the ranking'); the objective assumed, not documented; the A/B without the consent note; the change log ignored; the platform's welfare goals dismissed as irrelevant to the alignment.

## Decision Heuristic

Before any claim or recommendation, ask:

1. Which stage is the failure at, and what does the evidence show?
2. What does the platform document it optimizes, and is the strategy aligned with that?
3. Is this signal documented, reconstructed, or rumored — and does its status match its use?
4. What does the A/B say, with its window and its consent note?
5. Has the perimeter moved since the strategy was written?

If a claim cannot survive all of these questions, it is not in the artifact.

## Final Principle

The goal is not to outsmart the algorithm. It is to know the machine's documented shape, align the strategy with its stated objective, and test the rest with the account's own evidence — so the distribution is earned on the ground, not on the rumor.
