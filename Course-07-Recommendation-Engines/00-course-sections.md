# Course 07 — Recommendation Engines: Reverse-Engineering How TikTok, YouTube, and Meta Prize Watch-Time, Completion, and Engagement Velocity (Researched Curriculum Outline)

Source: `uploads/Virality.txt`, entry 7. Section count: 8 (compressed to ≤15). Progression: beginner → expert.

**How Platforms Allocate Attention: The Ranking Pipeline**

**Summary:** The ground floor: the machine behind the feed. The section explains the ranking pipeline as it is generally understood — the candidate generation (the recall stage that narrows the universe of possible content to a manageable set), the ranking (the model that scores and orders the candidates for this user, this moment), and the impression (the final selection with the diversity, the freshness, and the policy filters) — and the objective the pipeline optimizes (the predicted user value, the engagement the user is expected to produce, in the platform's chosen definition). It covers what is known versus inferred (the platforms' documented signals, the academic reconstructions, the creator-community evidence), and the honest frame: the engine is a black box with a documented perimeter, and the strategy is built on the perimeter.

**Absorbed Skill:** Describe the ranking pipeline (recall, ranking, impression) and the objective it optimizes, with the known versus inferred clearly separated; Build a strategy on the engine's documented perimeter rather than on speculation about its internals.

**The Engagement Metrics That Matter: What the Objective Actually Measures**

**Summary:** The objective's anatomy. The section decomposes the engagement quantities the platforms document and prize — the views, the watch time and the completion rate, the likes and the shares and the comments (and the weight the platform's evidence gives each), the return visits and the follow conversions — and the distinction between the vanity metric and the causal one (the metric that the ranking demonstrably uses). It covers the short-term versus the long-term tension in the measured engagement (the immediate reaction versus the return behavior), the quality signals the platforms have added (the 'not interested' feedback, the satisfaction surveys, the dwell-quality measures), and the gaming of each metric (what the metric rewards, what the content that farms it does, and the platform's countermeasures). It closes with the metric map for a strategy: which metric the strategy optimizes, which it monitors, and which it refuses to farm.

**Absorbed Skill:** Decompose the platform's engagement metrics into the causal ones (the ranking's demonstrable inputs) and the vanity ones, with the evidence per classification; Build a strategy's metric map — optimized, monitored, refused — with the gaming dynamics of each metric named.

**Collaborative Filtering: The Engine's Core Inference**

**Summary:** The classic engine, and the intuition behind the modern one. The section teaches collaborative filtering from first principles: the user-item interaction matrix, the user-user and the item-item similarity (the 'people like you liked' and the 'items like this were liked' inferences), the matrix factorization (the latent factors that explain the interactions, the low-rank approximation, the training), and the cold start (the new user, the new item — the problem that the content features and the social graph solve). It covers the classical cases (the Netflix prize, the Amazon's 'customers who bought'), the failure modes (the popularity bias, the filter bubble's seed, the staleness of the taste model), and the honest frame for the strategy: the collaborative core is still the engine's heart, wrapped in the features and the context that the modern stack adds.

**Absorbed Skill:** Explain and implement the collaborative-filtering core — the similarities, the factorization, the cold start — and read its outputs as the engine's taste model; Diagnose the collaborative core's failure modes (popularity bias, staleness, the cold start) in a platform's behavior.

**Content-Based and Hybrid Systems: The Features the Engine Reads**

**Summary:** The other half of the engine: what the content itself says to the system. The section teaches content-based recommendation (the content's features — the topic, the format, the visual, the audio, the text — as the recommendation's input), the feature engineering for the modern platforms (the NLP of the caption and the transcript, the CV of the frame and the thumbnail, the audio's signature, the multimodal embedding that places the content in a shared space with the text), and the hybrid architectures (the collaborative core wrapped with the content features and the context — the modern stack's composition). It covers how the NLP and the CV courses connect here (the caption's topic is the recall's input, the thumbnail's quality is the ranking's signal), and the strategy's lever: the content's features are the part of the engine's input the creator fully controls.

**Absorbed Skill:** Explain the content-based and hybrid architectures and the feature pipeline (NLP, CV, audio, the multimodal embedding) that feeds them; Engineer the content's features deliberately — the metadata, the transcript, the thumbnail, the format — as the strategy's controlled input to the engine.

**The Feedback Loop and Velocity: How Early Performance Buys Distribution**

**Summary:** The engine's most consequential dynamic: the early engagement's purchase of the later distribution. The section teaches the two-stage test (the small seed audience, the performance read, the expansion decision — the test-and-expand pattern documented across the short-video platforms), the velocity metrics (the early engagement rate per unit of the seed, the completion, the re-watch, the share in the first window), the bandit logic (the exploration-exploitation allocation across the candidates, the UCB and the Thompson sampling as the formal models, the platform's informal version), and the loop's dynamics (the amplification of the strong, the starvation of the weak, the feedback's instability — the runaway and the correction). It covers the strategy's exposure: the launch's first window as the distribution's auction, the content's early design (the hook, the completion, the first-comment seed), and the honest frame (the velocity is a signal, not a verdict — the correction, the late bloom, and the quality layer's check).

**Absorbed Skill:** Explain the test-and-expand dynamic and the bandit logic behind the early-window allocation, and the loop's amplification and instability; Design the content's first window — the hook, the completion, the early engagement seed — as the distribution's auction entry.

**Optimizing Content for the Engine: The Design Levers and the Line**

**Summary:** The application: the content's design against the engine's signals. The section teaches the levers the strategy controls — the hook structure (the first seconds' pattern, the open loop, the payoff's timing), the retention curve (the shape the completion rewards, the dip's diagnosis, the re-watch's design), the thumbnail and the title (the click's first signal, the promise's design), and the format and the length (the consumption's expectation, the platform's format preference) — each lever against the metric it moves. It covers the A/B of the creative elements (the variants, the metric, the sample, the platform's native test where it exists), the platform-specific norms (the short-video's grammar versus the long-form's, the feed's versus the search's), and the line: the clickbait (the promise the content does not keep), the doomscroll's design (the infinite engagement without the value), and the engagement bait — the three games, the platform's countermeasures, and the strategy's refusal as the standing policy.

**Absorbed Skill:** Design the content's elements — hook, retention, thumbnail, format — against the engine's metrics, with the A/B that prices each choice; Hold the line on the three games (clickbait, doomscroll design, engagement bait) as the standing policy, with the platform's countermeasures tracked.

**Filter Bubbles, Polarization, and the Engine's Externalities**

**Summary:** The engine's costs, measured honestly. The section covers the filter bubble (the exposure narrowing from the recommendation's inference — the evidence for it, the measure, and the degrees: the narrowing that is the taste's service versus the narrowing that is the opinion's cage), the polarization's evidence (the studies of the feed's effect on the attitude and the affect, the mixed findings, the distinction between the exposure and the agreement), the well-being research (the usage's effect, the dose-response, the vulnerable population, the honest state of the evidence), and the platform's responses (the welfare objective, the diversity injection, the break-up of the infinite scroll, the transparency report). It closes with the strategist's position: the alignment with the engine's dynamics is the job, and the share of the externality the strategy owns (the niche farming, the outrage's design, the vulnerable targeting) is the professional's line, stated.

**Absorbed Skill:** Evaluate the filter-bubble and polarization evidence honestly — the measure, the degrees, the mixed findings — and the well-being research's state; State the strategist's share of the engine's externalities and hold the line on the niche farming, the outrage design, and the vulnerable targeting.

**Summary:** The capstone: the engine as the strategy's environment, read end to end. The section integrates the course — the pipeline, the metrics, the collaborative and the content-based halves, the velocity loop, the design levers, the externalities — into the strategic practice: the engine read (the platform's documented objective and its per-niche behavior), the content system (the feature-aligned content, the first-window design, the lever A/B in rotation), the measurement (the velocity's read, the quality layer, the exposure measure), and the alignment (the welfare direction, the line, the statement of the share). It closes with the strategy's standing document: the engine read, the content system spec, the metric map, the line policy — the four, reviewed on the cadence.
