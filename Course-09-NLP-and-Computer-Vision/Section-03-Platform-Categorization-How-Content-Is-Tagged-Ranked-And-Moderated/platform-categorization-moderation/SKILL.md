# Platform Categorization: How Content Is Tagged, Ranked, and Moderated: Professional Module Builder

## Mission

Produce professional-level material on how the platform's reading shapes the content's lifecycle — the tag, the ranking input, the moderation — and how the strategy manages the placement and the risk. The unit of work is a defensible artifact — a learning module, an evidence brief, a case diagnosis, or a strategy memo — in which every claim is tied to a source, a mechanism, and a boundary condition.

Optimize simultaneously for:

- empirical precision (what the canonical studies and works actually found)
- conceptual clarity (the core terms kept strictly distinct)
- causal discipline (correlational and causal claims never blurred)
- practical utility (a practitioner can act on the output the same day)
- intellectual honesty (limits and boundary conditions as visible as the findings)

Do not produce generic advice or pop-science hype. The register is that of an advanced professional training program: confident where the evidence is strong, explicitly conditional where it is not.

## Use Cases

### New professional learning module
When asked to create a full module, course unit, or workshop on this topic:
1. Establish scope: audience, depth, and which claims the module must cover.
2. Build the evidence base from primary sources and canonical works.
3. Develop the core mechanisms and diagnostic logic.
4. Add method, critique, and application sections.
5. End with exercises and the delivery checklists.
6. Deliver the complete artifact with no placeholders.

### Brief or leadership memo
When asked for a short document that must decide something:
1. State the question the document must answer in one sentence.
2. Include only findings strong enough to bear that decision, each with its mechanism and source.
3. Convert results into plain-language implications.
4. End with the specific decisions the evidence changes.
5. Cap length; cut context before cutting caveats.

### Case diagnosis
When asked to explain why a specific piece of content, campaign, or product spread (or did not):
1. Identify the operative mechanisms — never a single assumed cause.
2. Map the case onto the diagnostic framework of this skill.
3. Run the differential: which mechanisms were active, which alternatives were considered and set aside.
4. State confidence and the evidence that would revise the diagnosis.

### Strategy design
When asked to design a campaign, content system, or program around this lever:
1. Fix the objective, audience, and platform context.
2. Select the mechanisms from this skill that fit that context.
3. Specify implementation, sequencing, and the measurement plan.
4. State the boundary conditions under which the plan breaks.

## Core Output Requirements

Regardless of format:

- Every named finding or claim carries its source or mechanism in the same breath.
- Correlational and causal claims are labeled as such wherever they matter.
- Effect claims carry their metric and a plain-language rendering.
- Boundary conditions are stated as explicit “applies when / breaks down when” conditions.
- Examples use real, named studies, campaigns, or artifacts — not invented case studies.
- No padding sections, generic intro/outro filler, or advice that would read the same for any topic.

## Phase 1 — Scope and Claim Audit

### Artifact and audience
What the user actually needs (module vs. memo vs. diagnosis) and who reads it. Different readers make different cuts of the same material.

### Claim inventory
List the claims the artifact will make. For each, note the source or mechanism that supports it and whether that support is correlational, experimental, or canonical. Downgrade or cut unsupported claims — not the caveat.

### Depth budget
Match length to the decision the artifact serves. The fix for a weak document is choosing stronger claims to carry it, not adding hedges to weaker ones.

## Phase 2 — Foundations and Core Content

1. Cover the categorization: the platform's taxonomy (the topic's hierarchy, the format's type, the audience's rating — the structure the content is placed in), the read that produces the tag (the classifier per the taxonomy, the multimodal read per the format), and the tag's effect (the recall's input — the content's eligibility per the recommendation course; the recommendation's neighborhood; the search's placement) — the tag as the content's address, and the misplacement as the reach's failure at the reading's layer.
2. Cover the ranking input: the read's features into the ranking's model (the quality's prediction — the click's model, the completion's model, the dwell's model; the engagement's prediction from the content's features; the originality's and the authenticity's signal) — the feature per the read, the model's use in the pipeline, and the strategy's lever (the content's features as the controlled input — the connection to the content-based course section, at the platform's model).
3. Cover the moderation: the policy's enforcement by the read (the safety's detection — the harmful, the deceptive, the infringing; the authenticity's detection — the synthetic, the impersonation, the manipulated; the originality's check — the duplicate, the derivative), the detection's task per the policy (the classifier per the policy's definition), the decision's path (the detection, the action — the demotion, the removal, the account's; the appeal's design), and the error's type (the false positive — the content removed that the policy does not cover, with the creator's cost; the false negative — the content kept that the policy covers, with the audience's cost) — the errors, their different costs, and the platform's documented error's state in aggregate.
4. Cover the transparency materials: the documented signal (the help page's statement of the signal used, the policy's statement, the appeal's design), the invisible design (the model's architecture, the task's spec, the threshold's placement — the platform's proprietary), and the honest frame (the strategy built on the documented, tested on the behavior, never on the inferred — the discipline from the recommendation course, applied to the moderation's perimeter).
5. Teach the strategy's position: the placement as the design input (the taxonomy's understanding, the tag's verification, the misplacement's correction — the metadata's design per the taxonomy), the quality signal as the standing read (the quality's score per the content, the trend, the demotion's risk), and the moderation's risk as the line's management (the policy's boundary per the content type, the appeal's readiness, the synthetic's disclosure where the policy requires) — the three positions, with the practice per each.

## Phase 3 — Method and Technique

- The taxonomy map per platform: the hierarchy, the format's type, the rating — with the documentation status
- The tag's verification: the content's tag versus the intended placement, the misplacement's diagnosis, the correction
- The quality signal read: the score per the content, the trend, the demotion's risk per the threshold
- The moderation risk assessment: the policy's boundary per the content type, the error's type per the policy, the appeal's readiness
- The transparency inventory: the documented signal, the policy's statement, the appeal's design — the citable perimeter

## Phase 4 — Analysis and Diagnosis

- Diagnose the reach's failure at the reading's layer: the tag's misplacement (the taxonomy's read error, the metadata's gap), the quality signal's low read (the feature's state, the model's prediction), the moderation's flag (the policy's boundary, the error's type) — the layer per the case, and the correction.
- Read the error's type in the moderation's case: the false positive (the removed content, the appeal's path, the correction's record) and the false negative (the kept content, the audience's cost, the policy's gap) — the cost per the type, and the platform's response per the type.
- Check the documented perimeter's currency: the policy's update, the signal's change, the appeal's redesign — the change's record, and the strategy's re-alignment.
- Separate the platform's aggregate error's state from the individual's case: the documented rate, the individual's experience — the gap, and the appeal as the individual's path.

## Phase 5 — Application and Design

- Map the taxonomy per platform and verify the tag per content: the placement as the design input, the misplacement corrected per the content, the metadata's design per the taxonomy — the placement managed, not assumed.
- Read the quality signal as the standing instrument: the score per the content, the trend per the type, the demotion's risk per the threshold — the read feeding the content's design and the line's management.
- Assess the moderation risk per the content type: the policy's boundary, the error's type, the appeal's readiness — the risk managed per the content, with the disclosure where the policy requires.
- Hold the transparency inventory as the citable ground: the documented signal, the policy's statement, the appeal's design — current, with the re-alignment on the change.

## Phase 6 — Ethics and Boundary Conditions

- The moderation's false positive is the creator's cost (the removed content, the account's action, the appeal's time); the line for the strategy is the policy's boundary understood before the posting, and the appeal's readiness after — the risk managed as the design, not discovered as the incident.
- The synthetic's and the manipulated's detection is the authenticity's enforcement; the line for the creator is the disclosure's policy (the synthetic's label, the manipulation's context) stated per the platform, and the honest frame is the content's nature stated, not the detection's evasion.
- The taxonomy's and the quality's read is the content's address and standing; the strategy's use of the read (the placement, the quality's design) is bounded by the read's honesty (the platform's documented state) and the content's nature (the tag that describes, not the tag that deceives).

## Phase 7 — Exercises and Assessment

For learning artifacts, include exercises that force the diagnosis skill, not recall. Use the standard set:

1. Map the taxonomy for two platforms: the hierarchy, the format's type, the rating — with the documentation status per the element.
2. Run the tag's verification on a content set: the intended placement versus the tag, the misplacement's diagnosis per the piece, the correction per the cause.
3. Run the quality signal read on a content set: the score per the piece, the trend per the type, the demotion's risk per the threshold — with the content's design response.
4. Run the moderation risk assessment for a content type: the policy's boundary, the error's type, the appeal's readiness — with the disclosure's policy per the platform.
5. Build the transparency inventory for a platform: the documented signal, the policy's statement, the appeal's design — with the re-alignment rule on the change.
6. Write the placement and risk document for the operation: the taxonomy map, the verification's cadence, the quality's read, the risk assessment, the appeal's readiness — as the standing practice.

For each exercise provide scenario, objective, constraints, expected deliverable, evaluation criteria, and an expert-quality example solution where appropriate.

## Anti-Patterns

the misplacement undiscovered; the quality's score unread; the policy's boundary unknown until the removal; the appeal's absence at the incident; the tag that deceives; the synthetic undisclosed; the perimeter assumed, not inventoried.

## Decision Heuristic

Before any claim or recommendation, ask:

1. Where is this content tagged, and does the tag match the placement intended?
2. What does the quality signal say, and what is the demotion's risk?
3. What is the policy's boundary for this content type, and is the appeal ready?
4. Is the content's nature disclosed where the policy requires?
5. Is the transparency inventory current, and did the last change trigger the re-alignment?

If a claim cannot survive all of these questions, it is not in the artifact.

## Final Principle

The goal is not the platform's agreement. It is the content placed where it belongs, standing on the quality's read, clear at the policy's boundary, ready at the appeal — so the reading's layer is the strategy's managed input, not its discovered failure.
