# Course 08 — Feedback Loops and A/B Testing: Closed-Loop Optimization of Hooks, Thumbnails, Copy, and Posting Times (Researched Curriculum Outline)

Source: `uploads/Virality.txt`, entry 8. Section count: 8 (compressed to ≤15). Progression: beginner → expert.

**The Feedback Loop as a System: Data, Decision, Change**

**Summary:** The ground floor: improvement as a system, not an act. The section defines the feedback loop in its full form — the data (the measurement of the current state), the decision (the read of the gap between the state and the target), and the change (the iteration that closes the gap) — and distinguishes the loop from the dashboard (the measurement without the decision is the ornament) and from the reaction (the change without the loop is the thrash). It covers the loop's types (the balancing loop that holds the state, the reinforcing loop that compounds the state, and the virtuous and the vicious as their valence), the loop's time constants (the delay between the change and its signal, the period the loop must respect), and the loop's failure modes (the lag ignored, the noise read as the signal, the loop that optimizes the proxy without the target). It ends with the operating discipline: the loop's cadence, the owner per beat, and the decision that each beat produces.

**Absorbed Skill:** Design a feedback loop as a system — the data, the decision, the change — with the time constant and the cadence defined; Diagnose the loop's failure modes (the lag, the noise, the proxy drift) and the distinction between the loop and the dashboard or the reaction.

**From Data to Iteration: The Measurement-to-Change Cycle**

**Summary:** The loop's working rhythm: how a number becomes a change. The section teaches the cycle in its operational form — the measurement (the metric's collection, the sample's adequacy, the baseline's state), the read (the gap's diagnosis: the cause hypothesis from the metric's pattern), the iteration (the change spec: the element modified, the expectation, the success criterion), and the close (the comparison: the change's effect versus the expectation, the loop's log updated). It covers the cause diagnosis (the pattern's reading: the level shift, the trend break, the segment divergence — the hypothesis per pattern), the expectation discipline (the predicted effect, written before the change, with the mechanism — the prediction as the learning's instrument), and the comparison (the before-after with the control where it exists, the difference's significance, the attribution's honesty). It ends with the iteration's economy: the cheap iteration first (the element's swap, not the rebuild), the iteration's queue (the candidates ranked by the expected value), and the no-iteration decision (the gap that is the noise, the change that is the cost — the recorded pass).

**Absorbed Skill:** Run the measurement-to-change cycle: the read with the cause hypothesis, the iteration spec with the written expectation, the close with the honest comparison; Manage the iteration's economy — the cheap-first ordering, the queue by expected value, and the recorded pass.

**A/B Testing Fundamentals: The Design That Makes the Call**

**Summary:** The instrument's foundations. The section teaches the A/B test in its rigorous form — the hypothesis (the null and the alternative, the direction, the effect of interest), the unit (the unit of randomization: the user, the content, the impression — and the contamination between units), the sample (the size from the effect, the variance, the power, the duration), the assignment (the randomization, the holdout, the segmentation), and the read (the difference, the confidence interval, the significance, and the multiple-comparisons problem when the test has many metrics or many arms). It covers the classic errors in order of damage: the peeking (the early stop on the significance that inflates the false positive), the underpower (the sample too small for the effect of interest), the novelty (the effect that is the newness, not the change), the interference (the units that affect each other's outcomes), and the metric-gaming (the variant that wins on the tested metric and loses on the untested). It ends with the test's decision: the ship, the reject, the extend — with the pre-registered rule, not the read's convenience.

**Absorbed Skill:** Design an A/B test that makes the call — the hypothesis, the unit, the sample, the assignment, the read — with the classic errors named and avoided; Apply the decision rule (the ship, the reject, the extend) pre-registered, with the multiple-metric and the novelty handling.

**Testing the Creative Elements: Hooks, Thumbnails, Copy, and Timing**

**Summary:** The application of the instrument to the creative system. The section teaches the test design per creative element — the hook (the first seconds' variants: the open loop, the value preview, the question; the completion and the hold metrics; the sample per the element's variance), the thumbnail and the title (the visual and the verbal first signal: the click-through metric, the native multiview where the platform provides it, the honesty constraint), the copy (the caption, the description, the CTA: the engagement and the conversion metrics, the copy's role per platform), and the timing (the posting time and the frequency: the audience's activity data, the time-of-day and the day-of-week tests, the interaction with the velocity window). It covers the creative test's specific problems: the variance (the creative's effect is large and the sample's cost is high — the platform's native test as the cheap instrument), the fatigue (the variant's effect decays with the exposure — the rotation's design), and the interaction (the element's effect depends on the audience segment and the content type — the segmentation's read). It ends with the creative test's pipeline: the variant's production (the per-element spec), the test's scheduling (the queue per the expected value), and the winner's integration (the element's change into the content system's spec, the learning folded back).

**Absorbed Skill:** Design the creative-element tests — hook, thumbnail, copy, timing — with the metrics, the variance handling, and the fatigue and interaction reads; Run the creative test pipeline: the variant production, the scheduling by expected value, and the winner's integration into the content system's spec.

**Causal Inference Beyond the A/B: Quasi-Experiments and the Causal Ladder**

**Summary:** When the clean test is impossible. The section teaches the causal ladder (the association, the action, the counterfactual — the rungs, and what each rung's evidence permits the strategy to claim) and the quasi-experimental methods for the rungs above the clean A/B: the interrupted time series (the before-after across the event — the platform's change, the policy's shift), the difference-in-differences (the treated and the untreated across the event — the parallel-trends assumption, and its examination), the synthetic control (the constructed untreated from the comparators — the match, the counterfactual's path), and the instrumental variable (the instrument that moves the treatment without moving the outcome — the relevance, the exclusion). It covers the natural experiment (the event that assigns the treatment — the platform's rollout, the algorithm's change — and the read of its effect on the system), and the honest claims per method (what each one can say, what it cannot, and the assumption that carries the claim). It ends with the strategy's use: the causal question the business needs answered, the method per the question, the assumption's examination, and the claim's boundary stated.

**Absorbed Skill:** Choose and apply the quasi-experimental method — the interrupted time series, the difference-in-differences, the synthetic control, the instrumental variable — with the assumption and the claim's boundary per method; Read the natural experiment (the platform's change) as the causal evidence for the system, with the honest claim.

**Bandits and Online Learning: Allocating the Traffic That Learns**

**Summary:** When the experiment is the production. The section teaches the multi-armed bandit (the allocation across the arms — the variants, the hooks, the content types — that balances the exploitation of the known good and the exploration of the untested), the formal models (the UCB — the confidence bound as the exploration's purchase; the Thompson sampling — the posterior's draw as the allocation; the contextual bandit — the arm's choice per the context: the audience segment, the content type, the time), and the platform's version (the test-and-expand as the bandit at the content level, the exploration slice as the allocation's floor). It covers the regret (the loss to the optimal, the bandit's cost measure, and its bound), the cold start in the bandit (the new arm's prior, the content's seed), and the ethics of the allocation (the inferior arm's exposure, the audience's cost, and the guardrail's bound on the exploration). It ends with the bandit's strategy use: the allocation design for the creative's production (the variant's budget per the posterior), the exploration's floor (the new content's minimum, the diversity's protection), and the handoff to the guardrail (the bandit's metric versus the target's pairing).

**Absorbed Skill:** Design the bandit allocation — the UCB, the Thompson sampling, the contextual version — for the creative's traffic, with the regret and the cold start handled; Set the exploration's floor and the guardrail's bound on the exploration, as the ethics and the target's pairing of the allocation.

**Measurement Integrity: Metric Gaming, Novelties, and Interdependencies**

**Summary:** The measurement's own failure modes. The section covers the metric-gaming (the Goodhart's law — the metric that becomes the target stops being the measure; the per-metric games: the clickbait's click, the padding's watch-time, the bait's comment; the detection: the metric's decoupling from the value, the cohort's divergence), the novelty and the primacy (the effect that is the newness, the position, the first impression — the design's handling: the duration, the rotation, the position's control), and the interdependencies (the metrics that move each other — the click's rise and the retention's fall, the reach's rise and the community's cost; the system's read: the metric's network, the substitution, the trade-off's naming). It covers the measurement's standing defenses: the paired metric (the target beside the proxy), the cohort's comparison (the new versus the returning, the segment's divergence), the holdout's permanence (the control that outlives the change), and the anomaly's protocol (the spike's diagnosis before the action). It ends with the integrity's culture: the metric's owner (the metric's definition, the game's watch, the correction's record) and the review that the number's provenance is part of the number's claim.

**Absorbed Skill:** Detect and correct the measurement's failure modes — the metric-gaming, the novelty, the interdependency — with the paired metric, the cohort's read, and the holdout; Run the integrity's standing practice: the anomaly's protocol, the metric's ownership, and the review that the provenance is part of the claim.
