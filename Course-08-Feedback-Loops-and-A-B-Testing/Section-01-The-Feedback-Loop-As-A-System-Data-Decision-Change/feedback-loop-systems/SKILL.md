# The Feedback Loop as a System: Data, Decision, Change: Professional Module Builder

## Mission

Produce professional-level material on how improvement becomes a system — the data, the decision, the change, the time constant, and the failure modes that break the loop. The unit of work is a defensible artifact — a learning module, an evidence brief, a case diagnosis, or a strategy memo — in which every claim is tied to a source, a mechanism, and a boundary condition.

Optimize simultaneously for:

- empirical precision (what the canonical studies and works actually found)
- conceptual clarity (the core terms kept strictly distinct)
- causal discipline (correlational and causal claims never blurred)
- practical utility (a practitioner can act on the output the same day)
- intellectual honesty (limits and boundary conditions as visible as the findings)

Do not produce generic advice or pop-science hype. The register is that of an advanced professional training program: confident where the evidence is strong, explicitly conditional where it is not.

## Use Cases

### New professional learning module
When asked to create a full module, course unit, or workshop on this topic:
1. Establish scope: audience, depth, and which claims the module must cover.
2. Build the evidence base from primary sources and canonical works.
3. Develop the core mechanisms and diagnostic logic.
4. Add method, critique, and application sections.
5. End with exercises and the delivery checklists.
6. Deliver the complete artifact with no placeholders.

### Brief or leadership memo
When asked for a short document that must decide something:
1. State the question the document must answer in one sentence.
2. Include only findings strong enough to bear that decision, each with its mechanism and source.
3. Convert results into plain-language implications.
4. End with the specific decisions the evidence changes.
5. Cap length; cut context before cutting caveats.

### Case diagnosis
When asked to explain why a specific piece of content, campaign, or product spread (or did not):
1. Identify the operative mechanisms — never a single assumed cause.
2. Map the case onto the diagnostic framework of this skill.
3. Run the differential: which mechanisms were active, which alternatives were considered and set aside.
4. State confidence and the evidence that would revise the diagnosis.

### Strategy design
When asked to design a campaign, content system, or program around this lever:
1. Fix the objective, audience, and platform context.
2. Select the mechanisms from this skill that fit that context.
3. Specify implementation, sequencing, and the measurement plan.
4. State the boundary conditions under which the plan breaks.

## Core Output Requirements

Regardless of format:

- Every named finding or claim carries its source or mechanism in the same breath.
- Correlational and causal claims are labeled as such wherever they matter.
- Effect claims carry their metric and a plain-language rendering.
- Boundary conditions are stated as explicit “applies when / breaks down when” conditions.
- Examples use real, named studies, campaigns, or artifacts — not invented case studies.
- No padding sections, generic intro/outro filler, or advice that would read the same for any topic.

## Phase 1 — Scope and Claim Audit

### Artifact and audience
What the user actually needs (module vs. memo vs. diagnosis) and who reads it. Different readers make different cuts of the same material.

### Claim inventory
List the claims the artifact will make. For each, note the source or mechanism that supports it and whether that support is correlational, experimental, or canonical. Downgrade or cut unsupported claims — not the caveat.

### Depth budget
Match length to the decision the artifact serves. The fix for a weak document is choosing stronger claims to carry it, not adding hedges to weaker ones.

## Phase 2 — Foundations and Core Content

1. Define the loop in its full form: the data (the measurement of the current state, per the metric defined), the decision (the read of the gap between the state and the target, per the decision rule defined), and the change (the iteration that closes the gap, per the iteration spec) — the three elements, and the distinction that the loop is the system of the three, not the measurement alone (the dashboard without the decision is the ornament) nor the change alone (the reaction without the loop is the thrash).
2. Teach the loop's types: the balancing loop (the correction that holds the state — the quality control, the error's reduction) and the reinforcing loop (the compounding that grows the state — the retention flywheel, the audience's growth), and the valence (the virtuous and the vicious as the reinforcing's two faces) — the types, and the system's design in terms of them (the target loop, and the guardrail loop that balances it).
3. Teach the time constant: the delay between the change and its signal (the iteration's latency — the posting's cycle, the engagement's window, the retention's horizon), the period the loop must respect (the decision's cadence shorter than the constant is the noise's domain, longer is the lag's cost), and the design of the cadence from the constants (the fast loop on the fast signal, the slow loop on the slow one, and the nesting of the two).
4. Teach the failure modes: the lag ignored (the decision before the signal's arrival — the thrash on the noise), the noise read as the signal (the significance's absence, the sample's inadequacy), and the proxy drift (the loop that optimizes the proxy without the target — the metric that moves while the value does not) — the three modes, their signatures in the loop's history, and the cure per mode (the constant respected, the significance required, the target paired with the proxy).
5. Institute the operating discipline: the loop's cadence (the beat per loop — the daily, the weekly, the monthly, per the constant), the owner per beat (the decision's owner, not the measurement's), and the decision recorded per beat (the read, the gap, the change, the reason — the loop's log as the system's memory, and the review that the log enables).

## Phase 3 — Method and Technique

- The loop spec: the data (the metric, the source), the decision (the rule, the target), the change (the iteration, the owner) — the document per loop
- The time-constant estimate: the latency per signal, the period per loop, the cadence from the constants
- The loop-type design: the target loop, the guardrail loop, the nesting — the system in terms of the loops
- The failure-mode audit: the thrash's signature, the noise's signature, the proxy's drift — in the loop's history
- The loop log: the read, the gap, the change, the reason — per beat, the standing record

## Phase 4 — Analysis and Diagnosis

- Diagnose the non-improving team by loop element: the data present and the decision absent (the dashboard's state), the decision present and the change absent (the meeting's state), the change present and the data absent (the thrash's state) — the element the history shows, and the fix.
- Read the loop's history for the failure modes: the thrash (the change before the signal), the noise (the decision on the small sample), the proxy drift (the metric's rise and the value's flat) — the mode per period, and the cure applied.
- Check the cadence against the constant: the decision's period shorter than the latency (the thrash's cause) or longer than the cycle (the lag's cost) — the constant's violation, and the re-cadencing.
- Separate the virtuous from the vicious in the reinforcing loop: the compounding that serves the target versus the compounding that serves the proxy — the valence the target's read decides.

## Phase 5 — Application and Design

- Spec the loops before the program starts: the target loop and the guardrail loop, per system — the spec with the metric, the rule, the iteration, the constant, the cadence, the owner.
- Design the nesting: the fast loop on the fast signal (the creative's A/B), the slow loop on the slow one (the strategy's review) — the nesting with the interface (the fast's finding feeds the slow's decision).
- Hold the decision per beat: the read, the gap, the change, the reason — the log per beat, with the skip rule (the beat missed, the decision deferred, the record of both).
- Audit the loops quarterly: the failure modes in the history, the proxy drift against the target, the constant's re-estimate — the audit that keeps the system honest.

## Phase 6 — Ethics and Boundary Conditions

- The loop's data is the user's behavior; the measurement's design (the metric, the sample, the tracking) carries the privacy's constraints, and the loop's speed is bounded by the data's lawfulness as much as by the constant.
- The loop that optimizes the creative at the user's cost (the hook that holds, the value that arrives) is the proxy drift with a human side; the line is the target's pairing (the value's metric beside the proxy's) and the review that the pairing enforces.
- The loop's log is the organization's record of what it tried, what it learned, and why; the log's honesty (the failure recorded, the reason stated) is the learning's design, and its suppression is the learning's death.

## Phase 7 — Exercises and Assessment

For learning artifacts, include exercises that force the diagnosis skill, not recall. Use the standard set:

1. Spec three loops for a content operation: the target loop (the engagement's improvement), the guardrail loop (the quality's holding), the strategy loop (the direction's review) — with the metric, the rule, the iteration, the constant, the cadence, the owner.
2. Estimate the time constants for a system: the latency per signal (the posting's cycle, the engagement's window, the retention's horizon) — and the cadence design from the constants.
3. Design the nesting: the fast creative loop and the slow strategy loop — with the interface (the finding's feed) and the owner per beat.
4. Audit a loop's six-month history for the failure modes: the thrash, the noise, the proxy drift — with the mode per period and the cure applied.
5. Write the loop log template: the read, the gap, the change, the reason — and fill three beats from a real system, including the beat with no change and its reason.
6. Write the quarterly audit agenda: the failure modes, the proxy drift, the constant's re-estimate — with the decision each audit feeds.

For each exercise provide scenario, objective, constraints, expected deliverable, evaluation criteria, and an expert-quality example solution where appropriate.

## Phase 8 — Quality Gate and Delivery

Before delivering, run the delivery checklists. The artifact fails the gate on any “no.”

### Loop spec
- The data, the decision, the change are defined
- The constant is estimated
- The cadence follows the constant

### System design
- The target and the guardrail loops are present
- The nesting has its interface
- The owner per beat is set

### Failure modes
- The audit ran on the history
- The mode per period is named
- The cure is applied and recorded

### Delivery gate
- The log is kept per beat
- The skip rule is honored
- The quarterly audit is on the calendar

## Anti-Patterns

the dashboard without the decision; the reaction without the loop; the decision before the signal; the sample too small for the call; the proxy optimized without the target; the beat skipped without the record.

## Decision Heuristic

Before any claim or recommendation, ask:

1. Does this loop have the data, the decision, and the change — or only the measurement?
2. Is the cadence shorter or longer than the constant, and does the thrash or the lag show in the history?
3. What is the target beside this proxy, and does the target move when the proxy does?
4. What does the loop log say about the last three beats, and was the no-change beat recorded?
5. When was the loop last audited for the failure modes?

If a claim cannot survive all of these questions, it is not in the artifact.

## Final Principle

The goal is not more data. It is the loop that turns the data into the decision and the decision into the change, on the constant's time, with the target beside the proxy and the log as the memory — so the improvement compounds, and the system remembers why.
