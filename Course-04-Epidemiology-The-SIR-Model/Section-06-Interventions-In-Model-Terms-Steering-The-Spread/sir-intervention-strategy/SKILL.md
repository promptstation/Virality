# Interventions in Model Terms: Steering the Spread: Professional Module Builder

## Mission

Produce professional-level material on how the strategy levers map onto the model's parameters — and how to time and dose the intervention against the curve. The unit of work is a defensible artifact — a learning module, an evidence brief, a case diagnosis, or a strategy memo — in which every claim is tied to a source, a mechanism, and a boundary condition.

Optimize simultaneously for:

- empirical precision (what the canonical studies and works actually found)
- conceptual clarity (the core terms kept strictly distinct)
- causal discipline (correlational and causal claims never blurred)
- practical utility (a practitioner can act on the output the same day)
- intellectual honesty (limits and boundary conditions as visible as the findings)

Do not produce generic advice or pop-science hype. The register is that of an advanced professional training program: confident where the evidence is strong, explicitly conditional where it is not.

## Use Cases

### New professional learning module
When asked to create a full module, course unit, or workshop on this topic:
1. Establish scope: audience, depth, and which claims the module must cover.
2. Build the evidence base from primary sources and canonical works.
3. Develop the core mechanisms and diagnostic logic.
4. Add method, critique, and application sections.
5. End with exercises and the delivery checklists.
6. Deliver the complete artifact with no placeholders.

### Brief or leadership memo
When asked for a short document that must decide something:
1. State the question the document must answer in one sentence.
2. Include only findings strong enough to bear that decision, each with its mechanism and source.
3. Convert results into plain-language implications.
4. End with the specific decisions the evidence changes.
5. Cap length; cut context before cutting caveats.

### Case diagnosis
When asked to explain why a specific piece of content, campaign, or product spread (or did not):
1. Identify the operative mechanisms — never a single assumed cause.
2. Map the case onto the diagnostic framework of this skill.
3. Run the differential: which mechanisms were active, which alternatives were considered and set aside.
4. State confidence and the evidence that would revise the diagnosis.

### Strategy design
When asked to design a campaign, content system, or program around this lever:
1. Fix the objective, audience, and platform context.
2. Select the mechanisms from this skill that fit that context.
3. Specify implementation, sequencing, and the measurement plan.
4. State the boundary conditions under which the plan breaks.

## Core Output Requirements

Regardless of format:

- Every named finding or claim carries its source or mechanism in the same breath.
- Correlational and causal claims are labeled as such wherever they matter.
- Effect claims carry their metric and a plain-language rendering.
- Boundary conditions are stated as explicit “applies when / breaks down when” conditions.
- Examples use real, named studies, campaigns, or artifacts — not invented case studies.
- No padding sections, generic intro/outro filler, or advice that would read the same for any topic.

## Phase 1 — Scope and Claim Audit

### Artifact and audience
What the user actually needs (module vs. memo vs. diagnosis) and who reads it. Different readers make different cuts of the same material.

### Claim inventory
List the claims the artifact will make. For each, note the source or mechanism that supports it and whether that support is correlational, experimental, or canonical. Downgrade or cut unsupported claims — not the caveat.

### Depth budget
Match length to the decision the artifact serves. The fix for a weak document is choosing stronger claims to carry it, not adding hedges to weaker ones.

## Phase 2 — Foundations and Core Content

1. Map the levers to parameters: β (message strength, hook quality, incentive), the contact rate (exposure volume, retargeting, seeding placement), and γ (fatigue rate, content refresh, topic exhaustion) — the three dials of the model.
2. Teach the contact-reduction interventions and their analogs: limiting exposure frequency (throttling, capping retargeting), narrowing the audience (targeting), and the social-distancing analog (slowing the spread of an unwanted idea or rumor).
3. Teach ring vaccination: pre-activating the neighbors (followers, communities) of the likely spreaders so the exposure they receive is into 'recovered' ground — the network-targeted seeding logic.
4. Teach prophylaxis: priming the susceptible pool (awareness, pre-launch) so the effective contact produces activation faster — and its limit (priming without a payload creates fatigue, raising γ).
5. Cover the timing problem: intervention at takeoff moves the threshold; at growth it moves the peak height and date; at the peak it mostly moves the tail; after the peak it is largely cosmetic — and the cost of over-intervention (suppressing a diffusion you want) is real.

## Phase 3 — Method and Technique

- The intervention spec: the parameter target, the mechanism, the dose, the duration
- The counterfactual simulation: the model with and without the intervention, the difference is the effect
- The timing grid: the intervention's effect (peak height, peak date, final size) as a function of start time
- The ring-seeding design: the spreader list, the neighbor reach, the payload, the validation
- The over-intervention monitor: the early-stop rule when the suppression is outpacing the goal

## Phase 4 — Analysis and Diagnosis

- Diagnose the intervention failure by parameter: was the dial turned that was actually binding (β when the contact structure was the constraint)?
- Read the timing: an intervention that moved only the tail was applied too late; one that collapsed the takeoff was either well-timed or over-dosed.
- Separate the intervention effect from the concurrent drift (seasonality, competing events) with the counterfactual simulation.
- Detect the γ risk: priming and refresh programs that extend reach can raise fatigue and shrink the final size — the monitor must watch both.

## Phase 5 — Application and Design

- Specify the intervention by its parameter target before the creative work; a creative without a dial is decoration.
- Run the counterfactual before committing spend: the simulated with/without difference is the expected effect, with its uncertainty.
- Time the intervention from the region reading (takeoff/growth) and set the early-stop rule for over-intervention.
- For network cases, budget to the ring, not the population: the neighbor reach of the spreaders is the unit of spend.

## Phase 6 — Ethics and Boundary Conditions

- Throttling and targeting are also the tools of censorship-adjacent suppression; the line is the stated goal (protecting the audience, protecting the brand) and its honesty.
- Ring-seeding that manipulates the neighbors' networks without disclosure is astroturfing wearing an epidemiology costume.
- The suppression case (slowing a harmful rumor) carries its own ethics: the intervention's reach and the correction's accuracy both matter.

## Phase 7 — Exercises and Assessment

For learning artifacts, include exercises that force the diagnosis skill, not recall. Use the standard set:

1. Specify the intervention for a below-threshold diffusion: the binding parameter, the mechanism, the dose, the duration — and the counterfactual effect.
2. Run the timing grid for a β-intervention: peak height, peak date, and final size as functions of the start time; mark the no-effect region.
3. Design the ring-seeding plan: the spreader list, the neighbor reach, the payload, and the validation against the counterfactual.
4. Diagnose a failed intervention from its curve: the parameter that was turned, the one that was binding, and the fix.
5. Design the over-intervention monitor for a suppression case: the early-stop rule, the metric, and the ethics note.
6. Write the intervention memo: the goal, the dials, the counterfactual, the timing, the stop rule — one page, decision-ready.

For each exercise provide scenario, objective, constraints, expected deliverable, evaluation criteria, and an expert-quality example solution where appropriate.

## Phase 8 — Quality Gate and Delivery

Before delivering, run the delivery checklists. The artifact fails the gate on any “no.”

### Intervention spec
- The parameter target is named
- The mechanism and dose are defined
- The duration is bounded

### Counterfactual
- The with/without simulation was run
- The effect estimate carries its uncertainty
- The concurrent drift was controlled

### Timing and monitoring
- The start time follows the region reading
- The early-stop rule is set
- The γ monitor (fatigue) is live

### Delivery gate
- The ethics note was written (suppression cases)
- The memo is decision-ready
- The post-intervention re-fit is scheduled

## Anti-Patterns

the creative without a dial; the intervention without a counterfactual; the late intervention sold as strategy; the over-dose without a stop rule; the ring seeding that is undisclosed manipulation; the suppression without a correction.

## Decision Heuristic

Before any claim or recommendation, ask:

1. Which dial is actually binding, and did we turn it?
2. What does the counterfactual say the intervention is worth?
3. Is the start time in the region where it can still matter?
4. Is there a stop rule, and what triggers it?
5. Does this intervention survive the ethics note?

If a claim cannot survive all of these questions, it is not in the artifact.

## Final Principle

The goal is not to steer everything. It is to know which dial moves what, when it can be turned to count, and when the honest move is to stop — so the intervention is an instrument, not a reflex.
